Hypothetical Laboratory 7 — Morphological and set-based concepts

Book 6: System Conceptual Design · Fictional Northstar case · Week 7

Mission brief

You are the independent review team for a proposed Northstar regional mobility service. Your job is not to optimize the supplied favorite; it is to decide which claim can be supported now and what should happen next. The supplied material is synthetic. No result here qualifies a real vehicle, a transportation service or a safety-critical system.

Case packet: Three fleet modes × two dispatch arrangements × two stop concepts produce 12 nominal combinations. Removing four combinations for accessibility and right-of-way conflicts leaves eight candidates, not a ranked winner.

Technical focus: Morphological analysis creates structured divergence: enumerate choices along independent dimensions before combining them. Feasibility screening comes before preference scoring, and the elimination reason should be recorded. Set-based reasoning keeps several promising alternatives alive while the most consequential uncertainty is retired.

Your deliverable

Create a 3×2×2 morphology table and document exclusion criteria for any infeasible combination.

Create a small but reviewable package with these four files:

  1. W07-decision.md — claim, decision alternatives, scenario, recommendation and residual uncertainty.
  2. W07-evidence.csv — claim_id,source_revision,configuration,assumption,method,observed_or_predicted_result,status,limitation.
  3. W07-model.md — your calculation, causal chain, interface contract or structural representation, with units and revisions.
  4. W07-review.md — a red-team objection and your technical response.

Task sequence

  • First pass, without calculation: Identify the objective, the stakeholder bearing a consequence, and at least two competing explanations or design alternatives.
  • Model: Construct the minimum useful representation. Keep the difference between a projected result and a measured result visible.
  • Stress: Vary one critical assumption, introduce a fault or change the mission scenario; determine whether the decision reverses or becomes conditional.
  • Evidence: Define an observable outcome, acceptance rule and test article or model version. Name what this exercise cannot establish.
  • Review: Write a recommendation of accept for next stage, accept conditionally, defer pending evidence or reject; support it with specific facts.

The twist

A numerical score must not rescue an alternative that violates a mandatory safety constraint. A reviewer asks: How many concept families remain after objective feasibility screening? Your package must answer this in a way that changes either a design decision, a test plan or an explicit uncertainty status. Do not give a generic “more testing needed” response.

An appropriate response begins by classifying the packet: the supplied numbers are teaching assumptions or example observations, not automatically operational evidence. For this week's situation, the essential conceptual distinction is: Morphological analysis creates structured divergence: enumerate choices along independent dimensions before combining them. Feasibility screening comes before preference scoring, and the elimination reason should be recorded. Set-based reasoning keeps several promising alternatives alive while the most consequential uncertainty is retired.

A credible submission explicitly handles the counterargument that a numerical score must not rescue an alternative that violates a mandatory safety constraint. State whether a different plausible assumption could overturn your recommendation and what experiment would discriminate the possibilities. Strong work can conclude that the information is insufficient; unsupported unconditional approval should score poorly.

The best answer is not uniquely determined by this case. Grade the traceability, calculation, falsifiability, and clarity of decision limits rather than whether the learner matches a preferred diagram or recommendation.
