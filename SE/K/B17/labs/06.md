# Hypothetical Lab 06 — Root-cause triangulation

**Course:** EN.645.783 — Systems Engineering Process Improvement  
**Read first:** [Chapter 06](../chapters/06.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 6](../../../C/P4/EN645783.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **process capability assessment, root causes, and midcourse exam**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Build two competing causes for late reviews and identify an observation that would separate them.

## Provided evidence and constraints

The apparent “slow reviewer” has a queue of incomplete change packages. Measure input quality and arrival rate before adding reviewers.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W06; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

A five-whys chain is a possible causal explanation; look for disconfirming cases, workload severity, rework loops and interface defects. Root cause can be multi-factorial and evolving.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

<details><summary>Expand reference reasoning</summary>

A defensible answer begins with **use root-cause hypotheses, not blame stories**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. A fishbone or five-whys session generates hypotheses; it does not identify causes by itself. Triangulate process maps, event logs, interviews, defect records and direct observation. Assess process capability relative to explicitly stated requirements while separating special causes from ordinary variation.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Build two competing causes for late reviews and identify an observation that would separate them. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
