# Hypothetical Lab 04 — Event-log integrity

**Course:** EN.645.783 — Systems Engineering Process Improvement  
**Read first:** [Chapter 04](../chapters/04.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 4](../../../C/P4/EN645783.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **current-state process mapping, event logs, and conformance**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Flag any event sequence where safety review is later than approval; describe two alternative explanations.

## Provided evidence and constraints

Case C4 has events Submitted 09:00, Approved 11:00, SafetyReview 14:00. This is a potential approval-before-safety exception; ask whether the log order reflects reality before concluding misconduct.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W04; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Process-mining claims depend on consistent case IDs, clocks and recorded actors. An activity appearing out of order may reflect batching, synchronization, parallel execution or a real bypass. Investigate before correcting the process.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

<details><summary>Expand reference reasoning</summary>

A defensible answer begins with **an event log can show what actually happens**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. A process model is a claim; an event log is a partial observation. Map each case ID to time-stamped events, activities and actors. Compare expected vs observed sequences while considering incomplete logs, time-zone errors, parallel work and legitimate exceptions.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Flag any event sequence where safety review is later than approval; describe two alternative explanations. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
