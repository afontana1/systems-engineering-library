# Hypothetical Lab 07 — Simulation credibility

**Course:** EN.645.783 — Systems Engineering Process Improvement  
**Read first:** [Chapter 07](../chapters/07.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 7](../../../C/P4/EN645783.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **process modeling and simulation for improvement decisions**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Model a two-reviewer queue with demand uncertainty and assess both mean and upper-tail lead time.

## Provided evidence and constraints

A two-reviewer queue receives eight cases/week and processes ten/week in total. Stability is possible, but heavy-tailed complexity can still produce long p90 lead times; run replications and report uncertainty.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W07; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Process simulations require input rates, service-time distributions, routing rules and rework. Compare simulated lead-time distributions with observed logs before trusting intervention forecasts.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

<details><summary>Expand reference reasoning</summary>

A defensible answer begins with **simulations are experiments on assumptions**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. A queue or event simulation lets the organization test WIP limits, extra reviewers and batching without disrupting production. Calibration and validation require comparing output distributions with observed case data, not just matching the average. Track utilization, service-time variability and feedback-induced demand.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Model a two-reviewer queue with demand uncertainty and assess both mean and upper-tail lead time. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
