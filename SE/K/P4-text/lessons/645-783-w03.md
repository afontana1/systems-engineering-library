# EN.645.783 · Week 03 — Process definition, boundaries, stakeholders, and outcomes

**Phase 4 Companion Textbook · Systems Engineering Process Improvement**  
**Primary concept:** Process boundaries and SIPOC  
**Status:** Original companion explanation plus adapted excerpts from the supplied Week 3 learning guide.  
**Data:** Worked numerical values are synthetic teaching illustrations, not field observations.

## Why this idea matters

A systems engineer must produce a decision that remains defensible when the conditions change. This chapter focuses on **process boundaries and sipoc**: how to reason from purpose and evidence to a bounded action. The objective is not to memorize a term, but to state what it permits, when it fails, and what observable evidence would change your conclusion.

## Build the mental model

A process has an entry condition, accountable boundary, terminal output and process customer. SIPOC (Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers) exposes where handoffs, authority and evidence are unclear. Include external regulators, suppliers and operators where they affect the result. Define what a process instance is; otherwise lead-time comparisons mix unlike cases.

**Useful diagnostic distinction:** A neat swimlane without precisely defined events cannot support defensible process metrics.

## The underlying mechanisms: lessons to retain

The following instructional notes are adapted from the corresponding original course guide. They form a compact mechanism checklist to use while reading the new explanation and doing the chapter exercise.

* Boundary choice is a hypothesis about where causal leverage and governance reside.
* A process outcome is an achieved result, not an activity such as “hold a review.”
* Closure requires explicit technical and configuration evidence; an administrative status alone is insufficient.
* Stakeholder analysis must include people who bear process burden and risk, not only process owners and executives.
* Process requirements should be testable and distinguish mandatory outcomes from preferred performance targets.

## Worked reasoning: the Northstar mobility case

Northstar defines the process start as acceptance of a valid change request and end as verified release or formal rejection. Requests waiting for clarification are classified separately so the clock cannot be manipulated by delaying “acceptance.”

### Compare with the guide's worked case

The original guide uses the following short case discussion. Reproduce the reasoning with a changed assumption rather than copying its conclusion.

The worked example rejects “change control from submission to approval” because it hides implementation, V&V, baseline closure, and operational feedback. The accepted boundary starts with a recognized change trigger and ends only when the authorized baseline, evidence, communication, and residual-risk record are complete—or when the request is formally rejected with rationale.

Reproduce the example using controlled course data, change one assumption, and explain how the result or decision changes.

### How to challenge the example

1. **Boundary:** Name the engineered product, process, actors and environment relevant to this claim. List one important exclusion.
2. **Evidence:** Identify one source that would let an independent reviewer reproduce or falsify the result.
3. **Failure mode:** Test what happens when an input is stale, unavailable, biased, incorrectly configured or in conflict with another artifact.
4. **Decision:** State who can authorize the next action, which guardrail could block it, and how the decision will be recorded.

## Learning objectives from the original guide

* Define process purpose, outcomes, start/end events, units of work, states, and closure criteria.
* Identify customers, suppliers, performers, authorities, affected parties, and information owners.
* Construct SIPOC, context, outcome, and process-requirement views.
* Define process performance objectives and acceptance criteria.

## Practice questions with guided answers

Attempt the questions before expanding each answer. These five knowledge checks and their answer guidance are **adapted from the supplied Week 3 course guide**; their inclusion does not replace the larger independent assignment.

1. **What is the unit of work?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   The item whose progression and outcome the process manages—in this case a controlled change request or change package.

   </details>

2. **Why model states?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   To clarify valid progression, waiting, rejection, rework, cancellation, completion, and evidence conditions.

   </details>

3. **What makes a process outcome useful?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   It is observable, stakeholder-relevant, and linked to process purpose.

   </details>

4. **Why include neighboring processes?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   Inputs, outputs, constraints, and feedback often cross the selected boundary.

   </details>

5. **What is the purpose of a definition review?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   To prevent detailed analysis of an incoherent or unauthorized process scope.

   </details>

## Extended transfer exercise

Construct a one-page **claim–evidence–decision map** for this week's topic. Include a claim, current baseline, configuration or version, measurable response, source and timestamp of evidence, alternative explanation, reviewer, unresolved uncertainty and stop/go rule. Then deliberately inject one defect (missing source, wrong version, unexpected consequence or inadequate coverage) and explain how the decision should change.

**Stretch:** Use a second scenario with a different consequence level or stakeholder group. Explain why the same method may require different assurance controls.

## Bridge to the assessed curriculum

The companion explains the concepts; the original guide supplies the complete assignment, sources, rubric and formal mastery gate. This is the guide's weekly deliverable:

**Process Definition Review Package** containing charter v2, context and SIPOC, stakeholders, unit-of-work states, outcomes, process requirements, quality attributes, assumptions, exclusions, review criteria, findings, and dispositions.

Submit native or textual source, controlled data and transformations, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

**Common critical failures to avoid (from the guide):**

* Using approval as the only definition of process completion.
* Excluding performers or affected stakeholders from the process definition.
* Writing activities as outcomes or unverifiable process requirements.
* Proceeding with unresolved boundary or authority ambiguity.

**Mastery and revision standard:**

Pass the Process Definition Review with no unresolved critical finding on purpose, scope, authority, outcome, or closure evidence.
