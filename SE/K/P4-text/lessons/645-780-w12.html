EN.645.780 · Week 12 — Present, challenge, revise, and defend the Agile Systems Engineering operating model

Primary concept: Defending an agile operating model

Why this idea matters

A systems engineer must produce a decision that remains defensible when the conditions change. This chapter focuses on defending an agile operating model: how to reason from purpose and evidence to a bounded action. The objective is not to memorize a term, but to state what it permits, when it fails, and what observable evidence would change your conclusion.

Build the mental model

A defensible transformation proposal connects baseline pain, an explicit response objective, candidate interventions, pilot design, assurance boundaries, owner and measurements. Show what you will stop, start and sustain. Stage changes so that outcomes can be attributed to interventions and unintended effects become visible. A decision board needs the limits of evidence, financial exposure and criteria for reversing course.

Useful diagnostic distinction: A transformation slide deck is not evidence of a functioning operating model.

The underlying mechanisms: lessons to retain

The following instructional notes are adapted from the corresponding original course guide. They form a compact mechanism checklist to use while reading the new explanation and doing the chapter exercise.

  • The final product is a recommendation to leadership, not a generic agile handbook.
  • Every proposed practice should connect to a case-specific problem, mechanism, expected measure, risk, and learning/reversal plan.
  • A credible transformation uses staged experiments and does not promise enterprise-wide benefits before evidence.
  • Benefits should include response, outcome, flow, quality, integration, workforce, and economic dimensions with baselines and uncertainty.
  • The oral defense tests personal understanding. A polished deck cannot compensate for inability to explain calculations, decisions, or limitations.
  • The final retrospective separates course-method lessons, case conclusions, unresolved uncertainties, and repository improvements.

Worked reasoning: the Northstar mobility case

The CEO asks whether to adopt a company-wide framework. Present three staged options instead: local queue repair, selected cross-functional team pilots and enterprise-scale governance. Recommend the pilot only after showing lead-time, defect-escape and safety-evidence baselines with a decision date.

The final recommendation does not state “implement Scrum and DevSecOps.” It proposes three 90-day experiments: (1) accessibility capability team with bounded interface authority, (2) weekly interface/integration evidence flow using the simulator and digital-thread queries, and (3) risk-tiered continuous assurance in the pipeline. How to challenge the example

  1. Boundary: Name the engineered product, process, actors and environment relevant to this claim. List one important exclusion.
  2. Evidence: Identify one source that would let an independent reviewer reproduce or falsify the result.
  3. Failure mode: Test what happens when an input is stale, unavailable, biased, incorrectly configured or in conflict with another artifact.
  4. Decision: State who can authorize the next action, which guardrail could block it, and how the decision will be recorded.

Extended transfer exercise

Construct a one-page claim–evidence–decision map for this week's topic. Include a claim, current baseline, configuration or version, measurable response, source and timestamp of evidence, alternative explanation, reviewer, unresolved uncertainty and stop/go rule. Then deliberately inject one defect (missing source, wrong version, unexpected consequence or inadequate coverage) and explain how the decision should change.

Stretch: Use a second scenario with a different consequence level or stakeholder group. Explain why the same method may require different assurance controls.

Bridge to the assessed curriculum

The companion explains the concepts; the original guide supplies the complete assignment, sources, rubric and formal mastery gate. This is the guide's weekly deliverable:

Final Agile Systems Engineering CEO Recommendation and Operating Model containing executive decision brief, context/suitability, principles and tailoring, value/learning roadmap, team/governance model, flow design, architecture strategy, digital/evidence thread, continuous assurance and pipeline, scale/transition plan, measures, investment, risks, pre-mortem, review dispositions, appendices, and 20-minute oral defense.

Submit controlled source files, data/calculations, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review against the rubric. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Common critical failures to avoid (from the guide):

  • Submitting a framework summary instead of a case recommendation.
  • Promising benefits without baseline, mechanism, measure, and experiment.
  • Leaving critical review findings or risks hidden.
  • Failing the live-change or oral-defense demonstration of personal understanding.

Mastery and revision standard:

Achieve at least 80% overall and Proficient or better on every critical capstone dimension. Pass the oral defense, resolve all critical findings, and explicitly accept or plan every residual major risk.
