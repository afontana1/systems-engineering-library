# EN.645.782 · Week 12 — Integrated digital engineering for mission decisions

## Why this idea matters

A systems engineer must produce a decision that remains defensible when the conditions change. This chapter focuses on **integrating digital engineering into mission decisions**: how to reason from purpose and evidence to a bounded action. The objective is not to memorize a term, but to state what it permits, when it fails, and what observable evidence would change your conclusion.

## Build the mental model

An integrated strategy should demonstrate a complete bounded thread: mission claim → system requirement → model hypothesis → candidate intervention → simulation and test evidence → accountable decision. Assign ownership and change control to each link. Explain where uncertainty prevents approval, how cyber constraints affect data access, and what pilot will close the next critical gap.

**Useful diagnostic distinction:** The final product is a reviewable decision record, not a portfolio of disconnected digital pictures.

## The underlying mechanisms: lessons to retain

The following instructional notes are adapted from the corresponding original course guide. They form a compact mechanism checklist to use while reading the new explanation and doing the chapter exercise.

* The capstone should request a decision and authorization, not merely summarize learning.
* Benefits should be tied to baseline and measured decision outcomes; uncertainty about benefit should be explicit.
* A staged strategy should prioritize use cases and infrastructure that create reusable capability while controlling risk.
* Live change is the strongest test of whether the thread, model, analytics, and governance are real.
* Mature digital engineering includes knowing what cannot yet be trusted, automated, shared, or reused.

## Worked reasoning: the Northstar mobility case

In a final review, the sponsor asks to approve a new dispatch algorithm. Present performance gains for the approved scenarios, a stale-twin failure case, accessibility evidence status, residual risks and a recommended controlled pilot. Do not present an unqualified “mission success” percentage.

### Compare with the guide's worked case

The original guide uses the following short case discussion. Reproduce the reasoning with a changed assumption rather than copying its conclusion.

The review board changes the severe-weather assumption: the primary cellular network fails two hours earlier and mutual-aid vehicles have inconsistent accessibility metadata. The example runs the impact query, updates mission assumptions and affected threads, revises the analytic scenario, regenerates the recommendation, and records why the preferred portfolio changes from data-sharing plus mutual aid to local pre-positioning plus verified accessible capacity.

Reproduce the example using controlled course data, change one assumption, and explain how the decision or evidence changes.

### How to challenge the example

1. **Boundary:** Name the engineered product, process, actors and environment relevant to this claim. List one important exclusion.
2. **Evidence:** Identify one source that would let an independent reviewer reproduce or falsify the result.
3. **Failure mode:** Test what happens when an input is stale, unavailable, biased, incorrectly configured or in conflict with another artifact.
4. **Decision:** State who can authorize the next action, which guardrail could block it, and how the decision will be recorded.

## Learning objectives from the original guide

* Integrate strategy, competencies, model portfolio, MBSE, ecosystem, thread, twin, mission architecture, and analysis.
* Quantify benefits, costs, resources, risks, transition stages, measures, and governance.
* Demonstrate live queries, provenance, model/twin state, and change impact.
* Adapt the mission recommendation to a changed assumption while preserving configuration and rationale.
* Defend evidence, limitations, use restrictions, and residual risk to executive and technical reviewers.

## Practice questions with guided answers

Attempt the questions before expanding each answer. These five knowledge checks and their answer guidance are **adapted from the supplied Week 12 course guide**; their inclusion does not replace the larger independent assignment.

1. **What is the strongest evidence that your digital engineering approach adds value?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   A measurable improvement in a consequential decision or evidence cycle with a credible causal chain, not artifact volume.

   </details>

2. **What is the weakest link in your evidence chain?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   Learner must identify a specific authority, data, model, transformation, interface, or governance weakness and its consequence.

   </details>

3. **Why is the live change important?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   It demonstrates connected semantics, impact analysis, reproducibility, configuration, decision update, and organizational response.

   </details>

4. **What should EN.645.783 improve?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   A measurable process bottleneck or governance weakness revealed by actual evidence, not a generic maturity aspiration.

   </details>

5. **When should the organization not use the twin or mission model?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   Outside approved use, context, data quality, synchronization, validation, uncertainty, security, or authority limits.

   </details>

## Extended transfer exercise

Construct a one-page **claim–evidence–decision map** for this week's topic. Include a claim, current baseline, configuration or version, measurable response, source and timestamp of evidence, alternative explanation, reviewer, unresolved uncertainty and stop/go rule. Then deliberately inject one defect (missing source, wrong version, unexpected consequence or inadequate coverage) and explain how the decision should change.

**Stretch:** Use a second scenario with a different consequence level or stakeholder group. Explain why the same method may require different assurance controls.

## Bridge to the assessed curriculum

The companion explains the concepts; the original guide supplies the complete assignment, sources, rubric and formal mastery gate. This is the guide's weekly deliverable:

**Final Digital Mission Engineering Strategy and Evidence Release** containing all capstone contents, controlled source, three live queries, twin state demonstration, live-change result, executive decision request, review recording, findings, dispositions, and handoff to EN.645.783 and Phase 5.

Submit native or textual source, data and transformations, generated views, query/analysis source, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

**Common critical failures to avoid (from the guide):**

* Providing a portfolio of disconnected artifacts rather than an integrated decision system.
* Failing the clean reproduction or live change without transparent recovery.
* Overstating twin, thread, model, or mission-analysis credibility.
* Recommending transformation without owners, resources, measures, risks, and stop/scale criteria.
