# EN.645.782 · Week 02 — Digital Engineering strategy, policy, competencies, and adoption

## Why this idea matters

A systems engineer must produce a decision that remains defensible when the conditions change. This chapter focuses on **digital-engineering strategy and workforce adoption**: how to reason from purpose and evidence to a bounded action. The objective is not to memorize a term, but to state what it permits, when it fails, and what observable evidence would change your conclusion.

## Build the mental model

A strategy is a capability roadmap, not a procurement catalog. Align policy, workforce competence, model methods, identifiers, data governance, infrastructure and leadership incentives with measurable decision outcomes. The 2018 DoD Digital Engineering Strategy emphasizes model-based decisions, authoritative sources, innovation, infrastructure and workforce transformation. Local adoption should select a thin vertical slice through these goals before investing across the enterprise.

**Useful diagnostic distinction:** Tool installation is an input, not a digital-engineering outcome.

## The underlying mechanisms: lessons to retain

The following instructional notes are adapted from the corresponding original course guide. They form a compact mechanism checklist to use while reading the new explanation and doing the chapter exercise.

* The 2018 strategy defines direction; DoDI 5000.97 establishes policy and responsibility; program plans define the actual implementation.
* The five goals are interdependent. Models without authority, infrastructure, methods, and workforce do not create a digital engineering capability.
* DECF is a workforce framework, not a single job description. Roles require different proficiency across data, models, methods, environment, and behaviors.
* Benefits should be framed as decision quality, cycle time, defect prevention, reuse, confidence, or lifecycle cost—not number of models or tools.
* Adoption should begin with high-value use cases, controlled pilots, baseline measures, and explicit stop/scale criteria.

## Worked reasoning: the Northstar mobility case

Pilot one Northstar decision: approve a fleet-size option. Specify who owns the demand model, which baseline it uses, what simulations answer the question, how outputs are reviewed, and how changes are traced. Measure decision rework before and after the pilot instead of counting software licenses.

### Compare with the guide's worked case

The original guide uses the following short case discussion. Reproduce the reasoning with a changed assumption rather than copying its conclusion.

A proposed initiative says, “Connect all tools and train everyone in SysML.” The example decomposes it into three use cases: automated requirement-to-test coverage, supplier-interface change impact, and emergency-route mission analysis. It maps each use case to strategy goals, competencies, owners, measures, data rights, risks, and a six-month pilot decision.

Reproduce the example using controlled course data, change one assumption, and explain how the decision or evidence changes.

### How to challenge the example

1. **Boundary:** Name the engineered product, process, actors and environment relevant to this claim. List one important exclusion.
2. **Evidence:** Identify one source that would let an independent reviewer reproduce or falsify the result.
3. **Failure mode:** Test what happens when an input is stale, unavailable, biased, incorrectly configured or in conflict with another artifact.
4. **Decision:** State who can authorize the next action, which guardrail could block it, and how the decision will be recorded.

## Learning objectives from the original guide

* Explain the five Digital Engineering Strategy goals and the procedural expectations of DoDI 5000.97.
* Apply the DoD Digital Engineering Fundamentals to a program-level adoption plan.
* Assess workforce capability using DECF concepts and identify role-specific gaps.
* Define a portfolio of digital-engineering use cases with benefits hypotheses and measures.

3. **Why is a use-case portfolio better than a tool roadmap?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   </details>

4. **What does DECF add?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   </details>

5. **What is a guardrail measure?**

   <details><summary>Show explanation</summary>

   A measure that detects harm, gaming, or degraded assurance while pursuing the primary benefit.

   </details>

## Extended transfer exercise

Construct a one-page **claim–evidence–decision map** for this week's topic. Include a claim, current baseline, configuration or version, measurable response, source and timestamp of evidence, alternative explanation, reviewer, unresolved uncertainty and stop/go rule. Then deliberately inject one defect (missing source, wrong version, unexpected consequence or inadequate coverage) and explain how the decision should change.

**Stretch:** Use a second scenario with a different consequence level or stakeholder group. Explain why the same method may require different assurance controls.

## Bridge to the assessed curriculum

The companion explains the concepts; the original guide supplies the complete assignment, sources, rubric and formal mastery gate. This is the guide's weekly deliverable:

**Digital Engineering Strategy, Use-Case, and Competency Package** containing policy crosswalk, use-case portfolio, benefit hypotheses, role/competency assessment, governance assignments, measures, pilot roadmap, risks, and review briefing. Length: 12–18 pages plus matrices.

Submit native or textual source, data and transformations, generated views, query/analysis source, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

**Common critical failures to avoid (from the guide):**

* Measuring success by tool count, model count, training hours, or digitized documents alone.
* Copying policy language without program implications or evidence.
* Assigning every role the same competency profile.
* Ignoring data rights, cybersecurity, contracting, sustainment, or workforce transition.

**Mastery and revision standard:**

Approve no more than four first-wave use cases. Each must have a decision owner, value hypothesis, baseline, target, guardrail, competency owner, data/model needs, rights/security constraints, and stop/scale criteria. Resolve all major review findings.
