EN.645.771 · Week 03 — Mission outcomes, capability dependencies, and logical SoS architecture

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Mission thread and dependency graph
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 3

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

Begin with an operational outcome—such as transferring vulnerable patients to a safe destination—rather than a list of installed technologies. Trace the mission thread through detection, dispatch, routing, handoff and confirmation. Each link needs a responsible actor, precondition, interface, deadline, evidence and fallback. The logical architecture describes the capabilities and information relationships required, without prematurely selecting a vendor or physical implementation.

A capability dependency graph is not the same as a product decomposition. It captures which functions must succeed together, which may substitute, and where resources or governance constrain coordination. AND dependencies, OR alternatives and time windows should be explicit.

A boundary worth remembering: Do not equate counts of satisfied requirements with probability of mission success.

Work through an independent case

A successful transfer requires an accessible vehicle AND a staffed receiving facility AND current route authorization. Radio contact may be provided by city network OR hospital backup. Draw the AND/OR graph, then block the hospital node: a working vehicle alone cannot fulfill the outcome.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Mission threads are scenario-specific sequences of activities and decisions required to achieve an outcome.
  • Logical architecture asks what roles, functions, information, and decisions are needed before naming current systems.
  • Capability dependencies may be conjunctive, alternative, conditional, capacity-limited, or time-dependent.
  • A cut set identifies a structural vulnerability only under stated assumptions about success and substitution.
  • Measures belong at outcome, mission-thread, interface, and constituent levels and must not be conflated.

The guide's worked illustration

The evacuation outcome is “transport all high-priority patients to accepting facilities within medically approved time windows while maintaining accessible handling and custody records.” The logical thread requires assess, prioritize, match destination, allocate accessible transport, establish route, move, hand over, and confirm. The current rideshare platform is one possible performer for a subset of tasks, not part of the logical definition.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Active reading task: Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

Learning outcomes from the original guide

  1. Define measurable mission outcomes, scenarios, constraints, and off-nominal conditions.
  2. Construct mission threads and capability dependencies independent of current implementations.
  3. Identify timing, information, decision, and resource dependencies across threads.
  4. Develop a logical architecture with alternatives and traceability to outcomes.
  5. Analyze path, cut-set, bottleneck, and substitutability questions without overclaiming.

<details><summary>What distinguishes a mission outcome from an MOP?</summary>

The outcome expresses mission success; an MOP describes a system or process performance contributing to it.

</details>

<details><summary>Why create solution-independent threads?</summary>

To expose required behavior and enable real alternatives rather than ratifying the current implementation.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a conditional dependency?</summary>

A capability or task is required only under a stated scenario, state, or prior result.

</details>

<details><summary>What does a cut set mean?</summary>

A set of losses that disconnects success under the model assumptions; it is not automatically a real-world failure proof.

</details>

<details><summary>Why include degraded threads?</summary>

SoS value often depends on reconfiguration and continuity when constituents or information are unavailable.

</details>

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Define three mission scenarios: normal disruption, severe storm, and hospital evacuation.
  • Construct one solution-independent mission thread and identify decisions and exchanges.
  • Build a capability dependency graph with alternatives and conditional branches.
  • Calculate candidate cut sets and then challenge their assumptions.
  • Create two logical architecture variants and compare their mission implications.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

Mission and Logical Architecture Baseline v0.3: outcomes, measures, scenarios, mission threads, capability model, dependency graph, logical nodes, alternatives, and trace links.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Encoding the incumbent physical solution as the logical architecture.
  • No off-nominal or degraded thread.
  • Critical task, decision, or exchange with no owner or dependency.
  • Using graph metrics without mission-semantic validation.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Achieve complete outcome-to-thread-to-capability traceability for at least three scenarios and correct every unsupported critical-path or cut-set claim.
