## Explain the concept

Intervention changes the system being understood. A policy pilot may alter trust, incentives and adaptive behavior, so test using a small, reversible and monitored action where appropriate. Dynamic risk assessment anticipates indirect and delayed effects as well as immediate technical failure.

Define safe-to-learn conditions with explicit non-negotiable constraints, observation periods, incident reporting, exit criteria and stakeholder authority. Use a portfolio of options when one large irreversible change would suppress valuable learning.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A small pilot can still impose unacceptable risk on a vulnerable group.

## Work through an independent case

Pilot a new accessible booking handoff for one route cluster, preserving an existing fallback. Monitor journey completion, complaint distribution and staff workload weekly. Stop the pilot if verified safety incidents exceed the predeclared control threshold; include riders in interpreting ambiguous findings.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Safe-to-learn does not mean low stakes; it means bounded exposure, protected essentials, monitoring, and the ability to stop or adapt.
* Dynamic risks change as actors learn, dependencies shift, and interventions redistribute load.
* Resilience includes anticipation, absorption, adaptation, recovery, and institutional learning.
* Leading indicators should be tied to mechanisms, not selected because they are easy to count.
* Guardrails protect critical outcomes and vulnerable groups; stop conditions convert ethics into operational decisions.
* Flexible intervention requires configuration control so changes and outcomes remain interpretable.

### The guide's worked illustration

Pilot a protected emergency-access protocol in two districts rather than region-wide. The pilot includes minimum accessibility service, workforce load limits, offline dispatch fallback, public communication, independent monitoring, and stop criteria for inequitable denial rates, safety incidents, or unmanageable staff fatigue.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Select one bounded intervention and state hypotheses about mechanisms and actor responses.
2. Map cascading risk, common-cause conditions, burden transfer, and vulnerable populations.
3. Define resilience functions and minimum acceptable service.
4. Specify leading indicators, outcome measures, guardrails, stop/reversal criteria, and review cadence.
5. Run tabletop stress tests for at least four compound disruptions and revise the pilot.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Intervention, Dynamic Risk, and Resilience Package**

Required contents:

* intervention hypotheses and causal mechanisms
* scope, authority, participants, and protected essential outcomes
* dynamic risk and cascading-effect map
* resilience and degraded-operation strategy
* measures, leading indicators, guardrails, and stop conditions
* stress tests and revised pilot design
* learning, governance, configuration, and communication plan

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, approach used, assumptions, boundary judgments, evidence, affected groups, limitations, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Proposes a large irreversible rollout as the first learning step.
* Uses generic risk scores without propagation, indicators, or ownership.
* Defines success while omitting safety, accessibility, workforce, or equity guardrails.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the Intervention and Resilience Review only when essential outcomes are protected, decision rules are explicit, and the pilot can generate useful learning without unacceptable exposure.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
