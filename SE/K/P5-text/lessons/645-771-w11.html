EN.645.771 · Week 11 — Decision making, deployment, evolution, and portfolio roadmap

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Roadmap, option value and decision triggers
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 11

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

An SoS roadmap is an investment and commitment strategy under changing constituent availability. Model alternatives as staged options: pilots, contract changes, interfaces, fallback capacity and technical retirement. Use common scenarios, benefits, costs, risk and dependencies; preserve alternatives that remain valuable under plausible future conditions.

A roadmap should contain trigger conditions and accountable decision owners, not just calendar milestones. Dependencies can make a small shared-data investment more valuable than a large local capability upgrade because it unlocks coordination across constituents.

A boundary worth remembering: A weighted score is only as credible as its scales, constraints, uncertainty analysis and governance.

Work through an independent case

A proposed unified dispatcher costs $2 million and needs unanimous adoption. A $0.35 million standards-and-adapters initiative enables three immediate exchanges and can later support the dispatcher. Compare the early option's reversible investment to the integrated option's lock-in; do not assume all constituent agreements will materialize.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • A feasible alternative includes authority, funding, agreements, workforce, transition, interfaces, evidence, and operations—not only technical performance.
  • Robustness asks whether an alternative remains acceptable across plausible futures, not whether its expected score is highest.
  • Real options preserve the ability to learn, defer, scale, switch, or abandon at a known cost.
  • Roadmaps should align mission increments, constituent upgrades, interface versions, tests, agreements, training, and decommissioning.
  • Decision records must state what would change the recommendation and who can make that change.

The guide's worked illustration

The centralized regional broker has the best nominal throughput but depends on a new legal authority and two vendors’ proprietary feeds. The federated directory performs slightly worse in the mean but remains acceptable under vendor withdrawal, supports phased adoption, and has lower regret. The recommendation selects the federated path with a time-bounded broker pilot as an option.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Active reading task: Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

Learning outcomes from the original guide

  1. Define decision authority, alternatives, objectives, measures, constraints, and uncertainties.
  2. Compare at least three architecture/evolution paths including governance-first and low-integration options.
  3. Perform uncertainty, sensitivity, robustness, regret, and decision-reversal analysis.
  4. Develop deployment waves, compatibility windows, triggers, options, and retirement/exit strategies.
  5. Create an implementable coalition roadmap with agreements, funding, evidence, and review points.

<details><summary>What is regret?</summary>

The loss from selecting an alternative compared with the best choice after the future becomes known.

<details><summary>What is a real option?</summary>

A designed right, not obligation, to defer, scale, switch, expand, or abandon as evidence changes.

</details>

<details><summary>Why include a current-course baseline?</summary>

To reveal the value, cost, and risk of intervention relative to doing nothing or continuing existing plans.

</details>

<details><summary>What belongs in a decision-reversal condition?</summary>

Observable threshold, evidence source, authority, timing, affected artifacts, and required action.

</details>

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Define the Week 11 decision and authority.
  • Construct three feasible alternatives and a no-action/current-course baseline.
  • Build objectives, measures, thresholds, uncertainty ranges, and stakeholder perspectives.
  • Run sensitivity, robustness, regret, and decision-reversal analysis.
  • Create a three-wave roadmap with compatibility, evidence, governance, and exit criteria.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

SoS Evolution Decision Baseline v1.1: decision frame, alternatives, objectives/measures, uncertainty, sensitivity, robustness, regret, participation scenarios, roadmap, agreements/funding needs, triggers, options, exit strategy, and signed recommendation.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Comparing technically attractive but governance-infeasible alternatives.
  • Hiding stakeholder conflict in a single unexplained weighted score.
  • No decision-reversal or participation sensitivity.
  • Roadmap without interfaces, evidence, agreements, resources, or retirement.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Pass the SoS Evolution Decision Review with feasible alternatives, transparent stakeholder conflict, correct uncertainty analysis, and an authorized, evidence-linked roadmap.
