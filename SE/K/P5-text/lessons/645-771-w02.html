EN.645.771 · Week 02 — Managing SoS development, governance, acquisition, fielding, and evolution

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Authority-constrained evolution
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 2

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

An SoS roadmap operates across different acquisition schedules and business priorities. A constituent owner may reject a technically sensible interface change because its own service obligations are more urgent. Governance mechanisms—memoranda of agreement, shared service-level targets, change boards and escalation paths—make cooperation feasible without pretending authority is unlimited.

Separate decision rights for mission objectives, interoperability contracts, local operations, emergency exceptions, and funding. Record each actor's incentive to participate, the minimum viable agreement, and the exit conditions. A delivery promise unsupported by constituent commitment is a planning assumption, not a baseline.

A boundary worth remembering: Directed, acknowledged, collaborative and virtual SoS are governance archetypes, not maturity rankings.

Work through an independent case

A regional coordinator requests real-time accessible capacity from two carriers. Carrier A will offer data every minute; carrier B permits 15-minute summaries. Instead of assuming identical telemetry, the first release provides a conservative dispatch rule that flags carrier B information as stale for acute emergencies.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Authority must be modeled per decision: architecture standards, interface changes, data sharing, test access, operations, funding, and emergency control may have different owners.
  • Governance defines how decisions are made and enforced; collaboration alone is not a control mechanism.
  • SoS configuration management often controls agreements, interfaces, mission baselines, and evidence rather than every constituent internal baseline.
  • Evolution plans need triggers, windows, compatibility policies, sunset rules, and fallback modes.
  • Participation is uncertain. Treat withdrawal, delay, noncompliance, and strategic behavior as scenario variables.

The guide's worked illustration

The regional coordinator can set emergency-routing objectives and convene reviews but cannot force a private rideshare vendor to expose vehicle-health data. A hospital can require arrival information for its own campus but cannot change municipal signal timing. The governance design therefore creates an interface council, emergency authority protocol, data-use agreements, test memorandum, and compatibility window rather than a fictitious single configuration board.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Create an authority matrix for ten decisions.
  • Map constituent lifecycle milestones and identify incompatible windows.
  • Draft a governance charter with representation, quorum, escalation, emergency authority, and dispute resolution.
  • Define controlled SoS artifacts and which constituent artifacts remain references only.
  • Run a scenario in which a vendor declines a required change.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

SoS Governance and Evolution Baseline v0.2: authority/decision matrix, governance charter, lifecycle synchronization map, agreements inventory, configuration strategy, participation risks, and evolution assumptions.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Assigning authority to an entity that does not possess it.
  • Using RACI as a substitute for consent, funding, or legal authority.
  • No process for constituent withdrawal, asynchronous upgrade, or emergency operation.
  • Attempting to control internal constituent baselines without agreement.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Pass the SoS Framing and Governance Review with no unowned mission-critical decision, fictitious authority, or unmanaged lifecycle incompatibility.
