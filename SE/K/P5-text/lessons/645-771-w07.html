EN.645.771 · Week 07 — Bayesian networks, causality, and decision uncertainty

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Bayesian belief and causal structure
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 7

Explain the concept

A Bayesian network is a probability model of conditional dependencies, not automatically a causal model. To update beliefs, define mutually exclusive hypotheses, priors and observation likelihoods; then calculate posterior probabilities. The direction of arrows and conditioning assumptions should be tested against a causal story if intervention decisions are required.

Prior sensitivity matters when evidence is scarce. A positive alarm can still imply a low event probability if false positives are common and the event base rate is low. For causal claims, examine confounders, selection effects and interventions rather than interpreting any conditional probability as a lever.

A boundary worth remembering: Posterior probability depends on the prior and test conditions; a Bayesian graph alone does not prove causality.

Work through an independent case

Suppose a route obstruction occurs 5% of the time, the alarm detects it 90% of the time, and false alarms occur on 15% of clear routes. After an alarm, the posterior obstruction probability is (0.05×0.9)/(0.05×0.9+0.95×0.15) ≈ 24%. The alarm warrants checking, not certainty.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • A Bayesian network factorizes a joint distribution according to graph assumptions; arrows do not automatically prove causation.
  • Conditional independence is the source of computational structure and a major source of model error.
  • CPTs may come from data, expert elicitation, physics, simulation, or combinations; provenance and uncertainty matter.
  • Calibration asks whether events assigned a probability occur at that rate in comparable cases.
  • A decision requires utilities, losses, constraints, and authority in addition to posterior probability.

The guide's worked illustration

Nodes represent storm severity, utility outage, signal availability, telecom degradation, reported route status, and actual route usability. A naive model treats signal and telecom reports as independent given route status, but both depend on the same utility outage. Adding the common cause lowers the apparent confidence from corroborating reports and changes the emergency-routing threshold.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Define a six-to-ten-node causal hypothesis for route or constituent availability.
  • Specify node states, evidence sources, and CPT provenance.
  • Verify normalization, impossible states, and d-separation expectations.
  • Run posterior updates for normal, conflicting, and missing evidence.
  • Perform one-way sensitivity and decision-threshold analysis.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Using arrows as evidence of causation.
  • Invalid or undocumented CPTs.
  • Double counting correlated evidence.
  • Reporting posterior precision unsupported by data/model quality.
  • Making a safety-critical decision without losses, thresholds, and authority.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Verify the network, document every CPT, pass structural and parameter sensitivity tests, and defend the decision threshold without overstating causality or precision.
