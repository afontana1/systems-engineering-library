## Explain the concept

An enterprise changes in response to its own outcomes. Funding affects service, service affects ridership, ridership affects political support, and political support affects funding. Delays between investments and visible outcomes can generate overcorrection or underinvestment. Model reinforcing and balancing feedback before recommending a structural change.

An evidence-based current-state assessment uses behavior over time, not snapshots alone. Distinguish stocks from flows: trained operators is a stock, hiring and attrition are flows. Test whether an apparent causal link has a plausible mechanism and observational support.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A feedback-loop diagram is a hypothesis about mechanisms, not validated causal proof.

## Work through an independent case

Budget cuts reduce operator training capacity. Service quality falls months later, ridership then falls, and revenue drops. A short-term cost-saving dashboard misses the delayed reinforcing loop. Model training backlog, qualified workforce stock and service quality separately.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Reference modes are evidence-backed patterns over time, not decorative line sketches.
* Stocks often include backlog, trained workforce, trust, asset condition, technical debt, and committed funding.
* Delays and nonlinear limits can make a reasonable policy produce overshoot, instability, or no visible short-term benefit.
* Model credibility begins with a clear question and bounded claim; complexity is not a substitute for validation.
* Competing hypotheses should be preserved when evidence cannot discriminate among them.

### The guide's worked illustration

The coalition responds to missed trips by adding short-term contractor capacity. This reduces backlog temporarily but diverts funding from maintenance and training, increases coordination load, and raises turnover. A reinforcing erosion loop eventually reduces effective capacity. The model shows why repeated emergency contracting can worsen long-run service despite improving the first month's throughput.

<details><summary>Why are backlogs stocks?</summary>

They accumulate the difference between arrivals and completions over time.

</details>

<details><summary>What is policy resistance?</summary>

Feedback and adaptation reduce, reverse, or delay the intended effect of an intervention.

</details>

<details><summary>Why test extreme conditions?</summary>

They reveal structural and equation errors that may be hidden near nominal conditions.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a breakpoint?</summary>

A condition where capability changes discontinuously or becomes unacceptable rather than gradually degrading.

</details>

<details><summary>Why retain competing hypotheses?</summary>

Because available evidence may not uniquely identify the true mechanism, and different structures imply different interventions.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Create reference modes for accessible-trip completion, backlog, workforce, trust, maintenance, and cost.
2. Develop two competing causal hypotheses for one persistent problem.
3. Convert the selected hypothesis into stocks, flows, auxiliaries, units, and equations.
4. Run structure, units, extreme-condition, time-step, and parameter sensitivity tests.
5. Compare simulated and observed patterns and record what the model cannot explain.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Enterprise Dynamics and Current-State Assessment**

Required contents:

* Reference modes and evidence table.
* Causal-loop and executable stock-flow/process model.
* Verification, behavior, sensitivity, and uncertainty results.
* Current-state strengths, weaknesses, stabilizers, reinforcing problems, and evidence gaps.
* Review package with findings and revisions.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Uses a causal-loop diagram without behavior-over-time evidence.
* Presents a model run with no units, verification, or alternative hypotheses.
* Claims prediction where the model supports only explanation or scenario comparison.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the review only when the model reproduces at least two important reference modes, survives verification tests, and the assessment clearly separates supported findings, plausible hypotheses, and unknowns.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
