## Explain the concept

Type C structural complexity invites system-dynamics thinking about accumulations, delays, feedback and policy resistance. Causal-loop diagrams can reveal plausible feedback; stock-and-flow models require dimensional consistency and bounded numeric assumptions.

Define key stocks such as trained drivers, unresolved cases or public trust proxies and flows that change them. Check whether the model reproduces relevant behavior-over-time patterns before making predictions. Explore policy resistance when a local fix shifts burdens to another part of the system.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A feedback diagram is not proof of causal strength, nor is a stock interchangeable with its flow.

## Work through an independent case

Hiring pressure increases training throughput, but trainers are diverted from operations and retirements outpace graduates. A simplified stock equation is `qualified_next = qualified + completions - departures`. Stress delays and attrition rather than extrapolating a hiring announcement into immediate capacity.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Stocks create memory and inertia; flows change stocks; delays separate action from observed effect.
* A causal-loop diagram is a hypothesis, not a validated model.
* Models should be built for a decision and tested against multiple behaviors and extreme conditions.
* Policy resistance often arises when feedback compensates for intervention.
* Structural uncertainty can dominate parameter uncertainty; alternative models should be preserved.
* System dynamics can obscure individual experience, power, meaning, and heterogeneity through aggregation.

### The guide's worked illustration

A model links service reliability, public trust, ridership, revenue, maintenance capacity, workforce stress, and failure frequency. A subsidy improves service temporarily, but delayed maintenance and workforce attrition later reverse the gain. A second structure attributes the reversal mainly to cyber-related trust loss; both require discriminating evidence.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

System feedback offsets, redirects, or reverses an intervention.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Select one reference mode and define model purpose, boundary, horizon, and audience.
2. Build a causal structure with at least two competing explanations.
3. Translate the preferred hypothesis into an executable stock-flow model with units.
4. Run structural, dimensional, extreme-condition, behavior, and sensitivity tests.
5. Compare at least three policies and document decision reversals across structural alternatives.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

**Type C Executable Structural Model and Policy Analysis**

Required contents:

* model purpose, boundary, horizon, reference modes, and hypotheses
* causal and stock-flow structure
* equations, units, data, initialization, and source files
* verification and behavior-validation evidence
* sensitivity, structural uncertainty, and decision-reversal analysis

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Uses a causal-loop diagram as proof.
* Calibrates to one behavior without structural or extreme-condition testing.
* Presents an aggregated policy gain while hiding subgroup harm.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Approve the model only for its stated use after reproduction, tests, uncertainty analysis, and a clear account of what it cannot represent.
