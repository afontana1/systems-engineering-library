# EN.645.753 · Week 11 — Transition architecture, experiments, adoption, evaluation, and sustainment

**Phase 5 companion · Enterprise Systems Engineering**  
**Central idea:** Experiments, adoption and transition states  
**Original guide:** [EN.645.753, Week 11](../../../C/P5/EN645753.md)

> **Learning outcome:** Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

## Explain the concept

Enterprise transformation should proceed through bounded, measurable changes. A transition architecture records interim capabilities and dependencies; a pilot tests both mechanism and organizational adoption. Process, policy, staffing and infrastructure may need to change together.

Define a predeclared outcome metric, guardrail, comparison design, ethical participation process, stop rule, and interpretation of adverse findings. After deployment, review whether a result persists and whether benefits are being shifted to an excluded group.

**A boundary worth remembering:** An encouraging pilot average is not strong causal evidence without credible comparisons and confounder checks.

## Work through an independent case

Pilot a shared accessible-transfer workflow in two districts with matched comparison areas. Track median transfer completion, missed appointments, staff workload and rider concerns. If speed improves but wheelchair abandonment reports rise, suspend expansion and investigate the mechanism.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Transition architectures are real operating states with their own risks, controls, service levels, and authority—not arrows between current and target diagrams.
* Pilots should answer decision questions; activity completion is not evidence of value.
* Adoption depends on workflow fit, incentives, competence, trust, workload, leadership, and local adaptation.
* Benefit realization often occurs after delivery and therefore needs owners, measures, baselines, and continuation decisions.
* Sustainment includes governance, funding, workforce, data quality, model maintenance, audits, and retirement of obsolete processes or systems.

### The guide's worked illustration

The preferred federated model begins with two operators and one hospital. The transition architecture includes parallel eligibility verification, a limited data trust, manual fallback, temporary funding, training, and an independent accessibility monitor. The pilot has explicit hypotheses, subgroup measures, stop conditions, and a decision to scale, adapt, or terminate. This is more credible than a region-wide launch milestone.

## Practice: retrieve before revealing answers

These five checks and answer explanations come from the supplied course guide. Try writing your answer before expanding the explanation in the website. In Markdown, expand the details tags or view the raw content.

<details><summary>What is an irreversible decision?</summary>

A commitment that is costly or impossible to undo, such as long-term lock-in, asset disposal, legal restructuring, or workforce displacement.

</details>

<details><summary>What makes an experiment decision-relevant?</summary>

Its result changes a specific scale, adapt, pause, stop, or redesign decision.

</details>

<details><summary>Why are guardrails needed?</summary>

To prevent learning activities from causing unacceptable safety, equity, privacy, workforce, financial, or service harm.

</details>

<details><summary>What is benefit realization?</summary>

The governed process of determining whether intended enterprise outcomes occur after changes are delivered.

</details>

<details><summary>Why treat sustainment as design?</summary>

Capabilities and governance decay without ongoing funding, skills, data quality, maintenance, monitoring, and adaptation.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Decompose the preferred alternative into transition architectures and irreversible decisions.
2. Define dependencies, owners, funding, workforce, contracts, policy, data, and continuity controls.
3. Select two high-uncertainty assumptions and design bounded experiments.
4. Define baselines, comparison logic, measures, guardrails, stop conditions, and evidence thresholds.
5. Create governance, adoption, benefit-realization, configuration, audit, and sustainment plans.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Enterprise Transformation Readiness Package**

Required contents:

* Transition architectures and five-year roadmap.
* Dependency, authority, funding, workforce, procurement, data, policy, and continuity plan.
* At least two transformation experiments with hypotheses and decision rules.
* Adoption, communications, learning, configuration, and sustainment plan.
* Evaluation/benefit-realization system and review dispositions.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Uses target-state completion as proof of outcome realization.
* Runs a pilot with no comparison, guardrails, stop conditions, or decision use.
* Ignores transition workload, service continuity, workforce, contract, or data-migration risk.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the review only when every major transition step has an owner, authority, resources, evidence gate, continuity plan, and condition for adaptation or reversal.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
