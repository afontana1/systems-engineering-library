## Explain the concept

Type B process complexity emphasizes how services actually unfold from the customer or citizen viewpoint. The Vanguard Method foregrounds studying demand, failure demand and the system conditions shaping service rather than imposing arbitrary activity targets.

Map cases end to end, listen to people doing the work, identify unnecessary handoffs, and test whether measures drive gaming. An imposed target such as calls handled per hour can make the visible desk look efficient while increasing repeat calls and unresolved needs.

**A boundary worth remembering:** Faster handling of an activity is not the same as improvement in service outcomes.

## Work through an independent case

A help desk reduces call duration by 20% but riders make more repeat calls. Measure the proportion of demand caused by earlier failure, first-contact resolution and elapsed time until transport is secured. Redesign the process around purpose, not call count.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Process maps built from procedures often differ from work as experienced by users and staff.
* Failure demand is demand caused by a failure to do something or do it right for the user.
* Targets can become de facto purposes and induce gaming, batching, or avoidance.
* Check studies purpose, demand, flow, capability, and system conditions before change.
* Plan redesigns work against demand; do tests assumptions rather than imposing a full rollout.
* Type B can underplay structural dynamics, plural values, coercion, and broader political economy.

### The guide's worked illustration

A regional emergency-access hotline reports 90% call closure within 24 hours. Event-log and interview evidence shows that cases are closed when transferred, causing repeated calls and delayed transport. The target improves departmental statistics while increasing failure demand and public frustration.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Select one end-to-end service process and define purpose from multiple user perspectives.
2. Sample and classify demand, including failure demand and exceptional cases.
3. Map actual flow, waits, handoffs, rework, rules, measures, and system conditions.
4. Identify management thinking that creates observed behavior.
5. Design one small process experiment with capability and user-outcome measures.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.
