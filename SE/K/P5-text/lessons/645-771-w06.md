## Explain the concept

Fusing reports is fundamentally an evidence problem. Ask whether observations refer to the same event, whether their errors are independent, whether the clocks agree and whether source reliability changes by context. Preserve original sources and transformation steps so an analyst can unwind a derived estimate.

When the same upstream sensor is republished by several systems, naive averaging gives it disproportionate weight. Confidence scores require calibration; absence of a report is not evidence that no event occurred. Favor a decision record that exposes conflict over one that hides it in a single consolidated number.

**A boundary worth remembering:** Never assume correlated feeds provide independent verification.

## Work through an independent case

Three feeds report 22, 25 and 24 passengers, but feeds 2 and 3 both copy the same vehicle counter. The independent evidence is at most two sources, not three. Reconcile their observation times, retain the unmodified values, and mark the outcome uncertain rather than averaging to 23.7 passengers.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

The following notes and example are **adapted from your supplied original Week 6 guide**, rather than presented as new external research. Return to that guide for the full reading instructions and assessments.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Fusion is a process tied to a decision; there is no universally best fused truth.
* Source dependence must be modeled. Two reports derived from the same upstream feed are not independent corroboration.
* Confidence should be decomposable into source quality, recency, method, consistency, and contextual validity.
* Human judgment can add context but also bias, delay, and nonrepeatability; preserve interventions and rationale.
* Abstention and escalation are valid outputs when evidence is inadequate.

### The guide's worked illustration

Traffic sensors, crowdsourced maps, police reports, and bus telemetry disagree about whether a flooded underpass is passable. The fusion design first associates reports to the same segment and time, records shared weather-source dependence, excludes a stale bus report, and returns “uncertain—do not route medically critical transport” rather than a forced binary answer.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

**Active reading task:** Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

## Learning outcomes from the original guide

1. Define a decision-specific fusion problem and distinguish association, estimation, classification, situation, and impact questions.
2. Develop a fusion architecture preserving source provenance and confidence.
3. Assess correlation, common-mode error, bias, deception, missingness, and latency.
4. Compare rule-based, probabilistic, and human-in-the-loop fusion approaches.
5. Validate fused outputs against decision needs and failure consequences.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

* Define the route-safety fusion decision and loss consequences.
* Create a source/error/provenance matrix.
* Implement a transparent rule-based baseline.
* Add confidence and abstention behavior.
* Test correlated errors, stale data, malicious input, and missing high-quality sources.

**Checkpoint:** compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Information Fusion Baseline v0.6**: decision/loss statement, source and dependence model, provenance graph, fusion architecture, baseline algorithm, confidence/abstention logic, validation results, and prohibited uses.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Assuming source independence without evidence.
* Producing a single fused value with no provenance or uncertainty.
* No abstention/escalation for safety-critical ambiguity.
* Validating against agreement with inputs instead of decision-relevant truth/evidence.
