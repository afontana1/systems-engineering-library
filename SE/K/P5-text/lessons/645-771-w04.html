EN.645.771 · Week 04 — Physical architecture, constituent allocations, interfaces, COTS, and legacy

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Physical allocation and interface contracts
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 4

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

The physical architecture maps logical capabilities to existing constituents, suppliers, COTS products and legacy infrastructure. Allocation should include version, owner, capacity, latency, safety boundary, upgrade path and what happens if a contributor withdraws. A protocol can be syntactically compatible while the underlying data semantics differ.

An interface agreement should define payloads, units, allowed states, error handling, timing, security, ownership and change notification. For legacy/COTS elements, verify claimed behavior at the actual deployed version rather than trusting a brochure. Where authority is limited, build adapters and graceful degradation around stable contracts.

A boundary worth remembering: A message arriving with a valid schema does not imply operational interoperability.

Work through an independent case

Carrier A reports wheelchair positions available now; carrier B reports daily maximum equipped seats. Both fields are called accessible_capacity, but cannot be aggregated. Define a shared contract containing available_positions, freshness, configuration and confidence. Require an adapter to mark B's unknown availability rather than fabricate it.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Allocation is a decision with evidence, authority, capacity, and lifecycle consequences—not a line on a diagram.
  • Physical alternatives should vary performers, centralization, mediation, fallback, and governance, not just product brands.
  • COTS shifts engineering effort from design control to selection, insight, interface control, qualification, update management, data rights, and exit strategy.
  • Legacy constraints may be stable interfaces, undocumented behavior, safety certification, scarce expertise, or unsupported hardware.
  • Compatibility windows and graceful degradation are part of architecture when synchronized upgrades are impossible.

The guide's worked illustration

Alternative A routes all emergency mobility coordination through a regional broker; Alternative B uses federated local brokers with a minimal regional directory; Alternative C retains bilateral agreements and adds only an event-specific coordination cell. The centralized option improves visibility but creates authority, availability, privacy, and scaling risks. The federated option requires stronger semantic contracts and distributed testing.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Allocate every logical activity in one mission thread to candidate performers.
  • Develop three physical variants including a low-integration alternative.
  • Create interface contracts with owner, version, service level, failure behavior, and change process.
  • Assess two COTS services and one legacy constituent using lifecycle criteria.
  • Run a change-impact scenario for an incompatible vendor update.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

Physical Architecture and Constituent Contract Baseline v0.4: allocations, three alternatives, interface contracts, COTS/vendor/legacy assessment, compatibility roadmap, trade analysis, and change-impact result.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Allocating a responsibility to a constituent without capacity, agreement, or authority.
  • No low-integration or fallback alternative.
  • Ignoring vendor update, data rights, obsolescence, or exit risk.
  • Interfaces described only by protocol or connector.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Pass the Logical and Physical Architecture Review with credible allocations, at least three true alternatives, and no unmanaged mission-critical interface or commercial dependency.
