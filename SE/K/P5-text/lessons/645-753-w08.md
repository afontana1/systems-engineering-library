## Explain the concept

Roadmaps need synchronized changes in technology, data, standards, supplier commitments, workforce and policies. A new system can be technically ready while staff lack the skills or authority to use it. Capture decision dependencies and risks at each transition.

Separate unavoidable technical debt from deliberately retained interfaces. A procurement promise is not evidence that partners will upgrade. Use phased architecture states with explicit migration criteria, fallback options, training readiness and retirement rules.

**A boundary worth remembering:** Technical readiness levels alone do not measure enterprise transition readiness.

## Work through an independent case

The coalition can publish a new accessibility data standard in six months, but the largest carrier replaces its dispatch software only every three years. Plan an adapter and an agreed deprecation window. Without supplier and workforce milestones, the target-state diagram is not an actionable roadmap.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* A technology roadmap without capability timing and transition constraints is a forecast, not a plan.
* Standards can enable interoperability while also delaying innovation or creating migration debt.
* Data capability includes meaning, quality, rights, stewardship, access, retention, privacy, and the workforce to use it.
* Supplier concentration and knowledge concentration can create enterprise single points of failure.
* Roadmaps need decision points, alternatives, leading indicators, and retirement criteria.

### The guide's worked illustration

The coalition plans a regional digital twin in Year 2, but the data-rights roadmap shows vendor contracts cannot be renegotiated until Year 3, the workforce roadmap lacks model-validation competence, and the standards roadmap depends on interfaces not adopted by two operators. The strategy is resequenced to establish governance, data semantics, skills, and contract options before investing in large-scale twin infrastructure.

<details><summary>What is capability pull?</summary>

Enterprise outcomes and capability needs drive the search for enabling technologies and standards.

</details>

<details><summary>What is technology push?</summary>

Available or emerging technology motivates possible enterprise applications, whether or not current capabilities require it.

</details>

<details><summary>Why can standards create risk?</summary>

They can be immature, inconsistently adopted, constrain alternatives, or require costly migration.

</details>

<details><summary>What is knowledge concentration risk?</summary>

Critical enterprise capability depends on a small number of people or suppliers whose loss cannot be rapidly replaced.

</details>

<details><summary>Why integrate roadmaps?</summary>

An enabling asset has little value if dependent policy, workforce, data, funding, or operational capability is not ready.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Identify critical enabling technologies, standards, datasets, suppliers, roles, skills, facilities, and institutional knowledge.
2. Score current/target maturity and evidence confidence.
3. Map dependencies and timing to target capabilities and portfolio items.
4. Develop at least two migration paths for a contested standard or vendor dependency.
5. Run a roadmap alignment review under funding, supplier, and workforce scenarios.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Integrated Enterprise Roadmap Baseline**

Required contents:

* Technology, standards, data, supplier, workforce, facility, and knowledge roadmaps.
* Maturity/readiness definitions and evidence.
* Cross-roadmap dependency and timing analysis.
* Migration, exception, lock-in, and retirement strategies.
* Architecture/Portfolio Review package and dispositions.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Uses vendor marketing or calendar dates as readiness evidence.
* Plans a data capability without rights, semantics, stewardship, privacy, or workforce treatment.
* Assumes one standard or supplier path with no migration or exit strategy.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the review only when target capabilities and selected investments have feasible enabling roadmaps, named owners, evidence gates, and credible alternatives for major dependencies.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
