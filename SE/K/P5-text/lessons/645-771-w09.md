## Explain the concept

Cascading risk propagates along dependencies when one failure changes loads, information, or behavior elsewhere. Common-cause risk arises when supposedly redundant paths share a vulnerable dependency such as power, identity service or vendor. Resilience requires more than prevention: detect, absorb, adapt, recover and learn.

Evaluate alternative mission paths under loss scenarios and quantify which outcomes remain possible. Security, safety and availability interact: a cyber lockdown might protect confidentiality while removing the dispatch information needed for emergency transport. Plan risk acceptance and restoration authority before an event.

**A boundary worth remembering:** Assuming two independently procured systems fail independently can greatly overstate reliability.

## Work through an independent case

The bus dispatch and hospital portal run on separate servers but share a regional identity provider. An identity outage disables both. ### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Risk registers organized by constituent hide propagation. Model causal chains, shared dependencies, barriers, recovery resources, and mission effects.
* Common cause can be technical, supplier, infrastructure, identity, policy, workforce, information, or environmental.
* Resilience includes anticipate, withstand, recover, adapt, and learn; each needs measurable outcomes.
* Graceful degradation must define preserved services, priority rules, communication, human authority, and unacceptable states.
* Residual risk ownership must match actual authority and consequence—not whoever writes the report.

### The guide's worked illustration

A ransomware event disables the shared identity/payment provider. Bus operations continue, but riders cannot validate eligibility, paratransit dispatch cannot confirm profiles, and campus gates deny vehicles. The architecture shifts to signed offline entitlements, local allow-lists, time-bounded manual override, later reconciliation, fraud monitoring, and an emergency privacy rule.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

These five checks and answer explanations come from the supplied course guide. Try writing your answer before expanding the explanation in the website. In Markdown, expand the details tags or view the raw content.

<details><summary>What is cascading risk?</summary>

Risk whose effects propagate through dependencies and interactions to create additional failures or mission consequences.

</details>

<details><summary>How does resilience differ from reliability?</summary>

Reliability emphasizes continued correct performance; resilience also addresses disruption, degradation, recovery, adaptation, and learning.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a common-cause failure?</summary>

Multiple failures produced by a shared cause that defeats assumed independence or redundancy.

</details>

<details><summary>Why assess risk transfer?</summary>

A mitigation may reduce one owner’s risk while increasing harm, burden, or exposure elsewhere.

</details>

<details><summary>What makes a degraded mode credible?</summary>

Defined trigger, preserved outcome, authority, resources, human role, evidence, communication, and exit criteria.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

* Build a dependency-based risk graph for utility, telecom, identity, and traffic services.
* Identify common causes and minimum cut sets under stated assumptions.
* Map cyber-resilience objectives and techniques to the physical/information architecture.
* Define degraded modes and restoration priorities for one mission thread.
* Conduct a tabletop exercise with conflicting safety, privacy, and continuity objectives.

**Checkpoint:** compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.
