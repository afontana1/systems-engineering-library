## Explain the concept

Scenario planning explores how a strategy behaves under uncertain drivers rather than predicting one future. Stress both performance and institutional viability under shocks: budget reduction, labor shortage, new accessibility obligations, cybersecurity incidents or extreme weather.

Resilience is a capacity to maintain essential outcomes through disturbance and recover or adapt afterward. Metrics should include minimum service, time to restore, retained equity, and the cost of adaptation. Ask which early signals would trigger a different investment path.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A scenario is not a probabilistic forecast unless its probabilities have been justified.

## Work through an independent case

Under a 25% grant reduction, the expansion portfolio fails but a shared reservation interface with neighborhood dispatch fallback preserves essential hospital transfers. Under rapid population growth the same interface becomes a constraint. Build triggers for both futures rather than declare a single optimal portfolio.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Scenarios are coherent alternative contexts, not point forecasts with different percentages.
* Predetermined elements differ from uncertainties; both should shape assumptions and monitoring.
* Enterprise resilience includes anticipation, absorption, adaptation, recovery, and learning across organizations.
* Risk and opportunity should be linked to objectives and transformation choices, not maintained as detached registers.
* Signposts and triggers make contingent strategy operational.

### The guide's worked illustration

Four scenarios combine stable versus reduced federal funding with cooperative versus restrictive data policy. The preferred technology-heavy portfolio performs well only with stable funding and permissive data sharing. A governance/data-rights foundation plus modular pilots yields lower peak benefit but remains feasible in all four scenarios and preserves an option to scale.

<details><summary>What is a strategic scenario?</summary>

A coherent description of a plausible external context used to test decisions and assumptions.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a predetermined element?</summary>

A future condition that is relatively certain within the decision horizon, even if its consequences are uncertain.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a no-regret action?</summary>

An action that creates value or preserves capability across a wide range of plausible futures.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a signpost?</summary>

Observable evidence indicating that one scenario or critical assumption is becoming more likely.

</details>

<details><summary>Why include opportunity?</summary>

Enterprise uncertainty can enable beneficial options and innovation, not only threaten objectives.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. List external drivers and classify control, influence, uncertainty, and time horizon.
2. Select two high-impact uncertainties and construct four scenario logics.
3. Describe consequences for actors, value, demand, funding, workforce, suppliers, policy, and capability.
4. Stress-test current portfolio and roadmaps; identify breakpoints and decision reversals.
5. Define no-regret actions, options, signposts, triggers, and contingency owners.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Strategic Scenario and Resilience Package**

Required contents:

* External-driver register with evidence and ownership.
* At least four coherent scenarios and narratives.
* Stress-test results for architecture, portfolio, roadmaps, governance, and measures.
* Risk, opportunity, resilience, and breakpoint analysis.
* No-regret actions, contingent options, signposts, triggers, and owners.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Uses only best/base/worst numerical scaling.
* Treats external factors as risks without changing enterprise assumptions or decisions.
* Claims resilience without degraded modes, recovery, adaptation, or monitoring.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Revise until at least one major current decision changes under scenario evidence and the recommended posture includes operational triggers rather than generic flexibility language.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
