## Explain the concept

Type E people complexity arises when actors interpret the problem differently, with legitimate but incompatible worldviews. Soft Systems Methodology treats models as devices for structured learning about purposeful human activity rather than as literal descriptions of one objectively agreed system. Strategic Assumption Surfacing and Testing makes conflicting assumptions and stakes discussable; interactive planning explores desired futures with participants.

Use rich pictures, stakeholder interviews, root definitions and conceptual activity models, then compare these with the real situation. Do not average away disagreement. Record where participants cannot safely speak or where participation itself is contested.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A facilitated workshop does not automatically equalize power among participants.

## Work through an independent case

An operator defines success as schedule adherence; a disability advocate defines it as a guaranteed accessible journey. Develop two purposeful activity models and identify which policy decisions differ. Facilitate comparison without pretending one model is the single truth.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Pluralism means actors can engage and negotiate despite different values; it does not imply easy agreement.
* SAST examines assumptions supporting competing policies and their importance and certainty.
* Interactive planning asks what would be designed now if unconstrained by the existing system, then works toward feasible movement.
* SSM models purposeful activity systems based on declared worldviews, not the real world itself.
* Accommodation is a change actors can live with, even without shared beliefs.
* Facilitators influence agenda, language, participation, and interpretation; neutrality should not be assumed.

### The guide's worked illustration

Transit managers frame emergency access as throughput, hospitals as clinical continuity, disabled travelers as autonomy and dignity, labor as safe sustainable work, and municipalities as fiscal legitimacy. An SSM inquiry reveals that a single “mobility optimization” model cannot represent these purposes. The group identifies an accommodation around minimum protected access and transparent burden-sharing.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

**Active reading task:** Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Create a rich picture including structures, processes, conflicts, emotions, exclusions, and environmental pressures.
2. Develop four root definitions and CATWOE analyses from distinct worldviews.
3. Build purposeful activity models and compare them with the current situation.
4. Run a SAST matrix for three intervention strategies.
5. Develop an idealized design and identify near-term changes that increase choice without pretending full consensus.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Type E Pluralist Problem-Structuring Package**

Required contents:

* rich picture and participation record
* four root definitions and CATWOE analyses
* purposeful activity models and real-world comparison
* SAST assumptions and strategy implications
* idealized design and feasible movement options
* accommodations, unresolved conflicts, and ownership
* facilitation reflection and method critique

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, approach used, assumptions, boundary judgments, evidence, affected groups, limitations, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Converts different worldviews into a single averaged objective.
* Treats CATWOE or a rich picture as a checklist.
* Claims consensus where actors merely complied or lacked power to object.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Proceed only when plural perspectives materially alter the problem framing and options, and unresolved disagreement is preserved rather than averaged away.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
