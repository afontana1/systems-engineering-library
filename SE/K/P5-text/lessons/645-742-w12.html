EN.645.742 · Week 12 — Critical Systems Practice: Check; final defense and the way ahead

Phase 5 companion · Management of Complex Systems
Central idea: Critical Systems Practice: Check and defend
Original guide: EN.645.742, Week 12

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

Checking is more than reporting KPI improvement. Examine outcomes, distribution, power shifts, unintended effects, fidelity to the original purpose and whether the method itself distorted what was seen. Distinguish observations from causal interpretation and keep the possibility that actors adapt after measurement.

A final defense should compare at least two interventions and explain why the method mix, evidence, boundaries and governance are appropriate. Identify what would falsify the favored explanation, how conclusions remain revisable, and who can halt harmful continuation.

A boundary worth remembering: A successful intervention is not simply one that confirms the sponsoring organization's theory.

Work through an independent case

The pilot reduces average booking time but raises no-show rates among travelers requiring assistance. Compare case mix, communication changes and access barriers. Revise the intervention rather than accept a positive headline metric. Report dissenting accounts with their evidentiary status.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Check asks whether improvement occurred, how it was judged, and who might disagree.
  • Evaluation should examine mechanisms and unintended consequences, not only target metrics.
  • Method evaluation asks which approaches helped, misled, conflicted, or required adaptation.
  • A live change tests whether the repository connects boundaries, assumptions, models, actors, interventions, and decisions.
  • A mature conclusion narrows unsupported claims rather than defending the original plan.
  • The way ahead is an adaptive learning agenda, not a declaration that complexity has been solved.

The guide's worked illustration

During final review, a private platform withdraws data access. The technical model loses demand visibility, the process pilot loses automated triage, the VSM intelligence function degrades, public trust risks increase, and participation concerns change. The learner narrows the pilot, activates offline processes, revises evidence thresholds, and pauses one automated feature.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

Active reading task: Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  1. Run a clean reproduction and complete a source/citation audit.
  2. Evaluate outcomes, mechanisms, distributional effects, participation, and unintended consequences.
  3. Complete a method-performance retrospective for Types A–F and EPIC.
  4. Execute a randomly selected live change and update connected artifacts and the decision.
  5. Conduct the final oral defense and write the next-learning agenda.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

Final Complex Systems Management Release

Required contents:

  • all capstone outputs in Section 16
  • clean reproduction and citation audit
  • evaluation of outcomes, mechanisms, harms, and participation
  • Type A–F and EPIC method-performance retrospective
  • live-change analysis and revised decision
  • executive and technical briefings
  • oral-defense record, findings/dispositions, and adaptive next-learning agenda

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, approach used, assumptions, boundary judgments, evidence, affected groups, limitations, and changes made to prior baselines.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Cannot reproduce or source a decision-critical claim.
  • Hides harm, coercion, failed assumptions, or negative evidence to preserve the recommendation.
  • Treats the final review as closure rather than a decision about next learning and action.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Complete the course only after clean reproduction, citation verification, critical-finding disposition, successful live change, and an oral defense that states both recommendations and non-claims.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
