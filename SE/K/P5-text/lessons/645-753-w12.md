# EN.645.753 · Week 12 — Final Enterprise Transformation Review and live enterprise change

**Phase 5 companion · Enterprise Systems Engineering**  
**Central idea:** Enterprise review and live transformation change  
**Original guide:** [EN.645.753, Week 12](../../../C/P5/EN645753.md)

> **Learning outcome:** Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

## Explain the concept

At the final review, present a defensible enterprise purpose, baseline, stakeholder and power map, dynamic mechanism, linked architecture, feasible portfolio, transition route and assurance plan. A live change scenario tests whether the strategy can adapt without denying new evidence.

Make values and governance explicit. State who is authorized to change funding, who can appeal, which groups were not represented, and which conclusions depend on disputed assumptions. The strongest defense is not certainty but a traceable basis for revisable decisions.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A transformation cannot be called successful solely because projects were delivered on time.

## Work through an independent case

Mid-review, the largest regional employer changes shift schedules and the state signals a new grant condition. Update demand assumptions, service equity evaluation and portfolio sequencing. Record what stays valid and which stakeholders require renewed consultation before authorization.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

### Instructor notes from the guide

* A final release is a controlled evidence chain, not a folder of disconnected weekly submissions.
* Executive communication should state the decision, value, authority, cost, timing, risks, conflicts, and evidence without overstating certainty.
* Technical review should expose model scope, uncertainty, correspondence, reproduction, and unresolved findings.
* Live-change analysis tests whether architecture and evidence are connected enough to support real revision.
* The complexity handoff should identify adaptive and emergent mechanisms that enterprise planning could not resolve.

### The guide's worked illustration

During the review, a major transit authority withdraws from pooled funding while remaining willing to share limited data. The learner must revise portfolio affordability, governance, capability ownership, value distribution, emergency mutual aid, measures, scenarios, transition experiments, and roadmap timing. A defensible response may preserve the strategy through a smaller coalition or may reverse the preferred target model.

Reproduce the example in a small table, model, or notebook before applying the method to the running case. Record where the example is intentionally simplified.

**Active reading task:** Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Run a clean checkout and reproduce the declared key outputs.
2. Complete cross-artifact consistency queries and close or accept findings.
3. Prepare executive, technical, and stakeholder-specific views.
4. Execute one randomly selected live change and update the decision record.
5. Conduct an oral defense and produce the EN.645.742 hypothesis/uncertainty handoff.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Final Enterprise Transformation Release**

Required contents:

* All capstone outputs listed in Section 16.
* Clean-reproduction log and automated/structured consistency checks.
* Executive and technical briefing materials.
* Live-change impact analysis and revised decision record.
* Oral-defense record, findings/dispositions, retrospective, and EN.645.742 handoff.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Cannot reproduce a decision-critical result.
* Hides an unresolved stakeholder harm, authority gap, or transformation infeasibility.
* Treats the live change as a narrative addendum without updating connected artifacts and decisions.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Complete the course only after clean reproduction, closure or explicit acceptance of critical findings, successful live change, and an oral defense that accurately states authority and evidence limits.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
