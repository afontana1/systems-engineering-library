**Phase 5 companion · Management of Complex Systems**

**Original guide:** [EN.645.742, Week 10](../../../C/P5/EN645742.md)

> **Learning outcome:** Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

## Explain the concept

In the Explore phase, frame the situation through multiple sources of evidence and identify complexity types without committing to a predetermined intervention. Produce means designing a coherent mixture of methods, participants, decision boundaries, outputs and ethical protections.

Create a method protocol specifying who will participate, what will be measured, which conflicts cannot be resolved in a single metric, and how unexpected findings alter the plan. Document power and consent issues before collecting information.

**A boundary worth remembering:** An elaborate methodology does not guarantee an honest starting boundary.

## Work through an independent case

The team proposes value-stream observation, actor mapping, system dynamics, and an 'is/ought' stakeholder dialogue. Assign each a distinct question. The diagnostic package should not assume the service redesign approved last month is still the preferred solution.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

The following notes and example are **adapted from your supplied original Week 10 guide**, rather than presented as new external research. Return to that guide for the full reading instructions and assessments.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Explore uses multiple systemic perspectives to prevent one framing from dominating.
* Primary issues receive direct intervention; secondary issues shape feasibility, ethics, and learning.
* Produce chooses methodologies, models, and methods based on issue structure and stakeholder conditions.
* Methods can be sequenced, nested, used in parallel, or triangulated—but handoffs must be designed.
* An intervention strategy includes participation, governance, data, evidence, facilitation, resources, safety, and adaptation.
* EPIC is iterative; new learning can require returning to Explore or Produce.

### The guide's worked illustration

The strategy begins with CSH to reopen boundaries and participation, uses SSM to structure different emergency-access purposes, applies system dynamics to test long-term workforce/trust mechanisms, uses VSM to redesign coalition coordination, and applies optimization only to a bounded staging decision. Each handoff has named products, owners, and limitations.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

**Active reading task:** Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

## Learning outcomes from the original guide

By the end of the week, you should be able to:

* apply the Explore stage to multiple systemic perspectives and primary/secondary issues
* select methods using explicit problem-method reasoning
* construct a Produce-stage intervention architecture with sequencing and handoffs
* define participation, governance, evidence, ethics, and adaptation arrangements
* plan resources, schedule, review, and configuration without pretending the intervention is linear

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Use multiple systemic perspectives to identify primary and secondary issues.
2. Construct a problem-method fit matrix using the Type A–F critiques.
3. Define method sequence, handoffs, iteration triggers, and conflict management.
4. Design participation, authority, data, facilitation, review, and ethics arrangements.
5. Build an intervention backlog, schedule, resource estimate, and configuration plan.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**EPIC Explore and Produce Intervention Strategy**

Required contents:

* multiperspectival Explore record
* primary and secondary issue map
* problem-method fit matrix
* method sequence, handoffs, iterations, and conflict handling
* participation, authority, ethics, and facilitation plan
* data, model, evidence, and review plan
* schedule, resources, governance, risk, and configuration
* rejected strategies and decision rationale

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, approach used, assumptions, boundary judgments, evidence, affected groups, limitations, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Selects methods because they are familiar or already available.
* Treats EPIC as a linear project lifecycle.
* Omits participation, authority, safety, or method-conflict planning.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the Intervention Strategy Review only when method selection, handoffs, participation, ethics, evidence, and adaptation are reviewable and justified.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
