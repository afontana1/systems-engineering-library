# EN.645.753 · Week 07 — Portfolio, investment, affordability, and resource allocation

**Central idea:** Portfolio choices under constrained resources  
**Original guide:** [EN.645.753, Week 7](../../../C/P5/EN645753.md)

## Explain the concept

Portfolio engineering allocates finite budgets, staff, calendar time and political attention across mutually constraining interventions. Some choices are prerequisites, some substitutes, and some produce benefits only as bundles. Treat affordability as both initial investment and recurring operating exposure.

Decision analysis should distinguish mandatory guardrails from discretionary outcomes. Evaluate scenarios and uncertainty, and identify options with staged learning. Avoid maximizing an overall return number before checking distributional consequences, implementation capacity and legal constraints.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A portfolio with the highest expected score may be infeasible because its dependencies cannot be delivered.

## Work through an independent case

An accessible stop-upgrade project costs $420k; a real-time interface program costs $250k; the first yields limited benefit until carrier agreements are signed. Under a $500k capital budget, choose a feasible sequence and explain whether a smaller pilot buys useful information before irreversible spending.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Portfolio items include disinvestment, retirement, policy, workforce, process, data, standards, and governance changes—not only acquisition programs.
* Feasibility depends on shared resources, dependencies, timing, organizational change capacity, and minimum viable capability combinations.
* Benefit estimates should state recipients, timing, evidence, confidence, and conditions for realization.
* Sunk costs are not benefits; existing commitments may still create legal, technical, or transition constraints.
* Stage gates should buy information and preserve options, not merely authorize continued spending.

### The guide's worked illustration

Three organizations propose separate dispatch modernization projects. Independent scoring ranks all highly. Portfolio analysis shows they compete for the same integration team, duplicate identity and mapping services, and cannot realize benefits until a shared governance and data-semantics initiative is complete. The preferred portfolio funds governance/data work first, pilots one interoperable service, pauses two acquisitions, and reserves an option to scale after evidence is available.

## Learning outcomes from the original guide

1. Define portfolio items, decision horizon, constraints, selection criteria, dependencies, and governance.
2. Estimate lifecycle cost, resource demand, timing, uncertainty, opportunity cost, and benefit realization.
3. Construct feasible portfolio alternatives rather than ranking items independently.
4. Analyze affordability, crowd-out, sunk-cost bias, duplication, and stop/continue/reshape decisions.
5. Recommend a portfolio with stage gates, evidence conditions, and contingency options.

## Practice: retrieve before revealing answers

These five checks and answer explanations come from the supplied course guide. Try writing your answer before expanding the explanation in the website. In Markdown, expand the details tags or view the raw content.

<details><summary>Why is portfolio selection not project ranking?</summary>

The value and feasibility of one item depend on shared constraints, interactions, dependencies, timing, and the rest of the portfolio.

</details>

<details><summary>What is opportunity cost?</summary>

The value of the best feasible alternative forgone by committing resources to the selected option.

</details>

<details><summary>Why include disinvestment?</summary>

Retiring or stopping low-value commitments can release resources and reduce complexity.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a stage gate for?</summary>

To make an evidence-based decision about continuing, changing, scaling, pausing, or stopping an investment.

</details>

<details><summary>Why model change capacity?</summary>

Organizations have limited attention, leadership, training, and implementation bandwidth even when money exists.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Create a portfolio-item register including investments, disinvestments, and enabling changes.
2. Define costs, resources, benefits, dependencies, timing, uncertainty, and evidence gates.
3. Construct at least four feasible portfolio combinations under budget and workforce constraints.
4. Run sensitivity and scenario analysis for cost growth, participation, benefit delay, and funding cuts.
5. Write stop/continue/reshape criteria for the five largest commitments.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Enterprise Portfolio and Investment Decision**

Required contents:

* Portfolio-item register and capability trace.
* Lifecycle cost/resource/benefit/uncertainty model.
* At least four feasible portfolio alternatives, including a low-investment path.
* Sensitivity, affordability, opportunity-cost, and decision-reversal analysis.
* Recommendation with stage gates and stop/continue/reshape conditions.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Ranks projects independently and labels the result a portfolio.
* Counts benefits without recipients, timing, evidence, or realization conditions.
* Recommends a portfolio that exceeds critical workforce, budget, or dependency constraints.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Revise until every selected item is affordable, dependency-feasible, traced to capability/value, and governed by evidence-based continuation criteria.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
