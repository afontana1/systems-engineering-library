EN.645.771 · Week 10 — Distributed integration, verification, validation, and operational T&E

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Distributed assurance and operational evaluation
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 10

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

SoS-level testing cannot always reproduce field scale or control every constituent. Build assurance from interface tests, constituent evidence, integrated scenario exercises, live operational observations and explicit residual gaps. Configuration and time matter: an old passing test may not support the current constituent combination.

A test campaign should measure mission outcomes under representative stress and degraded conditions, not simply the number of passed API checks. Record test coverage by mission thread, risk, interface version, actor and use environment, plus who accepts the unresolved gaps.

A boundary worth remembering: 100% constituent test pass is neither necessary nor sufficient for an SoS mission assurance claim.

Work through an independent case

Each vehicle passes local trip acceptance tests, but a coalition-wide exercise reveals that both agencies reserve the same hospital slot. Add an inter-agency concurrency test and an ownership rule for slot confirmation. The original local passes remain valid for their limited claims, not for the end-to-end mission claim.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Start from capability claims and failure consequences, then determine evidence—not from available test facilities.
  • Distributed T&E needs a common scenario, configuration manifest, time basis, data dictionary, instrumentation plan, custody, quality checks, and adjudication process.
  • Synthetic and surrogate constituents can reduce cost but create model-validity and interface-fidelity limits.
  • Operational evaluation must include representative users, workload, policy, degraded conditions, recovery, and organizational coordination.
  • Evidence sufficiency is a decision under uncertainty; document what remains untested and why.

The guide's worked illustration

A regional evacuation demonstration succeeds with all systems preconfigured and staffed by engineers. It does not validate operational suitability because normal dispatchers lack the shared tool, one hospital used a test-only interface, privacy approval was temporarily waived, and recovery from a dropped telecom link was not exercised.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Active reading task: Change one major assumption in the original example. Identify exactly which claim, conclusion or decision becomes unsupported and what evidence could restore it.

Learning outcomes from the original guide

  1. Define SoS capability claims and decompose required evidence across constituent, interface, thread, mission, and operational levels.
  2. Develop a distributed integration and T&E architecture with responsibilities, environments, instrumentation, data, and configuration control.
  3. Plan progressive tests from interface conformance through mission-thread and operational evaluation.
  4. Address test limitations, unavailable constituents, synthetic substitutes, safety, cyber, privacy, and operational constraints.
  5. Evaluate evidence sufficiency and residual uncertainty.

A claim-relevant uncertainty not adequately addressed by available analysis, model, test, demonstration, or operational observation.

</details>

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Build a claim-evidence matrix for three mission outcomes.
  • Assign evidence ownership and identify cross-organization gaps.
  • Design integration increments and entry/exit criteria.
  • Define a mission-thread test with normal, degraded, cyber, and recovery conditions.
  • Conduct an evidence-sufficiency review using model, lab, field, and operational results.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

Distributed SoS Integration and T&E Baseline v1.0-TRR: claim-evidence matrix, integration architecture, responsibilities, configurations, environments, scenarios, instrumentation/data plan, model/surrogate use, safety/privacy/cyber controls, criteria, gaps, and readiness recommendation.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Claiming SoS effectiveness from constituent acceptance tests.
  • No common configuration, scenario, or data semantics across test sites.
  • Using a surrogate without fitness-for-use evidence.
  • Ignoring unavailable constituents, operational users, or recovery conditions.
  • Overstating results beyond the tested context.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Pass the M&S, Risk, and T&E Readiness Review with no unsupported critical claim, unowned evidence gap, uncontrolled configuration, or unbounded surrogate/model use.
