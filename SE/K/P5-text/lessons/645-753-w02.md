## Explain the concept

Enterprise governance is the arrangement of decision rights, accountability, incentives, participation and conflict resolution. Formal authority is not the same as legitimacy in the eyes of affected groups. Map who has legal control, who has operational discretion, who pays, who bears consequences and who can veto or withdraw cooperation.

A governance design must support uncomfortable decisions about conflicting objectives. Specify escalation paths, appeals, transparency and independent review. Otherwise the most powerful actor can define every cost as someone else's problem.

**A boundary worth remembering:** A stakeholder consultation meeting is not proof of meaningful participation or accepted governance.

## Work through an independent case

The regional authority funds route planning, but independent operators set driver rosters and disability advocates can identify unsafe curb interfaces. A steering committee can vote to fund a new service, yet cannot credibly declare the curb problem solved without examining rider experience and jurisdictional street authority.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Authority is multidimensional and may be split among funding, policy, operations, data, safety, labor, procurement, and public accountability.
* Influence is not authority; a central network position does not grant the right to direct another organization.
* Governance defines how decisions are proposed, evaluated, authorized, contested, monitored, and revised.
* Legitimacy and consent affect adoption, data access, workforce behavior, and the durability of enterprise change.
* Objective conflict should be modeled, not smoothed into a fictitious shared objective.

### The guide's worked illustration

A regional steering board can approve a shared data standard but cannot compel a private vendor to expose historical data or require a transit authority to reassign union-covered dispatch work. A feasible governance design combines procurement clauses, a data trust, operating agreements, labor consultation, public reporting, and an escalation path rather than asserting coalition command authority.

These five checks and answer explanations come from the supplied course guide. Try writing your answer before expanding the explanation in the website. In Markdown, expand the details tags or view the raw content.

<details><summary>Why is influence not authority?</summary>

Influence can shape decisions but does not necessarily carry a legitimate or enforceable right to decide.

</details>

<details><summary>What is an accountability gap?</summary>

A condition where responsibility for an outcome exists without authority, resources, evidence, or consequences sufficient to deliver it.

</details>

<details><summary>Why model veto and appeal rights?</summary>

Because they determine feasible sequencing, negotiation, and the durability of decisions.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a principal-agent problem?</summary>

An actor makes decisions on another's behalf while incentives, information, or accountability are misaligned.

</details>

<details><summary>What makes governance an architecture concern?</summary>

Decision rights and coordination mechanisms constrain which technical and organizational configurations can operate coherently.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Populate an actor table with objectives, resources, formal authority, informal influence, accountability, and constraints.
2. Create a RACI-like decision-rights matrix but add veto, appeal, funding, data-ownership, and legitimacy fields.
3. Select five conflicts and distinguish negotiable preferences from legal or safety constraints.
4. Develop three governance mechanisms for one cross-enterprise decision.
5. Red-team each mechanism from the perspective of a low-power actor and an actor with blocking authority.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Enterprise Governance Baseline**

Required contents:

* Actor/authority/incentive/accountability registry.
* Objective-conflict and cooperation-dependency matrix.
* Decision-rights model covering at least eight enterprise decisions.
* Three governance mechanism alternatives with feasibility and legitimacy analysis.
* Review briefing, findings, and dispositions.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Assumes coalition authority over independent organizations.
* Treats public legitimacy, workforce consent, or legal accountability as communication issues only.
* Uses RACI alone for decisions involving veto, funding, data ownership, or appeal.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Pass the review only when every critical enterprise decision has a feasible authority path, unresolved conflicts are explicit, and no actor is assigned obligations without resources or consent mechanisms.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
