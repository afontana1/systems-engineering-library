# EN.645.753 · Week 01 — Enterprise definition, boundary, purpose, and receiving review

**Phase 5 companion · Enterprise Systems Engineering**  
**Central idea:** Enterprise boundary and purpose  
**Original guide:** [EN.645.753, Week 1](../../../C/P5/EN645753.md)

> **Learning outcome:** Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

## Explain the concept

An enterprise is a purposeful sociotechnical arrangement spanning organizations, people, policy, resources, markets, technology and external partners. It need not coincide with a legal entity. Define boundaries relative to an outcome and intervention decision, including actors who influence outcomes but are not centrally managed. State time horizon, beneficiaries, excluded dependencies, and how the model may and may not be used.

Enterprise outcomes often involve legitimacy and distribution as well as throughput or cost. If a coalition proposes to improve mobility, a boundary omitting affected riders, employers and land-use policy will yield attractive but incomplete solutions.

**A boundary worth remembering:** An org chart tells who reports to whom; it does not reveal all mechanisms producing public value.

## Work through an independent case

A regional coalition defines success as passenger trips completed. An evening-shift workforce organization points out that service ends before its workers finish. Expand the receiving review from a technology portfolio to shift schedules, funding rules and rider outcomes, while keeping a manageable decision boundary.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

## The source guide's instructional anchors

### Instructor notes from the guide

* An enterprise boundary is decision-relative. It should be broad enough to include mechanisms that materially shape the outcome and narrow enough to support analysis.
* The enterprise is not synonymous with the coalition office. It includes independent organizations and resources whose interactions create the result.
* Purpose, outcomes, value recipients, authority, time horizon, and transformation decision should be declared before architecture work begins.
* Exclusions require interface and assumption treatment; they do not disappear because they are outside the model.
* Receiving reviews distinguish inherited evidence from new enterprise assumptions and identify where SoS evidence is insufficient.

### The guide's worked illustration

The inherited SoS roadmap assumes region-wide sharing of accessible-vehicle availability. The enterprise receiving review reveals that three providers are funded under different statutes, one vendor contract prohibits secondary data use, and a union agreement limits dispatch-role changes. The technical interface is feasible, but the enterprise boundary must include funding authorities, procurement, labor, privacy, and service-accountability actors.

<details><summary>Why is an enterprise boundary decision-relative?</summary>

Because different decisions require different actors, resources, mechanisms, and external dependencies to be inside the analysis.

</details>

<details><summary>What distinguishes an enterprise from an enterprise system?</summary>

The enterprise is the purposeful sociotechnical network; an enterprise system is an engineered system used by or within that enterprise.

</details>

<details><summary>Why record prohibited model uses?</summary>

To prevent architecture and analysis outputs from being used for claims they were not built or validated to support.

</details>

<details><summary>What is a receiving review?</summary>

A controlled assessment of inherited artifacts, assumptions, gaps, applicability, ownership, and needed revision.

</details>

<details><summary>What makes an exclusion acceptable?</summary>

Its effect is represented through an interface, assumption, constraint, scenario, or uncertainty with an owner and evidence plan.

</details>

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Write a one-sentence enterprise purpose and a separate one-sentence transformation decision.
2. Create a context table with actors/resources inside, outside, and at the boundary.
3. For each excluded actor, record the dependency, assumption, owner, and evidence need.
4. Classify inherited artifacts as accepted, conditionally accepted, rejected, or not applicable.
5. Run a boundary challenge: remove one actor or resource and test whether the claimed outcome can still be explained.

Pause after each step and compare the result with the stated decision purpose. Record discrepancies rather than forcing the artifact to match the expected story.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

## Mastery evidence from the original course

**Weekly assessed deliverable:**

**Enterprise Framing Baseline**

Required contents:

* 2–3 page enterprise charter with purpose, decision, outcomes, time horizon, boundary, environment, assumptions, and exclusions.
* Context view and actor/resource registry with ownership and dependency fields.
* Receiving-review matrix for inherited SoS artifacts.
* Model intended-use and prohibited-use statement.
* One-page boundary sensitivity note.

Include a one-page review note stating the decision supported, evidence used, assumptions, limitations, unresolved conflicts, and changes made to prior baselines.

**Critical failure modes to avoid:**

* Defines the enterprise as only the coalition office or technology stack.
* Excludes a mission-critical actor or funding/policy dependency without interface treatment.
* Claims that inherited SoS evidence establishes enterprise value or implementation feasibility.

Any critical failure requires revision regardless of the numerical score.

**Revision and mastery requirement:**

Revise until the boundary challenge identifies no unowned critical dependency and the charter clearly separates the enterprise purpose from the proposed solution.

Minimum weekly score: 80%, with no critical failure and at least Proficient performance on the first two rubric criteria.
