EN.645.771 · Week 12 — Final SoS Engineering Review and live constituent change

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Review under live constituent change
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 12

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

A final SoS review should answer: what changed, which mission claims remain justified, which evidence was invalidated, and what action is authorized now? Changes may occur without permission from the coalition. The review must therefore use a dated configuration snapshot and clearly distinguish active agreements from assumptions.

Present an architecture, mission evidence graph, interoperability gaps, risk scenarios, model limitations, roadmap and decision options. During an adversarial review, introduce an unexpected constituent exit or version upgrade and trace its effect through outcomes and authority.

A boundary worth remembering: A confident slideshow is not a valid substitute for configuration-specific decision evidence.

Work through an independent case

Carrier B announces a new dispatch API tomorrow. The coalition knows the schema but lacks representative stress tests and an updated data-use agreement. Recommend a bounded fallback operating mode rather than treating the change as either harmless or catastrophically unmanageable. List evidence needed to reauthorize broader use.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • The final release requests a decision and defines authority, commitments, evidence, risks, and next action.
  • Cross-artifact consistency matters more than visual polish. Mission threads, interfaces, information, models, tests, and roadmap must agree.
  • Live change tests whether traceability and governance are operational rather than decorative.
  • A mature recommendation states where control is limited, evidence is conditional, participation is uncertain, and enterprise or complexity analysis is required.
  • The Phase 5 handoff should identify value conflicts, incentives, governance gaps, adaptation, feedback, and unintended consequences for later courses.

The guide's worked illustration

At the review, the identity/payment vendor is acquired and announces a six-month interface deprecation plus new restrictions on derived data. The learner identifies affected threads and exchanges, updates the COTS/data-rights risk, revises the federation and T&E plan, activates offline entitlement and federated identity options, changes Wave 2 scope, and escalates enterprise procurement/governance issues to EN.645.753.

Reproduce the reasoning with controlled case data, change one material assumption, and explain how the architecture, evidence, or decision changes.

Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

  • Run a clean repository reproduction and manifest audit.
  • Trace three mission claims across all architecture and evidence views.
  • Rehearse constituent withdrawal, interface deprecation, data-rights loss, cyber event, and accelerated upgrade.
  • Conduct executive, operator, constituent-owner, safety/security, accessibility/privacy, tester, and skeptical-reviewer challenges.
  • Close or explicitly accept all critical findings.

Checkpoint: compare the result with the reference rationale, record discrepancies, correct the source artifact or model, and rerun affected analysis.

Companion extension: Produce a one-page claim–evidence–authority ledger for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.

Mastery evidence from the original course

Weekly assessed deliverable:

Final Regional Mobility and Emergency Access SoS Engineering Release v1.0: complete capstone outputs, controlled sources/models/data, clean reproduction, live-change disposition, final decision request, review record, oral defense, and Phase 5 handoff.

Submit native/textual source, controlled input data, generated views, a change log, and a one-page self-review. Screenshots alone are not acceptable evidence.

Critical failure modes to avoid:

  • Disconnected artifacts or contradictory baselines.
  • Clean reproduction or live change failure without transparent recovery.
  • Unsupported mission, causality, model, test, authority, or risk claim.
  • Recommendation with no implementable commitments, residual risks, or revision conditions.

Revision and mastery requirement:

Complete clean reproduction, pass the live constituent change, defend every critical claim and limitation, close review findings, and achieve Proficient or better on all critical dimensions.
