## Explain the concept

Type D organizational complexity focuses on role structure, coordination, autonomy, environmental sensing and the capacity to maintain identity under change. Sociotechnical approaches consider jointly the technical workflow and the social organization that operates it. Viable System Model ideas offer one diagnostic for relationships among operational units, coordination, control, intelligence and policy; they are not a universal organizational template.

Ask whether local units have enough autonomy to respond and whether enterprise coordination can manage shared dependencies without overloading every decision. Study information asymmetries and unresolved escalation loops.

**A boundary worth remembering:** An organizational chart can satisfy formal reporting needs while leaving the actual coordination problem unresolved.

## Work through an independent case

Regional dispatch is centralized for every small exception. Delays grow and local operators improvise. Propose bounded local authority for reversible changes, with explicit escalation for cross-carrier or safety decisions. Test whether this reduces delay without increasing inequity or fragmentation.

### How to generalize the reasoning

1. **Purpose:** Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
2. **Actors and dependencies:** Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
3. **Mechanism:** Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
4. **Evidence:** Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
5. **Action:** Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

### Instructor notes from the guide

* Sociotechnical design rejects optimizing technology while treating people as adjustable components.
* Joint optimization seeks mutually supportive social and technical arrangements, not a compromise after technical design.
* The VSM distinguishes operations, coordination, control, intelligence, and policy/identity functions.
* Requisite variety means regulatory capacity must be adequate for environmental variety.
* Recursion allows viable units within viable units; it also complicates authority and information design.
* Type D can become functionalist if conflict, domination, and contested purpose are treated as coordination defects.

### The guide's worked illustration

The regional coalition centralizes all routing decisions to improve coordination. Local operators lose the ability to respond to street-level conditions, while the center becomes overloaded and slow. A VSM diagnosis suggests preserving local operational autonomy, strengthening coordination protocols, and improving strategic intelligence rather than centralizing every decision.

Reconstruct the example in a diagram, table, memo, or model. Then state where the example is deliberately simplified and why the same conclusion might not transfer to the regional case.

## Deliberate practice: build and critique an artifact

The original guide recommends the following weekly exercises:

1. Define the work system and identify social and technical subsystems.
2. Evaluate joint optimization across tasks, roles, skills, technology, measures, and environment.
3. Map recursive levels and VSM functions for the coalition.
4. Diagnose variety mismatch, channel overload, missing feedback, and inappropriate centralization.
5. Propose organizational design changes and test them against conflict and legitimacy concerns.

Pause after each step. Compare the result with the decision purpose and record disagreement, ambiguity, and negative evidence instead of forcing convergence.

**Companion extension:** Produce a one-page *claim–evidence–authority ledger* for the worked case. Include one supported claim, one disputed or untested claim, a dated configuration, an owner, a measure with units, a plausible failure path, an affected stakeholder who may disagree, and an explicit authorization or learning decision. Perform a red-team review: remove one critical source or change one actor's incentive and update the decision.
