Phase 5 — Six interconnected concept pathways

Each thread links SoS engineering, enterprise outcomes and complex-systems judgment. Start at a mission claim and move forward; reverse the chain to study change impact.

1. Technical coalition to legitimate capability

Constituent independence (771/W1) → governance agreements (771/W2) → mission thread (771/W3) → interoperability (771/W5) → enterprise authority and legitimacy (753/W2) → critical boundary review (742/W8).

2. Evidence to credible action

Data provenance (771/W6) → Bayesian uncertainty (771/W7) → model federation (771/W8) → distributed assurance (771/W10) → enterprise outcome metrics (753/W3) → methodological critique (742/W9).

3. Failures to resilient operations

Cascading dependencies (771/W9) → alternate mission paths (771/W11) → external strategic scenarios (753/W9) → enterprise feedback (753/W5) → dynamic risk and safe-to-learn interventions (742/W11).

4. Investment to enterprise transformation

5. From hard optimization to plural learning

Meridian Regional Access & Resilience Coalition — Phase 5 integrated case

Synthetic teaching case. None of the facts, prices, organizations, probabilities or regulatory claims below describe an actual regional system.

Mission

During routine travel and regional disruptions, the Meridian coalition aims to sustain reliable and accessible travel to critical services. Five participating constituents independently operate city buses, campus shuttles, paratransit, hospital admission coordination and emergency communications. Each has its own decision rights, workforce constraints and upgrade cycle.

SoS framing

A regional coordinator is responsible for the coalition-level emergency transport outcome but does not manage constituent operating budgets. Coalition capability requires accessible vehicle assignment, current routing conditions, hospital intake availability and an authorized handoff record. A shared identity provider is a common-cause risk; an alternate radio path has not been independently qualified.

The initial information exchange uses a misleading common field, available_capacity, whose semantics differ between agencies. Some feeds are snapshots of current seats; others give registered maximum capacity. Require state, time, version, owner, eligibility and intended use before aggregation.

Enterprise framing

The coalition office is not the whole enterprise. Funding agencies, unions, riders, road authorities, employers, suppliers and healthcare organizations influence outcomes. Current aggregate on-time rate is illustrative: conventional journeys 855/900, accessible journeys 50/100, total 90.5%. The gap cannot be concealed behind aggregate improvement.

A hypothetical first-stage program has a $600k capital ceiling: common data standard $150k, carrier adapter $200k (requires the standard), stop upgrades $350k, central dispatch replacement $700k. These alternatives are not automatically commensurate; accessibility obligations, operating spend, technical risk and adoption rights impose constraints beyond simple value scores.

Complex problem situation

An operator says the problem is slow dispatch; riders say it is unreliable end-to-end access; workforce representatives say understaffing and training losses drive degradation. Treat these as distinct claims with different evidence and authority. A system-dynamics hypothesis connects staffing, reliability, trust and revenue; it does not yet establish causal strength. Some participants lack safe representation in formal governance.

Integrated final assignment

  1. Draw the constituent and governance architecture, with typed interfaces, owner/version/freshness and at least one alternate path.
  2. Construct one mission logical graph and a minimal cut set analysis; challenge the assumed independence of alternatives.
  3. Calculate an example posterior from a noisy route obstruction alert; explain calibration and correlated evidence limits.
  4. Build an enterprise outcome hierarchy, disaggregating at least two populations. Identify one burden transfer and the actor accountable for it.
  5. Compare two dependent investment portfolios under budget and workforce constraints, including a reversible pilot.
  6. Produce two incompatible but intelligible problem definitions using Soft Systems approaches and perform an 'is/ought' boundary critique.
  7. Design a bounded intervention with primary outcome, distributional guardrails, evidence plan, affected-party review and stop authority.
  8. Defend a revised decision after a constituent API change, new grant requirement and new report of disproportionate harm.

Evidence discipline

Label every statement as observation, assumption, model inference or decision. Retain configuration and source. Never treat calculator outputs, guessed numbers, simulation demonstrations or a stakeholder workshop as certified safety, legal, causal or operational evidence.

EN.645.771 · Week 01 — SoS definition, attributes, behavior, and receiving review

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Constituent independence and emergence
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 1

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

A system of systems is not distinguished simply by its size or number of subsystems. Its constituents can serve useful purposes independently and normally retain some management autonomy. Thus the SoS architect must negotiate interfaces and commitments rather than assume that a central design authority can freely change every component. Operational independence asks whether a constituent still does useful work outside the coalition; managerial independence asks whether another decision maker controls its priorities, budget, and lifecycle. Independence and interdependence coexist.

Emergent properties arise from interactions among constituents and their environment. They can be desirable (coordinated evacuation capacity) or harmful (congestion caused by synchronized rerouting). Treat network centrality as a clue, not proof that a node is mission-critical: substitute routes, payload quality, and temporal deadlines matter.

A boundary worth remembering: A tightly centrally managed fleet with several subsystems may be a large system, not a genuine SoS. Define independence using evidence, not branding.

Work through an independent case

Northstar's city bus, campus shuttle, hospital intake, paratransit dispatch and emergency radio networks all have independent missions. A proposed shared dashboard has five connections, yet a rural bridge may be the true limiting dependency for a hospital transfer. The SoS boundary must include bridge availability as an interface and risk even if the region cannot manage bridge maintenance.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • A constituent is not simply a box in a decomposition; it has its own purpose, users, management, lifecycle, and ability to operate outside the SoS mission.
  • SoS classifications describe governance conditions, not maturity levels. A coalition may move among them by mission or period.
  • Emergent behavior can be beneficial, harmful, or ambiguous and may arise from interactions, timing, adaptation, policy, and human response.
  • Boundaries are decision-dependent. Include what must be considered to answer the decision, not everything that exists.
  • Network measures are prompts for investigation. They do not substitute for mission semantics, capacity, quality, authority, or failure behavior.

The guide's worked illustration

A municipal rail operator, two campus shuttle services, a paratransit provider, and EMS dispatch can each deliver useful services independently. During a hospital evacuation, however, coordinated routing, accessible capacity, traffic priority, and hospital intake status create a capability no constituent can deliver alone. The coalition is initially acknowledged: a regional coordinator has mission responsibility but limited authority over constituent budgets and designs.

What two independence conditions are most important?

Operational usefulness outside the SoS and managerial control over its own lifecycle and decisions.

Does every distributed system qualify as an SoS?

No. Distribution alone does not establish constituent independence or emergent cross-system capability.

Why can SoS type change?

Governance, authority, participation, mission, and lifecycle conditions can differ by scenario and time.

What is the purpose of the boundary?

To identify the entities and relationships required to answer the stated decision credibly.

What does betweenness centrality fail to show?

Mission semantics, quality, capacity, substitutability, authority, and actual failure consequence.

EN.645.771 · Week 02 — Managing SoS development, governance, acquisition, fielding, and evolution

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Authority-constrained evolution
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 2

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

An SoS roadmap operates across different acquisition schedules and business priorities. A constituent owner may reject a technically sensible interface change because its own service obligations are more urgent. Governance mechanisms—memoranda of agreement, shared service-level targets, change boards and escalation paths—make cooperation feasible without pretending authority is unlimited.

Separate decision rights for mission objectives, interoperability contracts, local operations, emergency exceptions, and funding. Record each actor's incentive to participate, the minimum viable agreement, and the exit conditions. A delivery promise unsupported by constituent commitment is a planning assumption, not a baseline.

A boundary worth remembering: Directed, acknowledged, collaborative and virtual SoS are governance archetypes, not maturity rankings.

Work through an independent case

A regional coordinator requests real-time accessible capacity from two carriers. Carrier A will offer data every minute; carrier B permits 15-minute summaries. Instead of assuming identical telemetry, the first release provides a conservative dispatch rule that flags carrier B information as stale for acute emergencies.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Authority must be modeled per decision: architecture standards, interface changes, data sharing, test access, operations, funding, and emergency control may have different owners.
  • Governance defines how decisions are made and enforced; collaboration alone is not a control mechanism.

The governance design therefore creates an interface council, emergency authority protocol, data-use agreements, test memorandum, and compatibility window rather than a fictitious single configuration board.

EN.645.771 · Week 03 — Mission outcomes, capability dependencies, and logical SoS architecture

Phase 5 companion · System of Systems Engineering
Central idea: Mission thread and dependency graph
Original guide: EN.645.771, Week 3

Learning outcome: Be able to explain the week's mechanism, apply it to a defensible case, identify the boundary of a method and present evidence for the associated engineering decision. This lesson is original companion instruction; source-guide notes are explicitly labeled below.

Explain the concept

Begin with an operational outcome—such as transferring vulnerable patients to a safe destination—rather than a list of installed technologies. Trace the mission thread through detection, dispatch, routing, handoff and confirmation. Each link needs a responsible actor, precondition, interface, deadline, evidence and fallback. The logical architecture describes the capabilities and information relationships required, without prematurely selecting a vendor or physical implementation.

A capability dependency graph is not the same as a product decomposition. It captures which functions must succeed together, which may substitute, and where resources or governance constrain coordination. AND dependencies, OR alternatives and time windows should be explicit.

A boundary worth remembering: Do not equate counts of satisfied requirements with probability of mission success.

Work through an independent case

A successful transfer requires an accessible vehicle AND a staffed receiving facility AND current route authorization. Radio contact may be provided by city network OR hospital backup. Draw the AND/OR graph, then block the hospital node: a working vehicle alone cannot fulfill the outcome.

How to generalize the reasoning

  1. Purpose: Write the actual mission, enterprise or people-centered outcome. Avoid replacing an outcome with a proposed tool.
  2. Actors and dependencies: Identify who owns the relevant systems, information, resources, decisions and consequences. Separate authority from influence.
  3. Mechanism: Name the claimed relationship: interface, dependency, feedback, incentive, perception, constraint or testable causal mechanism.
  4. Evidence: Define an observable measure, data provenance, time window, alternative explanation and relevant configuration.
  5. Action: Compare at least two interventions and state the preconditions, uncertainty, legitimate decision owner, stop rule and learning plan.

Instructor notes from the guide

  • Mission threads are scenario-specific sequences of activities and decisions required to achieve an outcome.
  • Logical architecture asks what roles, functions, information, and decisions are needed before naming current systems.
  • Capability dependencies may be conjunctive, alternative, conditional, capacity-limited, or time-dependent.
  • A cut set identifies a structural vulnerability only under stated assumptions about success and substitution.
  • Measures belong at outcome, mission-thread, interface, and constituent levels and must not be conflated.

What distinguishes a mission outcome from an MOP?

The outcome expresses mission success; an MOP describes a system or process performance contributing to it.

Why create solution-independent threads?

To expose required behavior and enable real alternatives rather than ratifying the current implementation.

What is a conditional dependency?

A capability or task is required only under a stated scenario, state, or prior result.

What does a cut set mean?

A set of losses that disconnects success under the model assumptions; it is not automatically a real-world failure proof.

Why include degraded threads?

SoS value often depends on reconfiguration and continuity when constituents or information are unavailable.

Explain the concept

A Bayesian network is a probability model of conditional dependencies, not automatically a causal model. To update beliefs, define mutually exclusive hypotheses, priors and observation likelihoods; then calculate posterior probabilities. The direction of arrows and conditioning assumptions should be tested against a causal story if intervention decisions are required.

Prior sensitivity matters when evidence is scarce. A positive alarm can still imply a low event probability if false positives are common and the event base rate is low. For causal claims, examine confounders, selection effects and interventions rather than interpreting any conditional probability as a lever.

A boundary worth remembering: Posterior probability depends on the prior and test conditions; a Bayesian graph alone does not prove causality.

Work through an independent case

Explain the concept

Enterprise architecture should support decisions through multiple linked views: outcomes, capabilities, business activities, actors, information, systems, standards, resources and transitions. Capabilities represent what an enterprise can reliably do, not its current department names. Architectures should expose options and dependencies instead of becoming static inventories.

For each capability define a level of performance, scenario, dependency, owning or participating actors, observable evidence, and improvement pathway. Maintain traceability from investment to expected outcome and the assumptions connecting them.

A boundary worth remembering: Replacing an application can change the technology view without improving enterprise capability.

The capability requires trained dispatchers, authority to prioritize vehicles, correct curb data, agreements with carriers and tests under emergency scenarios. One platform may help, but does not deliver the capability alone.

Explain the concept

Portfolio engineering allocates finite budgets, staff, calendar time and political attention across mutually constraining interventions. Some choices are prerequisites, some substitutes, and some produce benefits only as bundles. Treat affordability as both initial investment and recurring operating exposure.

Decision analysis should distinguish mandatory guardrails from discretionary outcomes. Evaluate scenarios and uncertainty, and identify options with staged learning. Portfolio feasibility, option value and transition architecture

An enterprise portfolio is not a bag of independent projects. Some interventions must precede others; some compete for scarce specialists; some produce value only when combined. A feasible portfolio meets budgets, legal guardrails, resource constraints and prerequisite dependencies over time. Its value includes the ability to learn before making irreversible commitments.

A small example

The region can fund at most $600k in a first stage. Options are a data standard ($150k), a carrier adapter ($200k, requires the standard), accessible stop upgrades ($350k), and a central dispatch replacement ($700k). The central replacement is infeasible in stage one. A standard plus adapter costs $350k, leaving $250k; stop upgrades alone cost $350k. Comparing their expected outcomes is valid only after including dependencies and distributional guardrails.

Staging as a real option

A small reversible pilot may reduce uncertainty about adoption, reliability and benefit. Its value is not just the direct pilot output; it can prevent a costly enterprise-wide mistake or justify acceleration. Treat information value separately from implementation value. Do not award a pilot fictional perfect information; measure what it can actually reveal.

Transition architecture

Describe current, interim and target states, including people, process, policy, interfaces, data, skills and legacy coexistence. Every stage needs entry evidence, service continuity conditions, an exit or rollback trigger and accountable owners. A time-based roadmap without acceptance criteria is a calendar, not an engineering strategy.

Soft systems and critical boundary critique

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.742 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Soft Systems Methodology (SSM) treats conceptual models as learning devices for situations in which purposes are contested. Rather than assuming one objective, it explores purposeful activity systems from different viewpoints. A root definition clarifies who is served, what transformation is imagined, who could control it, and which worldview makes it meaningful. Comparing these models with observations helps identify feasible, desirable change.

A paired example

From an operator's worldview, the system transforms incoming requests into efficient scheduled trips. From a disability advocate's worldview, it transforms a mobility need into dignified, reliable access to participation. Both may be reasonable, but they imply different activities and performance measures. SSM helps make those differences explicit without pretending a meeting will settle them automatically.

Boundary critique

Critical Systems Heuristics asks who is and who ought to be beneficiary, decision maker, expert and witness. It exposes how a boundary grants or removes visibility. The ability to provide data is not the same as the power to define what counts as valid evidence. Record excluded actors and potential coercion; use protected channels where participation could invite retaliation.

Epistemic care

A survey can describe respondents, not necessarily all affected people. A consensus workshop can produce agreement among participants while masking absent groups. A technical model can evaluate a chosen metric yet cannot justify why that metric should outrank other purposes. Each method has both power and limits.

Exercise. Write two opposing root definitions for a proposed centralized dispatch service. Then write 'is' and 'ought' statements about who defines success. Identify one decision that cannot be settled by a travel-time model.

Enterprise governance, legitimacy and contested decision rights

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.753 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

A governance table identifies who recommends, who controls resources, who authorizes operations, who bears risk and who is entitled to challenge a decision. Legitimacy is broader than formal power. An agency can be authorized to spend public money without having adequate evidence that affected users accept the resulting service design. Conversely, a stakeholder may suffer consequences yet lack a formal vote.

Power map as a decision artifact

Build one row for each actor: objective, resources, formal authority, informal influence, information held, veto or exit ability, consequences, representation and redress. Use the map to test a concrete decision, not to label groups permanently. Authority can change with emergency declarations, contracts and jurisdiction.

A flawed consultation

A regional coalition schedules a 2 p.m. online public consultation about new accessible transit. People working shifts and riders without reliable internet are absent. The meeting produces unanimous support. The correct inference is that attendees supported the idea; not that all affected people did. Improve accessible participation, record dissent and document the limits of representation. It may also be necessary to modify decision rights, not merely survey practices.

Three governance checks

  1. Transparency: can an affected person trace how the choice was made and which evidence was used?
  2. Accountability: is there an actor who can alter the decision or act on harmful results?
  3. Contestability: are there safe avenues to present counterevidence and obtain redress?

Cascading failures, cut sets and resilient mission paths

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.771 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

A mission capability is vulnerable when dependencies create paths for failures to propagate. Network drawings can help, but connectivity alone does not encode operational meaning, time, capacity or failure probability. A cut set is a set of components whose loss defeats a defined mission claim. A minimal cut set has no proper subset that also defeats it.

Logical structure

Let mission success require (bus OR paratransit) AND hospital AND (city_network OR backup_radio). Failure occurs if both transport alternatives fail, if hospital intake fails, or if both communication alternatives fail. The minimal failure sets are {bus, paratransit}, {hospital}, and {city_network, backup_radio}. Listing minimal sets suggests where redundancy could help—but shared dependencies may defeat apparent independence.

A common-cause trap

Suppose the city network and backup radio both require one regional identity service to authenticate dispatch staff. The identity service is itself a single-point cut set despite two communications channels. A typical architecture diagram that omits authentication misses this. One response is a preauthorized local break-glass mode with audit and limited privileges; another is genuine independence of the backup path. Neither is cost-free or universally safe.

Resilience sequence

Specify mission-essential function, detection threshold, absorption margin, graceful degradation, recovery time objective, restoration authority and subsequent learning. Verify these as scenarios, not as generic labels. A rapid recovery may restore technical availability while violating accessibility or information-integrity requirements.

Exercise. Add a shared power supply to the two transport coordination paths. Update the minimal cut sets, then explain what evidence would prove that the proposed backup is actually independent.

Enterprise outcomes, incentives and distribution of value

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.753 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Enterprise success is neither the count of delivered projects nor a single average performance metric. Separate four levels: resources invested, activities performed, outputs produced, and outcomes experienced by real beneficiaries. A new dispatch platform is an output; a reliable accessible ride to treatment is an outcome. Attribution between the two requires evidence.

Aggregate metrics can hide exclusion

Suppose 900 conventional riders have an on-time rate of 95% and 100 riders needing accessible service have a rate of 50%. Overall on-time performance is (900×.95+100×.50)/1000=.905, or 90.5%. Reporting 90.5% alone hides that half of the smaller group misses the standard. The enterprise should inspect disaggregated outcomes and consider legal, ethical and service obligations before trading one subgroup's performance for another's.

Incentives create feedback

Paying operators per completed trip may increase reported trips, encourage short routes or avoidance of complicated assisted journeys. Rewarding average handling time can turn unresolved needs into repeat calls. Design measures against purpose and observe unwanted adaptive responses. Explicitly test whether a metric can be improved without improving the intended outcome; if yes, it needs guardrails.

Practical outcome architecture

For each mission objective record beneficiary, desired change, metric definition, denominator, observation window, disaggregation, comparator, data quality, attribution claim, guardrails and decision owner. Capture burdens such as additional staff effort or privacy loss even when they are outside the primary budget line.

Exercise. An intervention raises overall throughput by 15% while lowering on-time accessible transport. Write a decision memo explaining whether the program should scale, pause or redesign and what evidence remains missing.

Diagnosing complexity without calling every problem chaotic

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.742 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Complexity has several dimensions: technical interconnection, process variation, dynamic feedback, organizational coordination, plural interpretations and power conflict. A complex problem situation can mix all of them. Categorizing its components does not eliminate interactions; the point is to choose methods with appropriate assumptions and limits.

Complicated versus complex

Repairing a known mechanical assembly may be complicated but tractable through decomposition, specifications and expert analysis. A regional transport redesign changes rider habits, employer scheduling, operator incentives and public trust while being implemented; its problem boundaries and behaviors may evolve. Some parts still benefit from hard optimization. What fails is claiming the optimization settles the whole situation.

Nonlinearity is not chaos

A queue can have a sharp increase in waiting as utilization approaches capacity. For the ideal M/M/1 queue, W=1/(μ−λ) if arrival rate λ is below service rate μ. With capacity 10/hour and demand 9/hour, average time is 1 hour; at demand 9.8/hour, it is 5 hours. This sharp response is nonlinear but does not establish deterministic chaos. The M/M/1 assumptions may not hold for real service.

Diagnostic protocol

Collect evidence for: changing actor strategies, disputed values, feedback, delays, power imbalance, nonlinear thresholds, known versus unknown mechanisms, and observational limits. State which elements are readily modeled and which demand participatory or critical inquiry. Keep a boundary critique alongside every technical model.

Practice. Classify a route optimization problem, a cross-agency funding dispute and the interaction between public trust and ridership. Assign one method and one important blind spot to each.

Enterprise feedback, stocks, flows and policy resistance

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.753 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Organizations develop inertia through accumulations: trained staff, service backlog, deferred maintenance, public trust proxies and contracted capacity. Stocks change through flows. A simple workforce model is Staff(t+1)=Staff(t)+Trained(t)-Departures(t). Hiring authorizations are not trained staff; a training delay makes the difference matter.

Worked example

At the start of a quarter, the coalition has 120 qualified operators. Fifteen graduate from training and 22 depart, so it ends with 113. If leaders celebrate 15 hires while ignoring departures, they misread capability. If more operators must be reassigned as trainers, the short-term flow of available service may decline before the training investment pays off.

Closed-loop explanation

Poor reliability reduces rider trust; reduced ridership may lower fare revenue; reduced revenue may lead to service cuts; service cuts further lower reliability. This is a reinforcing loop that can drive undesirable decline. A separate balancing loop—public funding triggered by unmet service thresholds—may counteract it with an administrative delay. Loops are causal hypotheses requiring investigation; their qualitative signs do not reveal numerical strength.

Modeling discipline

Define stocks with units, flows in units/time, realistic bounds, initial conditions, delays and measured series. Perform dimensional checks and stress extremes. Use simulations to compare interventions robustly across uncertain assumptions rather than to predict one exact trajectory. Pay attention to induced behavior: a new performance target can change how data are recorded.

Exercise. Start with 120 staff and simulate four quarters with completion rates [15,18,23,25] and departure rates [22,21,19,18]. Identify the first quarter in which capacity stabilizes, and propose what external validation is needed before using the results for staffing decisions.

Bayesian updating, conditional dependence and information fusion

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.771 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Bayesian reasoning combines prior belief and observed evidence. For a binary event H with alert A, Bayes' rule is P(H|A)=P(A|H)P(H) / [P(A|H)P(H)+P(A|¬H)P(¬H)]. It forces three questions into the open: how common is the event, how sensitive is the observation, and how often does it trigger without the event?

Numerical example

An obstruction occurs on 5% of route checks. The detector alarms with probability 0.9 when obstructed and 0.15 when clear. Then the posterior obstruction probability following an alarm is 0.045/(0.045+0.1425)=0.24. The alarm increases concern from 5% to 24%, but a positive alarm is still more likely to be false than true. This is not a verdict about an individual route without checking calibration and sampling.

Why multiple feeds may mislead

If independent evidence A and B is conditionally independent given H and ¬H, likelihood ratios can be multiplied: posterior_odds = prior_odds × LR(A) × LR(B). But if both dashboards copy one upstream sensor, the evidence is not independent. Multiplying both likelihood ratios counts the same underlying observation twice. Record source lineage and retain disagreeing observations.

A Bayesian network encodes conditional dependencies. Whether an arrow supports an intervention requires separate causal assumptions. A network fitted to historical correlations might reflect hidden common causes or changed reporting patterns rather than stable causal mechanisms.

Exercise. Raise the event prior to 20% while keeping the detector properties unchanged. Compute the new posterior. Explain why a model deployed after a major storm needs recalibration even when its software remains unchanged.

Systems of systems: autonomy, governance and emergence

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.771 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

A large integrated system and a system of systems can look similar in a component diagram. What separates them is not necessarily the number of components, but the independent purposes and decision rights of the participating systems. A city bus operator can run routes without a regional emergency-transfer coalition; its managers can also decide to replace a dispatcher independently. The regional capability must survive or adapt to those choices.

A diagnostic rather than a slogan

Ask of every claimed constituent: What useful independent service does it provide? Who funds and governs it? Who can change its software, hardware, staff rules and service contract? Who can reject an SoS request? What is the notice period? A coalition may be directed for one mission yet collaborative for another. Governance is context-specific.

Why emergence complicates guarantees

Imagine a hospital evacuation mission requiring an accessible vehicle, an available route and a receiving bed. Every constituent can pass its own availability test while the mission fails because all vehicles converge on one hospital at once. The failure arises through coordination timing, not necessarily a broken device.

A simple series approximation, P(mission)=P(vehicle)×P(route)×P(bed), gives 0.95×0.90×0.90≈0.77 only if the events are independent and appropriately defined. If weather simultaneously affects routes and vehicle availability, multiplication overstates success. Use an explicit dependence model or explore bounded conditional cases.

How to build a useful model

  1. Define the mission outcome and time window.
  2. Identify independent constituents and the decision owners.
  3. Draw logical AND/OR dependencies with timing, alternatives and fallback.
  4. Mark shared dependencies and autonomy constraints.
  5. Stress-test one constituent's unannounced change, including who could authorize a response.

Safe-to-learn interventions and credible evaluation

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.742 · Phase 5

In a complex adaptive setting, an intervention can change behavior, incentives and the meaning of measurement. A pilot should therefore be designed as bounded learning, not as a miniature rollout whose success is predetermined. Ethical limits may prevent randomizing exposure or testing certain harms, even on a small scale.

Predeclare decision rules

Define the intended outcome, unit of analysis, observation period, participant protections, guardrails, approval authority, incident escalation and stop/rollback conditions. Anticipate displacement: a new routing rule may help the pilot district by pushing congestion onto neighboring districts. Track both direct benefits and transferred burdens.

Worked evaluation

A new call-handling protocol reduces mean booking time from 18 to 13 minutes among observed pilot cases. That does not prove a five-minute causal effect. Perhaps simpler requests were overrepresented, queue conditions changed, or riders who could not get through were excluded. Compare case mix and unaffected locations if feasible, inspect distribution rather than only mean, and ask directly about access barriers. An ethical review may require stopping even when the top-line metric improves.

Safety and resilience

Use a conservative fallback, clear operator training, continuous incident reporting and independent monitoring for interventions with serious consequences. 'Safe to learn' does not mean risk-free; it means consequence limits and consent are explicit, mitigations are credible and the accountable authority can halt the experiment.

A passing API test proves that a test client can exchange a particular message under particular conditions. It does not show that actors understand the message the same way or may lawfully act on it. Engineering interoperability means defining exchange semantics under an operational purpose.

Consider a field named available. For a city bus fleet it may mean buses not in maintenance; for paratransit it may mean buses with certified accessible equipment and free operators. Numeric equality does not imply capability equivalence. A defensible contract specifies units, reference frames, service scope, observation time, update interval, confidence, source, authority to use the data, and a treatment for unknown values.

Worked freshness calculation

One dispatch feed reports a vehicle at 12:00 and a decision occurs at 12:07. If the allowed age for time-critical assignment is 180 seconds, the age is 7×60=420 s and the record must be rejected for that purpose. It might still be usable for hourly planning. Intended use determines acceptable age.

A four-layer verification strategy

  • Wire: can the two endpoints authenticate and exchange the stated schema?
  • Meaning: do fields describe equivalent quantities and state transitions?
  • Workflow: do ordering, retries, timeouts, authorization and exception handling work?
  • Mission: can the participants use this information to produce a safe outcome in stressed scenarios?

Test disagreement and stale data deliberately. A good interface agreement includes a data owner, change-notice process, conformance fixtures, incident escalation and a degraded mode. These remain necessary when software adapters are used.

Critical Systems Practice: selecting and combining methods

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.742 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Critical Systems Practice (CSP) treats method selection as a reasoned design decision. A method's assumptions about purpose, power, evidence and prediction must fit the situation. A diverse toolkit can improve understanding, but mixing methods without a rationale produces incoherent artifacts.

Explore → Produce → Intervene → Check

  • Explore: map the situation, conflicting purposes, evidence limits, complexity and power conditions.
  • Produce: choose a justified sequence of methods and participants; define outputs, decision criteria and ethical safeguards.
  • Intervene: take a bounded action, observe adaptation and manage risk.
  • Check: examine outcomes and unintended consequences, critique the original framing and decide whether to revise, scale or stop.

Worked multimethod design

A coalition faces chronic delays for accessible transport. Use event-level value-stream measurement to locate where delay accumulates; system dynamics to examine service-demand feedback and staff shortages; SSM to understand incompatible meanings of good service; and boundary critique to discover who lacks influence. These methods do not directly validate each other's conclusions. Instead, a decision record states what each contributes and which contradictions remain.

A method-choice rubric

For every method list: question it addresses, required assumptions, data and participation needs, possible harms, likely blind spots, outputs, and how a reviewer might falsify or challenge its conclusions. If a method cannot resolve an essential power issue, supplement it with governance action—not another quantitative model.

Practice. Defend why a Monte Carlo model can aid uncertainty analysis but cannot, by itself, decide whether a proposed exclusion of a rider group is legitimate.
