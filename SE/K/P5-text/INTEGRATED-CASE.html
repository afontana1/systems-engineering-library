Meridian Regional Access & Resilience Coalition — Phase 5 integrated case

Synthetic teaching case. None of the facts, prices, organizations, probabilities or regulatory claims below describe an actual regional system.

Mission

During routine travel and regional disruptions, the Meridian coalition aims to sustain reliable and accessible travel to critical services. Five participating constituents independently operate city buses, campus shuttles, paratransit, hospital admission coordination and emergency communications. Each has its own decision rights, workforce constraints and upgrade cycle.

SoS framing

A regional coordinator is responsible for the coalition-level emergency transport outcome but does not manage constituent operating budgets. Coalition capability requires accessible vehicle assignment, current routing conditions, hospital intake availability and an authorized handoff record. A shared identity provider is a common-cause risk; an alternate radio path has not been independently qualified.

The initial information exchange uses a misleading common field, available_capacity, whose semantics differ between agencies. Some feeds are snapshots of current seats; others give registered maximum capacity. Require state, time, version, owner, eligibility and intended use before aggregation.

Enterprise framing

The coalition office is not the whole enterprise. Funding agencies, unions, riders, road authorities, employers, suppliers and healthcare organizations influence outcomes. Current aggregate on-time rate is illustrative: conventional journeys 855/900, accessible journeys 50/100, total 90.5%. The gap cannot be concealed behind aggregate improvement.

A hypothetical first-stage program has a $600k capital ceiling: common data standard $150k, carrier adapter $200k (requires the standard), stop upgrades $350k, central dispatch replacement $700k. These alternatives are not automatically commensurate; accessibility obligations, operating spend, technical risk and adoption rights impose constraints beyond simple value scores.

Complex problem situation

An operator says the problem is slow dispatch; riders say it is unreliable end-to-end access; workforce representatives say understaffing and training losses drive degradation. Treat these as distinct claims with different evidence and authority. A system-dynamics hypothesis connects staffing, reliability, trust and revenue; it does not yet establish causal strength. Some participants lack safe representation in formal governance.

Integrated final assignment

  1. Draw the constituent and governance architecture, with typed interfaces, owner/version/freshness and at least one alternate path.
  2. Construct one mission logical graph and a minimal cut set analysis; challenge the assumed independence of alternatives.
  3. Calculate an example posterior from a noisy route obstruction alert; explain calibration and correlated evidence limits.
  4. Build an enterprise outcome hierarchy, disaggregating at least two populations. Identify one burden transfer and the actor accountable for it.
  5. Compare two dependent investment portfolios under budget and workforce constraints, including a reversible pilot.
  6. Produce two incompatible but intelligible problem definitions using Soft Systems approaches and perform an 'is/ought' boundary critique.
  7. Design a bounded intervention with primary outcome, distributional guardrails, evidence plan, affected-party review and stop authority.
  8. Defend a revised decision after a constituent API change, new grant requirement and new report of disproportionate harm.

Evidence discipline

Label every statement as observation, assumption, model inference or decision. Retain configuration and source. Never treat calculator outputs, guessed numbers, simulation demonstrations or a stakeholder workshop as certified safety, legal, causal or operational evidence.
