Critical Systems Practice: selecting and combining methods

Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.742 · Phase 5

All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.

Critical Systems Practice (CSP) treats method selection as a reasoned design decision. A method's assumptions about purpose, power, evidence and prediction must fit the situation. A diverse toolkit can improve understanding, but mixing methods without a rationale produces incoherent artifacts.

Explore → Produce → Intervene → Check

  • Explore: map the situation, conflicting purposes, evidence limits, complexity and power conditions.
  • Produce: choose a justified sequence of methods and participants; define outputs, decision criteria and ethical safeguards.
  • Intervene: take a bounded action, observe adaptation and manage risk.
  • Check: examine outcomes and unintended consequences, critique the original framing and decide whether to revise, scale or stop.

Worked multimethod design

A coalition faces chronic delays for accessible transport. Use event-level value-stream measurement to locate where delay accumulates; system dynamics to examine service-demand feedback and staff shortages; SSM to understand incompatible meanings of good service; and boundary critique to discover who lacks influence. These methods do not directly validate each other's conclusions. Instead, a decision record states what each contributes and which contradictions remain.

A method-choice rubric

For every method list: question it addresses, required assumptions, data and participation needs, possible harms, likely blind spots, outputs, and how a reviewer might falsify or challenge its conclusions. If a method cannot resolve an essential power issue, supplement it with governance action—not another quantitative model.

Practice. Defend why a Monte Carlo model can aid uncertainty analysis but cannot, by itself, decide whether a proposed exclusion of a rider group is legitimate.
