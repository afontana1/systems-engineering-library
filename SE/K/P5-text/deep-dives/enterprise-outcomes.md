# Enterprise outcomes, incentives and distribution of value

**Graduate companion concept essay · EN.645.753 · Phase 5**

*All case data are invented teaching examples; models and calculations are not approved operational or safety evidence.*

Enterprise success is neither the count of delivered projects nor a single average performance metric. Separate four levels: **resources** invested, **activities** performed, **outputs** produced, and **outcomes** experienced by real beneficiaries. A new dispatch platform is an output; a reliable accessible ride to treatment is an outcome. Attribution between the two requires evidence.

### Aggregate metrics can hide exclusion

Suppose 900 conventional riders have an on-time rate of 95% and 100 riders needing accessible service have a rate of 50%. Overall on-time performance is `(900×.95+100×.50)/1000=.905`, or 90.5%. Reporting 90.5% alone hides that half of the smaller group misses the standard. The enterprise should inspect disaggregated outcomes and consider legal, ethical and service obligations before trading one subgroup's performance for another's.

### Incentives create feedback

Paying operators per completed trip may increase reported trips, encourage short routes or avoidance of complicated assisted journeys. Rewarding average handling time can turn unresolved needs into repeat calls. Design measures against purpose and observe unwanted adaptive responses. Explicitly test whether a metric can be improved without improving the intended outcome; if yes, it needs guardrails.

### Practical outcome architecture

For each mission objective record beneficiary, desired change, metric definition, denominator, observation window, disaggregation, comparator, data quality, attribution claim, guardrails and decision owner. Capture burdens such as additional staff effort or privacy loss even when they are outside the primary budget line.

**Exercise.** An intervention raises overall throughput by 15% while lowering on-time accessible transport. Write a decision memo explaining whether the program should scale, pause or redesign and what evidence remains missing.
