# Resource shelf — open and authoritative starting points

These are links to *other authors' material*, not copies. Availability, accessibility and licenses can change. Prefer the Atlas article for first-pass intuition, then use these for more detailed reading and confirmation. Formal specifications are reference texts, not beginner tutorials.

## Phase 0: Foundations and management

- **[NASA Systems Engineering Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)** — Reference chapters for lifecycle, needs, requirements, design, technical management, integration and V&V.
- **[Guide to the Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Guide_to_the_Systems_Engineering_Body_of_Knowledge_(SEBoK))** — Breadth-first conceptual encyclopedia and pointers to further references.
- **[GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide](https://www.gao.gov/pdf/product/705312)** — Program cost estimation, uncertainty, assumptions and management.

## Phase 1: MBSE and software-intensive systems

- **[Guide to the Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Guide_to_the_Systems_Engineering_Body_of_Knowledge_(SEBoK))** — Breadth-first conceptual encyclopedia and pointers to further references.
- **[OMG SysML formal specifications](https://www.omg.org/spec/SysML)** — Precise notation and semantics; consult when you need authoritative modeling constructs; not a beginner textbook.
- **[OMG UML formal specification](https://www.omg.org/spec/UML)** — Formal language definition for software structure, interaction and behavior models.
- **[CMU SEI: Early Analysis of Software Architecture](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/early-analysis-of-software-architecture/)** — Detailed quality-attribute and architectural reasoning studies.
- **[NIST SP 800-160 Rev. 1 — Engineering Trustworthy Secure Systems](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/160/v1/r1/final)** — Security and trustworthiness integrated with engineering lifecycle reasoning.

## Reading protocol

1. Start with the **course map** and choose the next week.
2. Read the **linked Atlas primers** before the instructor notes.
3. Work through the original guide's examples and your own reproduction.
4. Open exactly one or two external references to resolve a definition, derivation, or method.
5. Produce the original week's artifact; log unclear terms for future encyclopedia expansion.

The list is curated, not an endorsement that every reference is freely downloadable or pedagogically self-contained.
