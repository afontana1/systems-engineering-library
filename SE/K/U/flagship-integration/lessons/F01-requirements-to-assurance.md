### F01 extension · Claim-to-measurement chain and conservative time evidence

**Problem and boundary.** The NS-U4.1 fictional emergency-dispatch service promises an acknowledgement within 8 seconds for at least 90% of accepted requests and adequate access for priority riders. An acknowledgement is not a vehicle arrival. The reported 80-case acceptance fixture is *synthetic* and covers one simulated dispatch endpoint, not the roadway, human operator, or incident response. Its timing instrument has a bounded ±0.25 s timestamp error; assume this is a worst-case bound, not a normal standard deviation.

**Derive the defensible pass rule.** For an observed latency $\hat t_i$ and absolute timestamp error bounded by $u$, the unknown true latency lies in $[\hat t_i-u,\hat t_i+u]$. To guarantee that a request satisfies $t_i\le T$, require $\hat t_i+u\le T$. Here $T=8$ s and $u=0.25$ s; five observations at 7.9 s count in the nominal test but fail the conservative classification. The nominal count is $72/80=90\%$, while the guard-band count is $67/80=83.75\%$. The subgroup count drops to $10/16=62.5\%$. A single aggregate point estimate is neither proof of threshold performance nor proof of fair access.

**Traceable argument.** A minimally useful record links `SYS-DISP-008` to (1) a signed requirement with load and operating-domain bounds; (2) a timestamping procedure and uncertainty bound; (3) a test campaign with configuration/revision and subgroup definitions; (4) a code/result digest; (5) deviations and dispositions. An interface contract and a dispatch timing record support different claims. They should not be merged into a single green indicator. The case evidence register has five claims, but only `C1` is actionable under its strict scope/revision/review/approval rule. `C2` references an old interface; `C3` has the wrong operating scope; `C4` lacks approval; `C5` has no record.

**Engineering judgment.** Even if all 80 observations were comfortably within 8 s, the sample would not prove behavior under a multi-agency communications outage. Measurement uncertainty, population selection, and intended-use validity remain separate evidence dimensions. An unsigned safety waiver cannot be inferred from a passing script.

**Worked exercise with solution.** Suppose the clock uncertainty is reduced to ±0.05 s without changing the recorded 7.9 s observations. The conservative rule now accepts all 72 nominal successes because $7.9+0.05\le8$. The point estimate returns to 90%, but its sampling uncertainty, scenario representativeness, and five-claim approval deficiencies remain. Explain why this instrument upgrade improves measurement credibility but not mission qualification.
