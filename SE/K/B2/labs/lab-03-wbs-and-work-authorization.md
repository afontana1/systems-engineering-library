# Hypothetical Lab 3 — The Missing Work Package

*EN.645.667 · Week 3 · Fictional rehearsal, not a substitute for the course assignment.*

**Read first:** [Book Chapter 3](../chapters/03-wbs-and-work-authorization.md). **Then consult:** [Course Week 3](../../../C/P0/EN645667.md).

## Scenario packet

A draft WBS contains vehicle build and software development but no named owner for accessibility demonstration or operator training. All people, figures and institutional decisions are synthetic. Use only the role authority and requirements stated here; if information is missing, list assumptions and propose the evidence needed.

## Student tasks

1. Repair the WBS using the 100-percent rule; list exclusions to prevent double counting.
2. Assign accountable control accounts and define two measurable work-package completion criteria.
3. Complete a fictional authorization request for the missing accessibility work.

## Suggested deliverables

Save `LAB03_DecisionMemo.md` and `LAB03_EvidenceRegister.csv`. The memo should define the decision, alternatives, authority, assumptions, analysis, remaining risks and exit condition. The register should contain claim, source, evidence date, configuration/version and reviewer status. Keep calculations and fact claims separable from judgments.

## Self-review rubric (10 points)

- 2 — Problem and authority framed correctly; 2 — All material scope, schedule, cost and technical effects surfaced; 2 — Reasoning and quantitative work reproducible where applicable; 2 — Counterargument and uncertainty addressed; 2 — Final decision bounded by evidence and approval rights.

## Reference response — read only after attempting

A sound repair makes the demonstration and training visible under unique WBS elements, allocates one accountable owner each, and refuses to release unbudgeted scope without change control. This response is a **minimum argument**, not a unique solution. Full credit requires a traceable rationale and acknowledgement of credible objections. In particular, a model or administrative approval must not be mistaken for a real-world safety authorization.
