# Hypothetical Lab 4 — The Estimate Challenge

*EN.645.667 · Week 4 · Fictional rehearsal, not a substitute for the course assignment.*

**Read first:** [Book Chapter 4](../chapters/04-estimating-and-reserves.md). **Then consult:** [Course Week 4](../../../C/P0/EN645667.md).

## Scenario packet

A vehicle-integration supplier proposes a fixed price based on an incomplete interface specification; the internal team offers a bottom-up estimate with uncertain rework. All people, figures and institutional decisions are synthetic. Use only the role authority and requirements stated here; if information is missing, list assumptions and propose the evidence needed.

## Student tasks

1. Build a three-line bottom-up estimate and explicitly name two correlated risk drivers.
2. Compare base cost, management reserve, fee and proposal price without double counting.
3. Conduct a cost challenge meeting and write a documented estimate-confidence statement.

## Suggested deliverables

Save `LAB04_DecisionMemo.md` and `LAB04_EvidenceRegister.csv`. The memo should define the decision, alternatives, authority, assumptions, analysis, remaining risks and exit condition. The register should contain claim, source, evidence date, configuration/version and reviewer status. Keep calculations and fact claims separable from judgments.

## Self-review rubric (10 points)

- 2 — Problem and authority framed correctly; 2 — All material scope, schedule, cost and technical effects surfaced; 2 — Reasoning and quantitative work reproducible where applicable; 2 — Counterargument and uncertainty addressed; 2 — Final decision bounded by evidence and approval rights.

## Reference response — read only after attempting

A defensible answer separates labor, purchases, scope and risk allowances; computes $342,000 from the sample inputs; and flags an unverified interface as a driver of both cost and schedule, not a generic percentage. This response is a **minimum argument**, not a unique solution. Full credit requires a traceable rationale and acknowledgement of credible objections. In particular, a model or administrative approval must not be mistaken for a real-world safety authorization.
