# Hypothetical Lab 2 — The Bid/No-Bid Table

*EN.645.667 · Week 2 · Fictional rehearsal, not a substitute for the course assignment.*

**Read first:** [Book Chapter 2](../chapters/02-rfp-and-proposal-strategy.md). **Then consult:** [Course Week 2](../../../C/P0/EN645667.md).

## Scenario packet

A fictional customer issues eight solicitation clauses, including one ambiguous uptime obligation and a strict price ceiling. All people, figures and institutional decisions are synthetic. Use only the role authority and requirements stated here; if information is missing, list assumptions and propose the evidence needed.

## Student tasks

1. Classify clauses as instruction, evaluation criterion, contractual requirement or ambiguity.
2. Produce an eight-row compliance matrix with evidence owners.
3. Write a one-page conditional bid/no-bid recommendation with three explicit decision triggers.

## Suggested deliverables

Save `LAB02_DecisionMemo.md` and `LAB02_EvidenceRegister.csv`. The memo should define the decision, alternatives, authority, assumptions, analysis, remaining risks and exit condition. The register should contain claim, source, evidence date, configuration/version and reviewer status. Keep calculations and fact claims separable from judgments.

## Self-review rubric (10 points)

- 2 — Problem and authority framed correctly; 2 — All material scope, schedule, cost and technical effects surfaced; 2 — Reasoning and quantitative work reproducible where applicable; 2 — Counterargument and uncertainty addressed; 2 — Final decision bounded by evidence and approval rights.

## Reference response — read only after attempting

A credible conditional bid requires the ambiguous uptime measurement to be clarified before binding price and test commitments. The $8.4M arithmetic does not cure a missing operational acceptance plan. This response is a **minimum argument**, not a unique solution. Full credit requires a traceable rationale and acknowledgement of credible objections. In particular, a model or administrative approval must not be mistaken for a real-world safety authorization.
