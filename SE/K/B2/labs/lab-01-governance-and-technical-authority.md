## Scenario packet

The sponsor wants public demonstrations next month; the safety engineer reports that an interface test has not been run; finance warns of a monthly funding threshold. All people, figures and institutional decisions are synthetic. Use only the role authority and requirements stated here; if information is missing, list assumptions and propose the evidence needed.

## Student tasks

1. Draft four decision-rights records and identify who can authorize testing, interface change, risk acceptance and funds.
2. Prepare a six-line decision brief showing what can proceed and what must remain blocked.
3. Stage a disagreement between PM, chief engineer, sponsor and assurance lead. Record an escalation rule.

## Suggested deliverables

Save `LAB01_DecisionMemo.md` and `LAB01_EvidenceRegister.csv`. The memo should define the decision, alternatives, authority, assumptions, analysis, remaining risks and exit condition. The register should contain claim, source, evidence date, configuration/version and reviewer status. Keep calculations and fact claims separable from judgments.

## Self-review rubric (10 points)

- 2 — Problem and authority framed correctly; 2 — All material scope, schedule, cost and technical effects surfaced; 2 — Reasoning and quantitative work reproducible where applicable; 2 — Counterargument and uncertainty addressed; 2 — Final decision bounded by evidence and approval rights.

## Reference response — read only after attempting

A defensible answer separates funding authority from technical concurrence and independent safety authorization. A public demonstration involving passengers is not approved by schedule pressure; limited bench work may proceed under an existing authorization. This response is a **minimum argument**, not a unique solution. Full credit requires a traceable rationale and acknowledgement of credible objections. In particular, a model or administrative approval must not be mistaken for a real-world safety authorization.
