# Phase 3 authoritative resource shelf

The companion is an instructional starting point; use these as external references when the original course guide calls for deeper theory or formal practice. These sites require internet and may update.

- [NIST/SEMATECH Engineering Statistics Handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/) — probability models, sampling, hypothesis tests, DOE, regression and reliability. Start by matching the analysis to its assumptions; do not use a significance test as a requirement acceptance rule.
- [BIPM/JCGM publications](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/jc/jcgm/publications) — Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement and related documents. Useful when separating measurement uncertainty from variation.
- [NASA-STD-7009, Standard for Models and Simulations](https://standards.nasa.gov/node/263) — modeling and simulation practices and credibility relative to purpose; check the applicability to your program.
- [NASA Systems Engineering Handbook](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/) — connect model outputs, technical assessment, requirements, verification and evidence.
- [SEBoK — Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Guide_to_the_Systems_Engineering_Body_of_Knowledge_(SEBoK)) — architecture, modeling, systems thinking and integration context.
- [FMI 3.0 specification](https://fmi-standard.org/docs/3.0/) — interfaces for model exchange, co-simulation and scheduled execution; does not certify coupling accuracy.
- [System Dynamics Society](https://systemdynamics.org/) — systems dynamics community and educational references; pair causal maps with explicit stock-flow equations.
- [SciPy stats reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/stats.html) — distribution estimation and testing APIs when you elect to use third-party tools. The included teaching toolkit itself uses standard Python.

## How to work with references

Maintain a reference record with access date, edition/version, exact section and why it supports your model assumption. A reference to a standard is not evidence that your particular implementation complies with it.
