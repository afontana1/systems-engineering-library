# Analyze transient and steady-state output with defensible run controls

**Course:** Foundations of Modeling and Simulation in Systems Engineering · **Week:** 08 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0  
**Study guide:** [Open the original course guide](../../../C/P3/EN645757.md) and jump to **Week 8** for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.  
**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

Transient simulations answer finite-horizon questions such as a school-day peak; steady-state simulations estimate long-run regimes after initialization effects diminish. Use multiple independent replications for finite horizons and justified warm-up or batch means for steady state. Autocorrelated observations within one run are not independent replications. Monitor interval width relative to a decision threshold.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A 30-minute simulation starting with an empty queue understates long-run waiting in a congested system. Conversely, discarding the first hour of a one-hour rush-period study removes the period of interest.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating every timestamp as an independent sample and reporting unrealistically narrow intervals. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 8 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is initialization bias?
2. Why use independent replications?
3. What does a confidence interval quantify?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is initialization bias?** Distortion caused by starting the simulation in an artificial state that differs from the process of interest.

2. **Why use independent replications?** They provide independent run-level observations suitable for many interval and comparison methods.

3. **What does a confidence interval quantify?** Uncertainty in an estimator under the model, sampling, and analysis assumptions—not all real-world uncertainty.

</details>
