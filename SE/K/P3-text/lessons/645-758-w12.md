**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

The final advanced M&S study must communicate what the model supports, what it does not and how that was tested. Preserve the experiment manifest, mathematical derivations, parameter provenance, solver diagnostics, challenge scenarios and independent comparison. A model can be correct about its equations and still wrong for the mission. Make a decision recommendation with conditional triggers and risks.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Two valid solvers agree on a charging trajectory but both use a battery capacity that predates a redesign. The consistency is computational evidence, not baseline applicability. A final review should detect this version mismatch.

**Assumption to challenge:** Using cross-tool agreement as proof of external validity when both share the same assumptions. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 12 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is the final evidence chain?
2. What must a reproduction log record?
3. Why require a live challenge?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is the final evidence chain?** Trace from decision and requirements through models, data, experiments, results, credibility, risk, and recommendation.

2. **What must a reproduction log record?** Environment, versions, commands, inputs, hashes/configuration, tests, runs, outputs, and deviations.

3. **Why require a live challenge?** To demonstrate that the model is usable for change impact rather than only for a rehearsed presentation.

</details>
