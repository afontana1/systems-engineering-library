# Quantify requirement compliance, technical margin, allocation risk, and design options

**Course:** Metrics, Modeling and Simulation for Systems Engineering · **Week:** 10 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0  
**Study guide:** [Open the original course guide](../../../C/P3/EN645756.md) and jump to **Week 10** for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.  
**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

Requirement compliance is a probability or logical judgment about specified scenarios, thresholds and confidence, not a comparison of two single averages. Margin is the distance from a limit in compatible units. Distinguish estimated compliance conditional on a model from demonstrated compliance with real test evidence. Allocation risk can increase if all subsystem margins assume favorable conditions simultaneously.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A 12-minute 95th-percentile wait limit is not satisfied by a simulated mean of 8 minutes if the 95th percentile is 14. When the high-percentile estimate itself is uncertain, report a confidence or credible interval.

**Assumption to challenge:** Calling a positive nominal margin “verified compliance” without quantifying uncertainty and evidence. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 10 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is probability of compliance?
2. What is technical margin?
3. Why report conditional compliance?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is probability of compliance?** The modeled or estimated probability that a precisely defined requirement event is satisfied under stated populations, conditions, configurations, and uncertainty.

2. **What is technical margin?** A controlled difference between a performance estimate or bound and a requirement or allocation, under a specified calculation convention.

3. **Why report conditional compliance?** Overall compliance can hide failure in zones, weather, configurations, or protected/critical subgroups.

</details>
