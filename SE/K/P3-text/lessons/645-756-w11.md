# Update lifecycle evidence and decide among upgrade, replacement, retirement, or more information

**Course:** Metrics, Modeling and Simulation for Systems Engineering · **Week:** 11 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0  
**Study guide:** [Open the original course guide](../../../C/P3/EN645756.md) and jump to **Week 11** for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.  
**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

Lifecycle decisions require evidence updating over time. Compare upgrade, repair, replacement and retirement against forecast benefit, cost, mission risk and learning value. Condition-monitoring data can change failure estimates, but the update must reflect selection and censoring. Value of information can justify a bounded experiment when the best action changes under credible uncertainty.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Replacing a charger costs $45k and reduces expected downtime cost by $14k/year for four years. A simple undiscounted benefit of $56k exceeds the purchase cost, but this is not sufficient without discounting, failure uncertainty, installation loss and alternatives.

**Assumption to challenge:** Choosing on sunk cost or assuming the expected-value winner is robust to unfavorable states. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 11 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is degradation?
2. What is a change point?
3. What is value of information?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is degradation?** A systematic loss of performance or condition over exposure or time, distinguished from reversible variation and measurement change.

2. **What is a change point?** A time at which the statistical behavior or governing process changes.

3. **What is value of information?** The expected decision benefit from reducing a specific uncertainty, compared with the cost and delay of obtaining information.

</details>
