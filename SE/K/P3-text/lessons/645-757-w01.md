# Frame the M&S decision, intended use, terminology, and model-method trade space

## The idea in context

Choose a simulation paradigm by the decision and dominant mechanism, not software popularity. Discrete-event simulation captures resource contention; system dynamics captures aggregated accumulation and feedback; continuous models capture physical states; agent-based methods capture heterogeneous local behavior. Write an intended-use statement naming outputs, scenarios, required fidelity and what the model will *not* support.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A shuttle queueing study needs rider arrivals, dispatch events, vehicle capacity and wait-time tails. A discrete-event approach is natural. A policy question about long-term ridership and fleet investment may require feedback modeling instead.

**Assumption to challenge:** Selecting a visually impressive simulation technology before identifying the decision mechanism. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 1 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is intended use?
2. How does a model differ from a simulation?
3. Why can more fidelity reduce usefulness?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is intended use?** The bounded decision purpose, user, claim, conditions, and consequence for which the model is evaluated.

2. **How does a model differ from a simulation?** A model is a representation; a simulation executes a model to produce behavior or sampled outcomes.

3. **Why can more fidelity reduce usefulness?** It can increase data, cost, defects, opacity, and validation burden without improving the decision.

</details>
