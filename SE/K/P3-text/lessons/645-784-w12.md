## The idea in context

A design recommendation is a traceable chain from objectives to measures, alternatives, models, constraints, uncertainty and evidence. Demonstrate reproducibility and identify near-optimal challengers. The final decision should specify what would invalidate its evidence and which future observations trigger redesign or additional learning.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A three-shuttle design meets current demand with 10% cost margin, but performance margin disappears under correlated rain and peak demand. The board should see the condition and a contingency, not only the attractive baseline score.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating optimization output as an irreversible engineering approval without validation or monitoring. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 12 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is an evidence chain?
2. Why separate recommendation from authorization?
3. What is a reversal condition?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is an evidence chain?** Traceable linkage from decision need through objectives, measures, alternatives, data/models, analyses, uncertainty/preferences, and recommendation.

2. **Why separate recommendation from authorization?** Other lifecycle, safety, regulatory, funding, or validation conditions may govern implementation.

3. **What is a reversal condition?** A stated change in evidence, assumptions, constraints, preferences, or context that would reopen or change the decision.

</details>
