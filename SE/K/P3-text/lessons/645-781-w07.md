# Explain delays, nonlinearities, oscillation, overshoot, and changing loop dominance

## The idea in context

Feedback delay and nonlinear decision rules explain overshoot and oscillation. A controller responding to outdated backlog can increase capacity just as demand eases, wasting resources; a threshold response may create switching. Test delay length, adjustment gains and saturation to identify the region where loop dominance changes. Avoid attributing every fluctuation to endogenous feedback when exogenous noise may dominate.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A service manager adds vehicles based on a 4-week-old queue average. By the time additional vehicles arrive, the seasonal peak has ended, producing oversupply. A 1-week signal changes the response but may amplify noise.

**Assumption to challenge:** Assuming every oscillation is generated by the modeled loop rather than measurement or external events. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 7 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What often creates oscillation?
2. What is overshoot?
3. Why does delay order matter?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What often creates oscillation?** Balancing feedback operating through significant delay or inertia.

2. **What is overshoot?** A stock exceeds a sustainable or target level before limiting feedback acts.

3. **Why does delay order matter?** It changes how dispersed or sharp the response is.

</details>
