## The idea in context

Robust decisions perform acceptably under variable states rather than optimizing only an average scenario. Explore regret, tail loss, downside probability and sensitivity to probability assumptions. Expected value of perfect information (EVPI) bounds what knowing an uncertainty exactly could be worth; imperfect observations usually have lower value and must justify acquisition cost.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Strategy A earns $50k in high demand and loses $20k in low demand; B earns $25k in either state. With equal state likelihood, A has expected $15k, B $25k. Perfect information could select A in high demand and B in low, giving $37.5k expected and EVPI $12.5k.

**Assumption to challenge:** Calling the highest nominal expected value robust or paying more for information than its decision value. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 11 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is global sensitivity analysis?
2. What is regret?
3. What is robust feasibility?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is global sensitivity analysis?** Assessment of input influence across their joint uncertainty ranges, including nonlinearities/interactions depending on method.

2. **What is regret?** Loss relative to the best decision that would have been chosen for the realized state or scenario.

3. **What is robust feasibility?** Meeting required constraints across a defined uncertainty/scenario set or with a specified probability.

</details>
