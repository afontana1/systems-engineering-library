## The idea in context

Observed data have provenance, selection mechanisms and measurement error. A sample can be large yet biased if it excludes overload periods or inaccessible trips. EDA should compare distributions by configuration and identify missingness, censoring, drift and suspicious outliers before model fitting. Quantify repeatability and calibration error of instruments instead of burying them in operational variance.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Two sensors report 12.0 and 12.8 minutes for the same pickup event because one starts the clock at request creation and the other at acceptance. An 0.8-minute discrepancy is definitional, not just instrument noise; no average can repair it.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating missing difficult cases as random observations and interpreting a biased dashboard as population truth. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 3 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is censoring?
2. What is selection bias?
3. What is a measurement model?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is censoring?** Partial observation of an outcome, such as knowing a component survived at least until study end.

2. **What is selection bias?** Systematic difference between observed and target populations caused by the observation or inclusion process.

3. **What is a measurement model?** A functional relationship linking the measurand to input quantities and corrections used to evaluate the reported value.

</details>
