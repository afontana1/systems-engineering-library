# Explore the trade space with alternatives, uncertainty, sensitivity, and model-based decision records

## The idea in context

Trade-space exploration begins with feasible constraints, then preferences, then uncertainty. Dominated alternatives should be removed before discussing scores. If stakeholders disagree about weightings, report the Pareto set and sensitivity to preferences. Model-linked results must record versioned inputs, solver choices, scenarios and evidence so a decision can be reproduced. A weighted score cannot rescue an infeasible or unsafe design.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Fleet A costs 1.0 M and serves 80 riders/h; fleet B costs 1.2 M and serves 110 riders/h. If minimum demand is 100 riders/h, A is infeasible despite its lower cost. Compare B with feasible C only after scenario and uncertainty checks.

**Assumption to challenge:** Publishing one preferred score without constraint violations or weight sensitivity. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 9 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is feasibility?
2. What is a decision reversal?
3. What is robustness?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is feasibility?** Satisfaction of mandatory constraints and applicability conditions before preference comparison.

2. **What is a decision reversal?** A change in selected alternative caused by plausible changes in assumptions, evidence, model form, thresholds, or preferences.

3. **What is robustness?** Performance or preference stability across relevant uncertainty, scenarios, and assumptions.

</details>
