## The idea in context

Feasibility constraints precede preference scoring. Dominance removes designs no worse on every objective and strictly better on at least one; Pareto frontiers reveal genuine trade-offs. Constraints can be deterministic, chance-based or scenario-specific. Ranking infeasible alternatives with arbitrary penalties can conceal unacceptable safety or capacity violation.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Option A: $900k, 85 trips/h. B: $1.1M, 100 trips/h. C: $1.2M, 98 trips/h. If higher capacity is better and lower cost is better, B dominates C; if minimum capacity is 95, A is infeasible.

**Assumption to challenge:** Calling a high-weighted score optimal while the candidate violates a hard service requirement. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 4 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. Does a Pareto frontier require weights?
2. Can a dominated option be chosen?
3. What is weak dominance?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **Does a Pareto frontier require weights?** No. It depends on objectives, directions, feasibility, and performance values.

2. **Can a dominated option be chosen?** Only with an explicit reason such as omitted objectives, uncertainty, constraints, or strategic considerations; the dominance result itself must not be hidden.

3. **What is weak dominance?** No worse on all objectives, with definitions varying on whether strict improvement is required.

</details>
