## The idea in context

Morphological design enumerates combinations of subsystem choices, but not every combination is physically or operationally compatible. A cross-consistency matrix eliminates impossible pairings before optimization. Set-based thinking preserves multiple feasible concepts until evidence is sufficient to converge; it is not indecision if learning plans and deadlines are explicit.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Choose propulsion {battery, fuel cell}, charging {depot, opportunistic}, dispatch {fixed, dynamic}. Eight combinations exist nominally, but some require infrastructure unavailable on campus.

**Assumption to challenge:** Counting every morphological combination as feasible or down-selecting before high-risk assumptions are tested. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 7 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is morphology?
2. What is cross-consistency assessment?
3. Why count the design space?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is morphology?** Systematic exploration of combinations across multiple design dimensions.

2. **What is cross-consistency assessment?** Evaluation of whether option pairs or combinations can coexist under stated rules.

3. **Why count the design space?** To understand combinatorial growth, coverage, and what has been excluded or sampled.

</details>
