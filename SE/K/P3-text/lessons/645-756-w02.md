## The idea in context

A probability distribution encodes a mechanism under assumptions. Counts, positive durations, proportions and rare failures have different supports; tail choice matters when a requirement is percentile-based. Dependence can overwhelm the effect of individual variance: bad weather may simultaneously raise demand and reduce effective capacity. Keep aleatory variability separate from epistemic parameter uncertainty and model-form disagreement.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

At 10 arrivals/minute, a Poisson model has variance 10. If measured variance is 35, heterogeneous demand or clustering may invalidate it. Treating arrival counts and boarding delays as independent would understate simultaneous peak stress.

**Assumption to challenge:** Fitting a convenient normal distribution to strictly positive, skewed waiting time without tail validation. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 2 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is a random variable?
2. What is a percentile?
3. What is overdispersion?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is a random variable?** A numerical representation of an uncertain outcome defined on a probability space.

2. **What is a percentile?** A value below which a specified proportion of the modeled or observed population lies.

3. **What is overdispersion?** Variance greater than expected under a reference count model such as Poisson, often indicating heterogeneity or dependence.

</details>
