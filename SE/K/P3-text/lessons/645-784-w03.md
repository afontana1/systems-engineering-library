## The idea in context

Decision models separate actions, uncertain states, outcomes and evidence. Influence diagrams show informational and causal dependencies; decision trees represent contingent choices. Assign probabilities only when justified and expose deep uncertainties instead of arbitrary decimal precision. A good model lists which uncertainty resolves before versus after a commitment.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A fleet order must be placed before winter demand is known. A pilot can collect demand data first but delays service. The value of the pilot depends on both information quality and opportunity cost.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating all uncertain variables as observed before a decision in an unrealistic “perfect foresight” comparison. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 3 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is a decision node?
2. What is a chance node?
3. Why predeclare constraints?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is a decision node?** A controllable choice owned by the decision authority.

2. **What is a chance node?** An uncertain quantity or event relevant to outcomes or information.

3. **Why predeclare constraints?** To reduce result-driven screening and make exclusion rules reviewable.

</details>
