# Build causal-loop diagrams as testable feedback hypotheses

## The idea in context

Causal-loop diagrams state signed hypotheses about change. A plus sign means variables move in the same direction *all else equal*; it does not mean desirable. A minus sign means opposing directional movement. Reinforcing loops amplify changes, balancing loops counter them. Delays alter timing and can produce oscillation. Build loops with meaningful measurable variables, then challenge every causal link.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Higher ridership increases revenue, enabling better service, which increases ridership: a reinforcing hypothesis. More ridership also increases crowding, which increases wait and reduces ridership: a balancing mechanism.

**Assumption to challenge:** Interpreting plus links as good outcomes or claiming that causal loops prove quantitative dynamics. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 3 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What does a positive causal link mean?
2. Does positive mean beneficial?
3. What makes a loop reinforcing?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What does a positive causal link mean?** Cause and effect move in the same direction relative to what they otherwise would have done.

2. **Does positive mean beneficial?** No.

3. **What makes a loop reinforcing?** It amplifies change; it contains zero or an even number of negative links.

</details>
