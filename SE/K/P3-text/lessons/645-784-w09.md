## The idea in context

Computational experiment design balances information gained per model run. Space-filling designs explore broad domains; factorial designs estimate structured effects and interactions; adaptive designs focus on promising or uncertain regions. Randomness and solver noise need replications. Hold out validation cases before fitting a surrogate to avoid testing on the same samples used for optimization.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

With a budget of 40 simulations, a coarse 4×4 grid uses 16 settings and leaves 24 runs for replication and targeted refinement. An alternative Latin hypercube explores a broader high-dimensional space but needs separate boundary checks.

**Assumption to challenge:** Using a dense plot of unreplicated outputs as if it were a noise-free response surface. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 9 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is confounding?
2. What is a screening design?
3. What is a response-surface design?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is confounding?** When effects cannot be separately estimated from the chosen design.

2. **What is a screening design?** A design intended to identify influential factors efficiently, often with limited interaction resolution.

3. **What is a response-surface design?** A design supporting estimation of curvature and local response behavior.

</details>
