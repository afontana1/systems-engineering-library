Establish why systems thinking is needed and frame a learning challenge

Cross-course case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

The idea in context

Systems thinking studies patterns generated by structure, feedback and delay—not simply lists of causes. Choose a dynamic problem with consequences over time, an observable reference mode and competing explanations. Stakeholders may disagree about boundary and time horizon; that disagreement is part of the model, not an inconvenience to hide. State what policy could change and what evidence could disconfirm the model.

Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Campus shuttle complaints rise after a service improvement. One hypothesis is that demand increased faster than capacity; another is that complaint reporting became easier. Both fit the same observed count, but imply very different actions.

Assumption to challenge: Using “everything is connected” as an explanation without testable feedback structure. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the original guide's Week 1 knowledge check, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

  1. Why is a recurring event not yet a dynamic problem?
  2. What makes an explanation endogenous?
  3. Is an external shock forbidden?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

  1. Why is a recurring event not yet a dynamic problem? It lacks an explicit behavior pattern, time horizon, comparison state, and causal structure.

  2. What makes an explanation endogenous? The principal behavior is generated by interactions inside the chosen model boundary.

  3. Is an external shock forbidden? No. It may initiate behavior, but the model should explain how internal structure shapes the response.

</details>

What not to conclude

The worked case shows a method and its reasoning; it does not establish a measured performance distribution, requirements compliance, model accreditation, or safety of an actual system. Refer to the full guide and authoritative sources for the deeper assignment and application constraints.
