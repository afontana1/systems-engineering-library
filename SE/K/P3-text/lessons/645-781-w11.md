# Design robust policy portfolios and identify leverage, resistance, and unintended consequences

## The idea in context

Policy design searches for leverage points where structural changes outperform surface fixes. Use portfolios with multiple mechanisms, test adoption delays, gaming, resistance and unintended consequences. Distinguish robust performance across plausible structures from a single optimized forecast. Communicate policies as conditional experiments with monitoring and reversal triggers.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A dashboard-only policy may create pressure to reject difficult trips to improve average wait. A portfolio also measures rejected trips and access equity, adjusts capacity and audits clock definitions.

**Assumption to challenge:** Optimizing an easily gamed KPI instead of stakeholder outcomes. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 11 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. Is the most sensitive parameter always the best leverage point?
2. What is a policy portfolio?
3. What is policy resistance?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **Is the most sensitive parameter always the best leverage point?** No; changeability, structure, feasibility, and side effects matter.

2. **What is a policy portfolio?** A coordinated set of interventions targeting complementary mechanisms.

3. **What is policy resistance?** Endogenous responses that offset or reverse an intervention.

</details>
