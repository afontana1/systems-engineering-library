## The idea in context

Human-service systems have queues, staff capacity, quality feedback, learning and delayed demand effects. Define outcomes from the served population’s perspective, not only provider throughput. Model behavioral adaptation and equity explicitly where evidence supports them; acknowledge uncertainty and ethics. A small illustrative stock-flow model is not enough for resource allocation affecting real patients or vulnerable groups.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A clinic reduces appointment length, increasing visits/hour while repeat visits rise because unresolved cases accumulate. Throughput increases but the care-outcome measure deteriorates; both mechanisms need data.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating faster processing as an unqualified improvement without quality or return-demand feedback. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 10 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. How can rapid discharge increase crowding?
2. What creates a burnout loop?
3. Why can observed demand be low despite high need?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **How can rapid discharge increase crowding?** Poor transition support can increase delayed readmission.

2. **What creates a burnout loop?** Workload increases fatigue/burnout, reducing effective capacity and further increasing workload.

3. **Why can observed demand be low despite high need?** Access barriers and poor trust suppress requests.

</details>
