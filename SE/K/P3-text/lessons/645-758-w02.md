## The idea in context

Continuous dynamics are often expressed as `dx/dt = f(x,u,t)`, with state x, input u and initial condition. Conservation laws, constitutive relations and unit consistency constrain the equations. Numerical solvers approximate trajectories; stability and accuracy depend on step size and algorithm. Test against analytical limits, conservation and step refinement before drawing physical conclusions.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

For simple cooling `dT/dt=-k(T-Ta)`, solution is `T(t)=Ta+(T0-Ta)exp(-kt)`. With k=0.1/min, Ta=20°C and T0=50°C, after 10 min T≈31.04°C. A numerical model should approach this value under step refinement.

**Assumption to challenge:** Confusing numerical convergence with physical validity or using inconsistent time units. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 2 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is a state?
2. Why compare step sizes?
3. When is a transfer function insufficient?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is a state?** The minimum information needed with future inputs to determine future system behavior.

2. **Why compare step sizes?** To estimate numerical convergence and detect discretization-driven conclusions.

3. **When is a transfer function insufficient?** For nonlinear, multivariable, mode-dependent, or initial-condition-sensitive behavior.

</details>
