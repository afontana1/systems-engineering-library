# Verify and validate the model, assess the design, respond to change, and defend the final baseline

## The idea in context

Model verification asks whether the implementation faithfully represents the intended model; validation asks whether the abstraction is fit for its stated real-world use. Inspect structural completeness, behavioral edge cases, numerical implementation and scenario representativeness separately. A final baseline should include unresolved weaknesses and decision conditions, not merely polished diagrams. Change control must rerun affected analyses and trace evidence to exact model revisions.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A simulation passes all syntax and trace checks yet predicts unrealistically short pickup times because it excludes boarding assistance. It is verified as coded but not validated for accessibility decisions. A review states which decision questions remain unsupported.

**Assumption to challenge:** Calling a model “validated” without identifying a particular decision and operating domain. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 12 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is model verification?
2. What is model validation?
3. What is design assessment?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is model verification?** Evidence that the model and its implementations, equations, queries, transformations, and configurations conform to their specifications and intended logic.

2. **What is model validation?** Evidence that the model is an adequate representation for its stated intended use.

3. **What is design assessment?** Evaluation of whether the proposed design, based on integrated evidence, is feasible, compliant, robust, supportable, and ready for a decision or next lifecycle step.

</details>
