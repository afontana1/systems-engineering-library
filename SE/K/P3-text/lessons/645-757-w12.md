## The idea in context

M&S continues into production and sustainment: throughput prediction, spares, training and decision support. Update model parameters as data arrive while preserving historical baselines so previous decisions remain reproducible. Hybrid, agent-based and real-time approaches can be valuable, but additional complexity requires its own validation. Close the study with a model handoff, maintenance strategy and retirement conditions.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Telemetry shows charger turnaround times drifting from 15 to 25 min over a year. A maintenance model should update the distribution and compare the costs of cleaning, replacement and scheduling changes.

**Assumption to challenge:** Continuously retraining a model without checking drift, configuration changes or lost reproducibility. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 12 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is model drift?
2. When is reaccreditation needed?
3. Why use a surrogate model?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is model drift?** Growing mismatch between model assumptions/parameters and the changing system or environment.

2. **When is reaccreditation needed?** When changes materially affect the intended use, evidence, risk, configuration, or acceptance conditions.

3. **Why use a surrogate model?** To approximate expensive model behavior for rapid exploration, while carrying approximation error and scope.

</details>
