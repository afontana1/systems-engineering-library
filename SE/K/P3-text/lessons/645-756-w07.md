# Build and validate performance response models across use and environment

**Course:** Metrics, Modeling and Simulation for Systems Engineering · **Week:** 07 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0  
**Study guide:** [Open the original course guide](../../../C/P3/EN645756.md) and jump to **Week 7** for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.  
**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

A response model approximates how performance changes with load, environment and design settings. Its purpose may be prediction, explanation or optimization; those goals require different validation. Check functional form, extrapolation, heteroscedasticity and physical feasibility. Predictive intervals for a new observation are wider than intervals for an estimated mean. Validate by scenario and time, not only random split, when conditions drift.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A quadratic fit predicts negative waiting time at zero demand. Even if R² is high across training data, it violates physical support. A constrained response surface or a queueing-informed model is more defensible.

**Assumption to challenge:** Using excellent in-sample fit as evidence of safe behavior beyond tested operating conditions. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 7 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is a prediction interval?
2. What is extrapolation?
3. Why is R-squared insufficient?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is a prediction interval?** An interval for a future observation that includes both model-estimation uncertainty and outcome variability under the model.

2. **What is extrapolation?** Using a model outside the input, configuration, population, or environmental region supported by development and validation evidence.

3. **Why is R-squared insufficient?** It does not establish correct form, unbiased prediction, valid assumptions, tail calibration, or external performance.

</details>
