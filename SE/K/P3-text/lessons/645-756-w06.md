## The idea in context

In a factorial experiment, an interaction means a factor changes the effect of another factor. Main effects averaged over conditions may conceal severe scenario-specific behavior. Analyze residuals, variance assumptions, blocking and missing runs before interpreting coefficients. Confirmation runs should be independent of the data used to select the candidate configuration.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Policy A reduces delay by 2 min in light traffic but increases delay by 5 min in heavy traffic. Its average main effect could be close to zero, yet the interaction is operationally decisive.

**Assumption to challenge:** Optimizing on a fitted factorial surface without checking residual structure or independent confirmation. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 6 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What does a main effect represent?
2. Why preserve model hierarchy?
3. What is aliasing?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What does a main effect represent?** The average change associated with a factor across the levels of other factors in the fitted design.

2. **Why preserve model hierarchy?** Including an interaction generally requires retaining its component main effects for coherent interpretation.

3. **What is aliasing?** Confounding of model terms such that the design cannot estimate them separately.

</details>
