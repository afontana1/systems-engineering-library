## The idea in context

Uncertainty propagation maps plausible inputs through a model to output distributions. Monte Carlo sampling approximates that transformation, but cannot fix a false model, biased input data or missing dependence. State seeds, scenario probabilities, convergence diagnostics and treatment of epistemic uncertainty. Sensitivity metrics answer different questions: local derivatives indicate nearby behavior; global indices or grouped scenarios expose large nonlinear effects.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

If a trip comprises 12±2 min travel and 3±1 min boarding, independent normal assumptions imply time SD ≈√5 min. Correlation changes this result. Simulate both positive and negative correlation scenarios before reporting a 95th percentile.

**Assumption to challenge:** Reporting a stable million-sample histogram while ignoring uncertain model structure or shared causes. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 9 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What does Monte Carlo simulation do?
2. What is Monte Carlo error?
3. Why model dependence?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What does Monte Carlo simulation do?** It numerically propagates specified uncertain inputs through a model to estimate a distribution or statistic of outputs.

2. **What is Monte Carlo error?** Numerical sampling uncertainty in the estimated output statistic, distinct from modeled system uncertainty.

3. **Why model dependence?** Joint extremes and common causes can materially change output variance, tails, and compliance probability.

</details>
