# Use simulation for concept exploration, alternatives, and scenario analysis

**Course:** Foundations of Modeling and Simulation in Systems Engineering · **Week:** 07 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0  
**Study guide:** [Open the original course guide](../../../C/P3/EN645757.md) and jump to **Week 7** for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.  
**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

Concept exploration compares alternatives over a common scenario and experimental protocol. Use common random numbers for paired comparisons when appropriate, so noisy demand variation does not obscure design differences. Analyze cost and service distributions, constraints and downside risk, not merely means. A concept that wins only in one narrow demand scenario should not become a universal recommendation.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Policy A and B use the same sampled demand streams for 100 replications. The paired difference in wait time is much more stable than comparing unrelated A and B draws, provided input streams represent equivalent events.

**Assumption to challenge:** Selecting the highest-ranked concept from a single random seed or unbalanced input conditions. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 7 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. Why use common scenarios?
2. What can common random numbers reduce?
3. What is robustness?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why use common scenarios?** To ensure differences arise from alternatives rather than inconsistent environmental conditions.

2. **What can common random numbers reduce?** Variance in estimated differences when paired stochastic inputs are meaningfully aligned.

3. **What is robustness?** Acceptable performance across plausible assumptions, scenarios, and uncertainty.

</details>
