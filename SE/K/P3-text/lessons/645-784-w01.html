Frame the decision, distinguish problem types, and expose decision-process risk

The idea in context

A decision is a choice among feasible actions under objectives, constraints and uncertainty. First distinguish selecting a discrete alternative from designing parameters or managing a sequence of choices. Document decision rights, timing, irreversibility, information gaps and who bears consequences. Decision-process risk includes hidden objectives, incompatible evidence and premature commitment.

Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Choosing whether to purchase 2, 3 or 4 shuttles is a selection problem; choosing battery capacity and charge policy is design. These choices may interact and should not be collapsed into one unstructured score.

Assumption to challenge: Starting with a list of favorite alternatives before establishing the decision frame and authority. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the original guide's Week 1 knowledge check, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

  1. What is decision quality?
  2. What is a selection problem?
  3. What is a design problem?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

  1. What is decision quality? The quality of framing, objectives, alternatives, information, reasoning, commitment, and implementation—not whether uncertainty happened to produce a favorable outcome.

  2. What is a selection problem? A decision among predefined alternatives.

  3. What is a design problem? A decision in which alternatives must be generated through architectural or parametric choices.

</details>
