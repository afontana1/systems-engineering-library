Elicit, derive, validate, and query system requirements from the integrated analysis

Course: Applied Analytics for Model-Based Systems Engineering · Week: 07 of 12 · Phase 3 companion: edition 1.0
Study guide: Open the original course guide and jump to Week 7 for prescribed readings, full assignment and mastery criteria.
Cross-course case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

The idea in context

Requirements are decisions about required behavior and constraint, not copy-pasted stakeholder prose. Derive them from scenario analyses, hazards, interfaces and allocations; distinguish origin, rationale, acceptance method and priority. Validate necessity and stakeholder relevance separately from syntactic quality. Query for unallocated requirements, conflicting thresholds, unmatched test methods and requirements with no source. Requirements remain provisional when the evidence or operating envelope is uncertain.

Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

“Shuttles shall be fast” is not testable. “For approved weekday scenarios, 95% of accepted requests shall have pickup wait ≤12 min” is measurable if “accepted,” measurement clock, sample plan and exclusions are defined.

Assumption to challenge: Mistaking bidirectional traceability for proof that the traced requirement is correct. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the original guide's Week 7 knowledge check, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

  1. What is requirement validation?
  2. What is a derived requirement?
  3. What is a circular derivation?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

  1. What is requirement validation? Confirmation that a requirement correctly and adequately expresses stakeholder, mission, and lifecycle need.

  2. What is a derived requirement? A lower-level or additional requirement logically necessary because of analysis, architecture, interface, risk, or constraint.

  3. What is a circular derivation? A set of requirements justified only by one another with no external need, constraint, analysis, or decision foundation.

</details>

What not to conclude
