## The idea in context

Baseline modeling identifies the gap between current operations and desired outcomes. Before testing alternatives, reproduce current-state counts, utilization, waiting distribution and known bottlenecks. Validate against independent observation, not the dataset used to tune parameters. If the baseline cannot reproduce dominant patterns, comparisons among alternatives may be meaningless.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

The real service experiences long waits after lunch, but the simulated baseline has a uniform wait profile. Rather than tuning to the overall mean, inspect time-varying demand and the vehicle recovery policy.

**Assumption to challenge:** Using an unvalidated baseline to claim a 20% improvement from a new scheduling algorithm. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 4 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is calibration?
2. Why use tail measures?
3. What is a counterfactual baseline?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is calibration?** Adjustment of model parameters or structure to improve agreement with selected referent data.

2. **Why use tail measures?** Stakeholder harm and capacity failures often occur in distribution tails, not at the mean.

3. **What is a counterfactual baseline?** An explicitly defined no-change or alternative reference condition used for comparison.

</details>
