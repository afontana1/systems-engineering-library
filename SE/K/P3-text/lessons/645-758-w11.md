## The idea in context

Hybrid simulation combines continuous physical states with discrete mode events. Events may create discontinuities; the solver must detect and localize them. Real-time and hardware-in-the-loop constraints add deadlines, clock synchronization and safe test isolation. Fixed-step schemes can be required operationally even if adaptive solvers are more accurate offline; characterize the trade explicitly.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A battery thermal controller switches on at 40°C and off at 37°C. Without hysteresis, measurement noise may cause rapid toggling; an adaptive solver may hide an event-timing problem that appears on a real-time controller.

**Assumption to challenge:** Assuming that numerical stability on a desktop establishes safe real-time or HIL operation. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 11 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. Why fixed step for real time?
2. What is jitter?
3. What is an overrun?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why fixed step for real time?** Execution must complete predictably on a wall-clock schedule.

2. **What is jitter?** Variation in execution or I/O timing around the intended schedule.

3. **What is an overrun?** A computation that fails to complete before its deadline.

</details>
