## The idea in context

Reliability, availability and maintainability answer different questions. For constant independent exponential failure and repair rates, steady-state availability is `MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR)`, but real systems may have wear-out, preventive maintenance, crew constraints and common-cause failures. Censoring occurs when an item has not yet failed by the end of observation; discarding censored cases biases life estimates.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

If mean time between failures is 100 h and mean repair is 5 h, the idealized availability is 100/105 ≈ 95.24%. It is not necessarily fleet mission availability, because standby policy, spares and correlated charging failures are omitted.

**Assumption to challenge:** Interpreting an exponential MTBF estimate as a guarantee of lifetime or hazard independence. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 8 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is reliability?
2. What is availability?
3. What is right censoring?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is reliability?** The probability of performing required functions without failure for a specified time or mission under stated conditions.

2. **What is availability?** The probability or proportion of time a system is in an operable and committable state under stated support conditions.

3. **What is right censoring?** Knowing that an item survived at least to a time without observing its failure time.

</details>
