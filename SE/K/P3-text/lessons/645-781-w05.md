## The idea in context

Stocks accumulate; flows change stocks; auxiliaries influence rates. The governing identity is `stock(t+dt)=stock(t)+(inflow-outflow)*dt`, with compatible units. A causal loop cannot determine behavior until accumulation and functions are specified. Stock-flow models should conserve entities where appropriate, prohibit impossible negative stocks and distinguish rates from levels.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

If 30 riders enter a waiting queue per hour and 24 board per hour for 2 hours, waiting backlog rises by 12 riders, assuming no abandonment. Neither flow alone is the queue level.

**Assumption to challenge:** Confusing number waiting (people) with boarding rate (people/hour), yielding dimensional errors. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 5 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What defines a stock?
2. What are flow units?
3. Can an auxiliary create delay?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What defines a stock?** It accumulates the net effect of inflows and outflows and carries system state through time.

2. **What are flow units?** Stock units per unit time.

3. **Can an auxiliary create delay?** Only algebraic or information-processing effects; true accumulation delay generally requires a stock or delay function.

</details>
