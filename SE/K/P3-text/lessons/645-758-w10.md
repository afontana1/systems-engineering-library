## The idea in context

Population and biological dynamics commonly involve flows, delays, capacity limits and heterogeneous behavior. Use the same systems principles—stock conservation, contact structure, feedback and units—while acknowledging ethically sensitive or uncertain assumptions. Visualizations should reveal parameter sensitivity and credible ranges, not visually imply precise forecasts. A low-fidelity illustrative model is not a substitute for validated public-health analysis.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A hypothetical susceptible population model gives `dS/dt=-βSI/N`. The incidence depends on β, contact patterns and initial conditions; a plotted trajectory without uncertainty should not be used as an operational health forecast.

**Assumption to challenge:** Transporting a simple toy population model into real policy decisions without domain validation. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 10 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is homogeneous mixing?
2. Why use an SEIR rather than SIR model?
3. When prefer an agent model?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is homogeneous mixing?** An assumption that contacts occur uniformly within the modeled population or group.

2. **Why use an SEIR rather than SIR model?** To represent a latent exposed period when it matters to timing.

3. **When prefer an agent model?** When individual heterogeneity, networks, schedules, or localized interventions drive the question.

</details>
