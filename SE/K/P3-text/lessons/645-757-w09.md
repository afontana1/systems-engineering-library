## The idea in context

Simulation experiments can estimate response surfaces or select among designs. Factors may include fleet size, threshold policies, charger reliability and demand season. Randomize experimental order, manage stochastic error and choose a selection method aligned with false-selection consequences. An optimizer must not exploit unphysical artifacts of the simulation model.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Vehicle count {2,3,4} and policy {fixed, adaptive} yield six design cells. Replicate each with aligned demand seeds; estimate the cost-delay frontier instead of optimizing one noisy realization.

**Assumption to challenge:** Treating the minimum observed simulated cost as the population optimum without confirmation. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 9 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is an interaction?
2. Why randomize or control run order/seeds?
3. What is a response surface?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is an interaction?** The effect of one factor changes depending on the level of another factor.

2. **Why randomize or control run order/seeds?** To avoid confounding factor effects with temporal or stochastic artifacts.

3. **What is a response surface?** An empirical approximation of the relationship between factors and response near a region of interest.

</details>
