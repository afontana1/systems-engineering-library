## The idea in context

A surrogate replaces an expensive model with a fast approximation. Its error depends on design-space coverage, smoothness, noise and extrapolation. Validate where decisions are sensitive, not only where data are dense. Uncertainty-aware optimization should penalize high prediction error or demand high-fidelity confirmation at predicted optima.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A quadratic surrogate predicts minimum wait at charger power 80 kW, just beyond its training range of 20–60 kW. Extrapolated confidence is not supported; simulate the proposed design and add boundary samples.

**Assumption to challenge:** Confusing surrogate predictive accuracy on training points with reliable optimization decisions. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 10 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is generalization error?
2. What is extrapolation?
3. Why assess frontier preservation?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is generalization error?** Prediction error on relevant unseen data.

2. **What is extrapolation?** Prediction outside or beyond the support of training data, often with greater uncertainty.

3. **Why assess frontier preservation?** Decision use may depend on relative ordering and non-dominance rather than average prediction error.

</details>
