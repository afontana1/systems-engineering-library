## The idea in context

Parametric design changes continuous or integer decisions under constraints. Objective functions may be smooth, discontinuous or noisy; engineering feasibility can include simulation-based requirements. A Pareto solution improves one objective only by worsening another. Search algorithms must handle discreteness, uncertainty and validated parameter ranges; numerical output does not create engineering evidence.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Let fleet count n be an integer from 2–5 and charge power p be continuous 20–80 kW. Optimize cost and 95th-percentile wait subject to charger capacity, safety and accessibility constraints, rather than blending all into one opaque score.

**Assumption to challenge:** Optimizing a surrogate outside its training domain and accepting the result without a feasibility recheck. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 8 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is Pareto rank 1?
2. Why repeat optimization seeds?
3. What is epsilon-constraint?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is Pareto rank 1?** The non-dominated set; later ranks are obtained by removing earlier fronts and repeating.

2. **Why repeat optimization seeds?** To assess stochastic search variability and coverage stability.

3. **What is epsilon-constraint?** Optimize one objective while bounding others at specified levels.

</details>
