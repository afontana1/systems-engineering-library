## The idea in context

Advanced simulation requires an explicit architecture: linked physical models, discrete controllers, environmental inputs and decision analytics, each at its own timescale. Define which output must be trusted and how errors from constituent models propagate. More fidelity is not always better; it may add unknown parameters, solver instability and irreproducibility. Plan credibility, collaboration and configuration control before coupling models.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A shuttle thermal-energy study needs drivetrain load, battery temperature, ambient conditions and charging behavior. It does not necessarily need detailed tire tread deformation; the omission needs a sensitivity-based justification.

**Assumption to challenge:** Making the model larger without defining interfaces, time step and decision-level error tolerance. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 1 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. Why define non-uses?
2. What makes a model requirement testable?
3. Who accepts model use?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why define non-uses?** To prevent evidence from being generalized beyond its validity and authority.

2. **What makes a model requirement testable?** A specified property, tolerance, condition, method, and acceptance threshold.

3. **Who accepts model use?** The designated decision or technical authority, informed by independent credibility evidence.

</details>
