## The idea in context

A systems insight is credible when its boundary, feedback assumptions, evidence, quantitative behavior and alternative explanations are visible. A final presentation should separate observed reference modes, hypothesized mechanisms, simulated behavior and policy recommendation. Identify which policy rankings survive parameter uncertainty and structural alternatives, and establish a learning loop for future evidence.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

An overtime proposal reduces modeled backlog only when preventive maintenance is assumed unaffected. When a plausible maintenance backlog mechanism is enabled, the ranking reverses. The recommendation becomes conditional and includes a monitoring experiment.

**Assumption to challenge:** Presenting a simulation result as a universal policy fact rather than a conditional hypothesis. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 12 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is the final product?
2. Why separate evidence, model finding, and recommendation?
3. What is model drift?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is the final product?** A controlled learning and decision package, not merely a model file.

2. **Why separate evidence, model finding, and recommendation?** They have different epistemic status.

3. **What is model drift?** Reuse for new purposes or conditions without renewed review.

</details>
