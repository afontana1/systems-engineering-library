## The idea in context

A confidence interval describes an inference procedure under model and sampling assumptions, not a probability that a fixed frequentist parameter lies in one realized interval. Hypothesis tests can show evidence against a null but are not engineering acceptance rules. Equivalence tests require a predeclared tolerance; non-significance alone does not demonstrate similarity. Always pair uncertainty estimates with an effect size and consequence.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Two controller versions differ by 0.4 s in mean dispatch time with a 95% interval from -0.2 to +1.0 s. This interval permits both improvement and degradation. It cannot establish equivalence unless the chosen equivalence bounds and method justify it.

**Assumption to challenge:** Using p > 0.05 as proof that two designs perform the same. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 4 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is an estimand?
2. What does a confidence interval communicate?
3. Why is p < 0.05 insufficient?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is an estimand?** The precisely defined quantity the analysis seeks to estimate for a target population and condition.

2. **What does a confidence interval communicate?** The range produced by a procedure with stated long-run coverage under its assumptions, used to assess plausible engineering values and precision.

3. **Why is p < 0.05 insufficient?** It does not establish effect importance, model validity, requirement compliance, or decision value.

</details>
