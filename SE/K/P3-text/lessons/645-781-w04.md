# Use archetypes and mental models to generate competing intervention hypotheses

**Course:** Systems Thinking and Systems Dynamics · **Week:** 04 of 12 · **Phase 3 companion:** edition 1.0

**Cross-course case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle. Figures are synthetic and illustrate reasoning; they do not validate real transport operations.

## The idea in context

Archetypes such as fixes-that-fail, shifting-the-burden and limits-to-growth are reusable hypotheses, not diagnostic labels. Mental models shape which mechanisms stakeholders see; collect disconfirming viewpoints. To use an archetype, identify stocks, delays, short-term benefits, long-term side effects and intervention points. Quantify later to test whether the alleged archetype can reproduce observed behavior.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

Offering temporary shuttle overtime reduces waits this week but increases maintenance backlog, causing later breakdowns and longer waits. This may be fixes-that-fail only if the maintenance mechanism and delay have evidence.

**Assumption to challenge:** Naming a familiar archetype and then ignoring evidence inconsistent with it. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.
