Integrate, reproduce, challenge, and defend the lifecycle analytics decision

The idea in context

A defensible analytics baseline is reproducible and contestable. A reviewer should be able to locate source data, preprocessing, equations, code versions, seeds, assumptions, fit diagnostics, uncertainty, limitations and the decision statement. Challenge the result with alternative models and withheld scenarios. When results disagree, document the disagreement rather than selecting the calculation that confirms the preferred answer.

Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

A model predicts 11.7 min 95th-percentile wait but a withheld rainy-day dataset shows 14.1 min. The recommendation should narrow its domain or require mitigation; a one-number traffic-light score is not a credible defense.

Assumption to challenge: Equating a polished dashboard or high sample count with an independently reviewable analysis. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the original guide's Week 12 knowledge check, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

  1. What is an evidence chain?
  2. Why separate recommendation from authorization?
  3. What is reproducibility?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

  1. What is an evidence chain? Traceable linkage from decision and requirements through measures, data, models, uncertainty, analysis, recommendation, and action conditions.

  2. Why separate recommendation from authorization? Funding, safety, regulatory, contractual, validation, or governance gates may still control implementation.

  3. What is reproducibility? The ability to regenerate results from controlled data, source, environment, parameters, and documented procedures.

</details>

What not to conclude

The worked case shows a method and its reasoning; it does not establish a measured performance distribution, requirements compliance, model accreditation, or safety of an actual system. Refer to the full guide and authoritative sources for the deeper assignment and application constraints.
