## The idea in context

Multiphysics models connect electrical power, mechanical torque, heat, fluid flow and control limits. Coupling must conserve energy and respect units, signs and timescales. A one-way coupling may fail if temperature changes resistance or viscosity; a fully coupled solve increases computational cost and can become stiff. Trace shared variables and check global balance, not just each subsystem’s local output.

## Worked reasoning: apply it to the shuttle case

At 10 kW electric input and 85% efficiency, mechanical output is 8.5 kW and losses are 1.5 kW. If the thermal model receives only 0.6 kW of waste heat, the interfaces omit 0.9 kW or an alternative loss path.

**Assumption to challenge:** Combining individually plausible component models that violate a shared energy budget. Record what observation or test would expose this failure before using the result in a decision.

## Test understanding before reading the answers

The next three questions are adapted from the **original guide's Week 4 knowledge check**, with its answer guidance retained so you can learn and self-check in one place.

1. What is a conserving interface?
2. Why record energy residual?
3. What is an algebraic loop?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal guided answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is a conserving interface?** An interface whose across/through variables enforce the relevant conservation law.

2. **Why record energy residual?** To detect signs, missing losses, inconsistent components, and numerical drift.

3. **What is an algebraic loop?** A set of instantaneous dependencies requiring simultaneous solution.
