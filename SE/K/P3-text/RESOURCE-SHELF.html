Phase 3 authoritative resource shelf

The companion is an instructional starting point; use these as external references when the original course guide calls for deeper theory or formal practice. These sites require internet and may update.

  • NIST/SEMATECH Engineering Statistics Handbook — probability models, sampling, hypothesis tests, DOE, regression and reliability. Start by matching the analysis to its assumptions; do not use a significance test as a requirement acceptance rule.
  • BIPM/JCGM publications — Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement and related documents. Useful when separating measurement uncertainty from variation.
  • NASA-STD-7009, Standard for Models and Simulations — modeling and simulation practices and credibility relative to purpose; check the applicability to your program.
  • NASA Systems Engineering Handbook — connect model outputs, technical assessment, requirements, verification and evidence.
  • SEBoK — Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge — architecture, modeling, systems thinking and integration context.
  • FMI 3.0 specification — interfaces for model exchange, co-simulation and scheduled execution; does not certify coupling accuracy.
  • System Dynamics Society — systems dynamics community and educational references; pair causal maps with explicit stock-flow equations.
  • SciPy stats reference — distribution estimation and testing APIs when you elect to use third-party tools. The included teaching toolkit itself uses standard Python.

How to work with references

Maintain a reference record with access date, edition/version, exact section and why it supports your model assumption. A reference to a standard is not evidence that your particular implementation complies with it.
