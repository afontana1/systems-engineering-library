# Hypothetical Lab 01 — Change response chain

**Course:** EN.645.780 — Agile Systems Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 01](../chapters/01.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 1](../../../C/P4/EN645780.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **distinguish agile systems engineering from software rituals and frame the transformation problem**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Response-time requirement: identify an operational stimulus, affected system boundary, measurable response, maximum elapsed time, permissible cost and explicit assurance guardrail.

## Provided evidence and constraints

Northstar observes 18 days from issue report to diagnosis, 22 days to approved change, 12 days to implementation, and 68 days to integrated test and release. Total response is 120 days. Even cutting implementation to 6 days yields 114, not the 45-day mission target.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W01; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Observed stages are not independent. Approval rework sends a change backward, so the total distribution can have a long tail even if typical stage means appear small. Plot waiting and work time separately and confirm which stage owns the largest avoidable delay.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

A defensible answer begins with **the unit of agility is a response to change, not a sprint**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. Specify the stimulus, detection latency, decision authority, intervention, and acceptance evidence. A team can complete many short iterations while the certified release path remains 120 days. Because the response is a chain of work and approval, optimizing only the programming interval shifts delay rather than reducing it. Model each stage separately and inspect which bottlenecks block effective response.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Response-time requirement: identify an operational stimulus, affected system boundary, measurable response, maximum elapsed time, permissible cost and explicit assurance guardrail. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
