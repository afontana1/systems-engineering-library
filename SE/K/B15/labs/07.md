# Hypothetical Lab 07 — Expected change cost

**Course:** EN.645.780 — Agile Systems Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 07](../chapters/07.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 7](../../../C/P4/EN645780.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **architect products and engineering processes for affordable change, options, and continuous integration**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Option test: at $80k premium and $25k saving/integration, derive break-even count as ceiling(80/25)=4 changes.

## Provided evidence and constraints

A modular adapter costs $80k more initially but saves an estimated $25k for each of six future integrations. Expected net advantage under those assumptions is $70k; test sensitivity to only two future integrations.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W07; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Expected change economics can be summarized as initial modularization premium versus the probability-weighted cost of future integrations. Also include interface test and version-governance costs. A module creates an option only if dependencies and assumptions are actually bounded.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

<details><summary>Expand reference reasoning</summary>

A defensible answer begins with **modularity changes the economics of learning**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. Architectural coupling creates a hidden cost for every experiment. A clean interface contract can isolate change, but only when its semantics, performance budget and configuration ownership are stable. Real options matter: an initially expensive modular design can be cheaper over many probable changes if it reduces expected rework.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Option test: at $80k premium and $25k saving/integration, derive break-even count as ceiling(80/25)=4 changes. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
