# Hypothetical Lab 01 — Decision-use register

**Course:** EN.645.782 — Foundations of Digital and Mission Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 01](../chapters/01.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 1](../../../C/P4/EN645782.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **lifecycle, acquisition, and digital/mission framing**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Create four decision rows with evidence freshness, decision consequences and required review owner.

## Provided evidence and constraints

Northstar must choose whether to reposition vehicles, curtail charging, or request mutual aid. The first needs near-term demand estimates; the second energy and state-of-charge data; the third cross-agency availability and permission. One generic dashboard cannot validate all three.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W01; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

A lifecycle artifact earns authority only for its configured purpose. The same demand model can be acceptable for fleet budgeting and insufficient for real-time dispatch. List evidence consumers and expiration times.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

<details><summary>Expand reference reasoning</summary>

A defensible answer begins with **digital engineering begins with decisions**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. For each lifecycle decision define the owner, timing, alternatives, evidence objects, and consequence of being wrong. One information asset may be authoritative for design geometry yet unsuitable for the current operational state. An end-to-end mission outcome may depend on nontechnical interventions, not merely a better digital product.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Create four decision rows with evidence freshness, decision consequences and required review owner. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
