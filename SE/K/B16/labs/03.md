# Hypothetical Lab 03 — Credibility matrix

**Course:** EN.645.782 — Foundations of Digital and Mission Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 03](../chapters/03.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 3](../../../C/P4/EN645782.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **m&s foundations, lifecycle uses, and credibility**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Define two different claims for one demand simulation and set an appropriate credibility gate for each.

## Provided evidence and constraints

A coarse demand model reproduces daily totals but misses five-minute surges. It may support annual fleet budgeting, not real-time curbside dispatch. Define separate credibility cases.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W03; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Use separate columns for intended use, conceptual model, input validity, implementation verification, validation domain, uncertainty and excluded uses. A model credible at annual scale may not resolve minute-level extremes.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

A defensible answer begins with **model credibility is specific to use**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. A simulation can be verified as correctly implemented yet invalid for a proposed operational inference. Separate conceptual-model adequacy, implementation verification, input pedigree, validation evidence, uncertainty, and scope limitations. Demand fidelity only where the decision sensitivity justifies it.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Define two different claims for one demand simulation and set an appropriate credibility gate for each. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
