# Hypothetical Lab 06 — Parametric units and uncertainty

**Course:** EN.645.782 — Foundations of Digital and Mission Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 06](../chapters/06.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 6](../../../C/P4/EN645782.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **model-based analytics and case-study review**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Calculate range in favorable and adverse conditions; state whether a 65 km duty cycle survives the bound.

## Provided evidence and constraints

For a vehicle with 24 kWh usable energy at 0.32 kWh/km, nominal range is 75 km. At 0.40 kWh/km under adverse conditions it is 60 km; a 65 km duty cycle is no longer feasible.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W06; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Range=energy/consumption is meaningful only when numerator and denominator use compatible units and conditions. Propagate bounded input uncertainty before asserting feasibility. Threshold crossing is a decision question, not merely a value recalculation.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

### Reference critique — view only after attempting

A defensible answer begins with **analysis closes the loop between model and decision**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. Parameter relationships can be derived and executed, but every numerical result requires units, assumptions, sensitivity and a decision threshold. Analyses should fail loudly when a parameter is missing or its version is incompatible with the architecture.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Calculate range in favorable and adverse conditions; state whether a 65 km duty cycle survives the bound. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
