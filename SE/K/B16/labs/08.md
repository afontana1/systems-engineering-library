# Hypothetical Lab 08 — Typed traceability

**Course:** EN.645.782 — Foundations of Digital and Mission Engineering  
**Read first:** [Chapter 08](../chapters/08.md) · **Assessed assignment:** [Original Week 8](../../../C/P4/EN645782.md)

## Situation and objective

Northstar's fictional program review committee has received a proposed change involving **digital thread architecture and executable traceability**. The program lead wants an immediate favorable recommendation, the safety lead requires an intact evidence chain, and the operator demands a measurable benefit. Your task is to avoid a purely procedural response and build a bounded, reviewable engineering decision.

**Practice question:** Change REQ-17 from B to C and compute which tests require re-execution or engineering justification.

## Provided evidence and constraints

REQ-17 rev C is allocated to COMP-8 rev D and verified by TEST-22 rev B. If TEST-22 references REQ-17 rev B, the coverage query must report stale evidence instead of a green check.

- Configuration: instructional Northstar baseline P4-W08; all observations are synthetic and must not be presented as measured field evidence.
- Required guardrails: traceability to the released requirement revision; safety and security exceptions must be reviewed; inaccessible demand cannot be discarded to inflate a service metric.
- You have authority to recommend an experiment, not to bypass a release gate or certify the system.
- An alternative explanation must be recorded and actively tested rather than dismissed.

## Investigation procedure

1. **State the decision.** Name the decision maker, available options, consequence of being wrong, and deadline. Define a testable success claim.
2. **Map the mechanism.** Diagram the causal chain or model relationships described in the chapter. Record inputs, units, release identifiers, and who owns each assumption.
3. **Calculate or trace a baseline.** Reproduce the supplied nominal result. Distinguish direct observation, derived value, and illustrative assumption.
4. **Stress the logic.** Apply the adverse change below. Note which constraints become active, which evidence becomes stale and which stakeholders are affected.
5. **Propose a controlled response.** Specify the smallest legitimate experiment, evaluation criteria, independent reviewer, and stop rule.
6. **Defend the recommendation.** Present one argument in favor and one credible counterargument. Explicitly identify evidence that would reverse the recommendation.

### Change challenge

Define a directed edge such as verifies(test,requirement_revision), not generic related_to. Broken, orphaned, unauthorized and stale links require different remedies. Show the effect of a requirement change through the graph.

## What to submit

Create a one- to three-page decision note (Markdown is fine) containing a decision table, the key calculation or trace, assumptions, uncertainty, configuration, guardrail status and next evidence-gathering action. Include one diagram or dependency map and a compact evidence index. Do not copy the reference critique as your answer.

A defensible answer begins with **a digital thread is an executable relation chain**, not with a preferred tool or ceremony. A useful thread supports requirement-to-design-to-test-to-deployment queries. Model each relation with source/target identifiers, version, meaning, owner and timestamp. Change-impact analysis must catch stale evidence, unresolved many-to-many mappings and orphaned items.

**Stress-case interpretation:** Change REQ-17 from B to C and compute which tests require re-execution or engineering justification. An unqualified approval would be inappropriate if critical evidence, stakeholder impact, or configuration ownership remains unresolved. A conditional decision can be valid when it specifies the next test, independent reviewer and stop rule. Compare your reasoning against this structure and revise your memo where the evidence chain is incomplete.
