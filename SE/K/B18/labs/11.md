## Decision brief

The Northstar regional review board is confronting the following change:

**A partner withdraws funding for the highest-scoring option. Rework the roadmap and identify the decision owner.**

Your role is a systems engineer serving an independent review panel, not the person empowered to overrule participating agencies or affected communities. The sponsor wants a quick positive decision; you must provide a conditional recommendation that survives explicit challenge. Keep the technical argument distinct from unresolved questions of consent, authority, procurement or distributive fairness.

## Case file and starting evidence

Represent rollout options with cost, dependencies, reversibility, learning value and separate agency approvals. Prefer a staged pathway when a small pilot can falsify a critical assumption before irreversible infrastructure changes.

**Reproducible starting model:** Use a constrained portfolio: option A costs 3 and yields 5 units of mission value; B costs 4 and yields 7; C costs 6 and yields 8, with a budget of 7. Enumerate feasible sets and check complementarity rather than greedily selecting the highest ratio.

Prepare an evidence ledger with a row for each claim: claimed outcome; measured or assumed input; provenance and revision; analysis method; affected population; owner; uncertainty; decision it informs. Treat copied or syndicated observations as one upstream source until independence is demonstrated.

### Investigation protocol

1. **Frame the competing decisions.** Specify at least three realistic options including maintaining the status quo with monitoring. For each option state who can approve it and who could lawfully refuse.
2. **Model the current state.** Draw the network, causal theory, process flow or boundary critique appropriate to the chapter. Identify the unit of analysis and time horizon. Distinguish organizational and technical interfaces.
3. **Reproduce the baseline.** Recalculate the numerical example when relevant. For a qualitative method, reproduce the classification or opposed stakeholder models faithfully rather than compressing them into one score.
4. **Introduce an adversarial change.** Use the decision brief to invalidate a favorable assumption. Show which lines in the evidence ledger, requirements, agreement or model must change. Do not reuse evidence attached to an incompatible revision.
5. **Review distribution and rights.** Report how the result differs by access need, neighborhood, participating agency or authority. State any missing groups; explain how they could contest the conclusion.
6. **Recommend the smallest informative action.** Define an acceptance threshold, a pilot or analytical experiment, independent reviewer, monitoring period, reversal/stop rule and a review date. State what would change your decision.
7. **Communicate the result.** Produce a one-page decision memo plus the technical annexes. Distinguish a verified fact from a calculated estimate, an interpretation and a value judgment.

## Stress case

A partner withdraws funding for the highest-scoring option. Rework the roadmap and identify the decision owner.

Maximizing a single numerical score can hide whose rights are ignored, what agreements can expire, or which measurements are untrusted.

Do not patch a discrepancy by silently changing the question being asked. Instead create a change log describing the old claim, new information, changed assumptions and downstream artifacts requiring revision. Record where an additional observation is necessary before proceeding.

## Artifacts to produce

- **Model or boundary representation:** one diagram, table, or structured argument with identified owners and exclusions.
- **Evidence and uncertainty register:** source, timestamp/revision, unit, confidence basis and independent reviewer for each important claim.
- **Alternatives table:** options, feasibility checks, outcome measures, sensitivity, affected groups and authority dependencies.
- **Decision memo:** recommendation, explicit conditions, rejected alternatives, operational non-claims and next evidence gate.

## Reference response and critique

A defensible submission will first identify the *mechanism* in **roadmaps are contingent agreements**, then make the scenario's new constraint active. The preferred action may change, and a conditional recommendation is perfectly acceptable. Treat a single solution advertised as universally optimal, a graph without authority, or a favorable metric without denominator and excluded populations as a major finding. In contrast, a useful answer names a decision-maker, provides observable evidence that could reverse the judgment, and preserves uncertainty and dissent.

**Specific expected warning:** Maximizing a single numerical score can hide whose rights are ignored, what agreements can expire, or which measurements are untrusted.

This is a reference *critique*, not a preapproved decision; different recommendations can earn full credit if assumptions, affected actors, arithmetic and evidence limitations are traceable.
