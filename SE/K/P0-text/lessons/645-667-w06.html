Integrated baseline reviews: can the plan actually be executed?

Conceptual explanation

Integration is the test

An Integrated Baseline Review (IBR) examines whether project scope, technical maturity, assigned responsibility, schedule, resources, budget, risks and performance measurement form a coherent executable plan. A spreadsheet can sum correctly and still conceal missing verification work or impossible facility usage. The review is not just 'budget validation'; it checks whether managers understand and can execute the authorized scope.

Build an explicit mapping: requirement and product deliverable → WBS control account → work package → schedule activities → assigned resources → budget and earning rules → verification/acceptance evidence. Every arrow is a place where phantom scope can hide. At the start of work, technical baselines may still evolve, but the management plan must make uncertainty and authorized planning packages visible.

Interview the people who own the work

A credible IBR asks a control-account manager: What do you deliver? Why is your duration plausible? Which predecessor must finish? What makes the activity complete? What assumption could break the estimate? Who approves a variance correction or a change? These interviews test whether data are truly understood rather than merely assembled by planning software.

Findings and conditional readiness

Flag missing scope, impossible resource loads, unowned dependencies, undefined earning rules, unsupported rates and risk exposure without contingency plans. Review decisions may authorize low-risk work while withholding funds or activity for unresolved high-consequence areas. Track closure evidence with owner and approved restrictions.

Forecast is not baseline

During execution, the team maintains actuals and estimates to complete. An IBR-approved plan provides a baseline for measuring variance; it is not evidence that no forecast changes will ever be needed. A disciplined project differentiates (1) approved performance measurement baseline, (2) current estimate at completion and (3) change-controlled new commitments.

Worked example: reason through the case

Example: WBS 1.4 'Integration and Verification' contains $70k but the schedule shows integration complete before the campus network is installed. The test lead has not confirmed facility access. A passing IBR would be premature. Correct logic links, secure facility and network availability, update resource load and risk contingency, then record new schedule/cost implications before formal readiness.

Retrieval and transfer practice

  1. Find two defects in a baseline with a test WBS account but no test activities or named tester.
  2. What should be reviewed when supplier hardware delivery moves by three weeks?
  3. Explain the difference between approved baseline and estimate at completion.

A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: approving an integrated baseline because all columns are filled, without testing causal relationships and resource feasibility.
