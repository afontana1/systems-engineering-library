## Conceptual explanation

### Project communication is a design problem
Communication planning should be based on decisions that recipients must make, not on a generic desire to 'keep everyone informed'. A senior sponsor needs deviations from commitments, options, cost/schedule consequences and requests for authority. A test engineer needs configuration, requirements and defect evidence. An operator needs operational impact, readiness restrictions and escalation contact. The same situation must be represented consistently at different levels of detail.

A stakeholder map considers interest, influence, affected outcomes, decision rights and likely communication needs. It is not a label of which people are 'difficult'. Resistance may reveal a legitimate neglected constraint. Listening for operational knowledge often improves requirements and implementation plans.

### Diagnose conflict before choosing a technique
Distinguish conflict over technical evidence, resources, priorities, authority, process and personal treatment. If a technical lead disputes a schedule because the test environment is unavailable, negotiating better rapport will not make the facility exist. Identify the underlying interests and constraints, and establish a shared fact base. Use escalation when delegated authority is exceeded, when safety is implicated or when the dispute blocks the schedule.

### Leader behavior under uncertainty
Effective technical leadership allows uncomfortable evidence to surface without punishing the messenger. Ask teams for what changed since the prior review, what assumptions are weakest and what help is needed. Keep decisions traceable, acknowledge uncertainty and avoid fictional green status. A concise decision log preserves date, options, facts, chosen action, authority, affected baseline and revisit conditions.

### Remote and multi-organizational teams
Write interfaces between organizations as carefully as software interfaces: ownership, turnaround times, inputs, expected outputs and escalation. Asynchronous reports should include context and explicit requests rather than unexplained metrics. Misaligned incentives and time-zone handoffs can create project failures even when individual specialists are competent.

## Worked example: reason through the case

**Example:** Software lead says a feature is 'done'; operations says it is unusable. Investigation shows software meant 'merged into branch' while operations meant 'trained, integrated and accepted in the field.' The PM convenes both parties, defines a common completion state with acceptance evidence, updates reporting and resolves schedule impact. A communication conflict was actually a definition-of-done and baseline integration failure.

## Retrieval and transfer practice

1. Draft a two-sentence executive escalation for missing test equipment.
2. How would you distinguish a relationship conflict from an engineering-evidence conflict?
3. What should a decision log record after a disagreement about release readiness?

<details>
<summary><strong>Reveal explained answers after attempting the problems</strong></summary>

1. State affected milestone/mission impact and current evidence; present options with cost/schedule/risk and request an identified decision by a specific date. Avoid concealing constraints behind optimistic percentages.

2. Check disputed facts, success criteria and authority first; independently review tests and constraints. If evidence agrees but trust/behavior remains problematic, address interpersonal/process issues separately.

3. Decision date and authority, alternatives, evidence/revisions reviewed, concerns and concurrence, selected restriction or release, action owners, updated forecast and conditions for reconsideration.

</details>

## A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: assuming more meetings solve a conflict whose root cause is incompatible acceptance definitions or unclear authority.
