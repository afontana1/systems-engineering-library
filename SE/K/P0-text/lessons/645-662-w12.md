## Conceptual explanation

### The system concept is an argument you can inspect
A defensible preliminary concept tells a continuous story: mission and stakeholders → operating scenarios and success measures → requirements → functions → alternative architectures → selected design rationale → interfaces → planned realization and evidence → managed risk and decisions. Each arrow represents a potentially falsifiable connection. If any step disappears, the concept may be attractive but ungrounded.

Do not conflate high document count with maturity. A concise, consistent architecture with clear assumptions is stronger than a huge repository of unlinked drafts. Construct a one-page evidence map in which each major claim points to a configuration-controlled artifact. A reader should be able to identify the 'as-of' version, unresolved questions, and the conditions under which the recommendation would change.

### Defend decisions by separating facts, assumptions and values
Facts are supported by observations or accepted references. Assumptions are plausible but awaiting evidence. Values are stakeholder preferences and constraints used to make tradeoffs. A choice may be technically feasible yet unacceptable to an operating authority, or preferred under one weight profile but not another. The defense should not disguise those different kinds of judgments.

A graduate-level oral defense probes counterfactuals: What changes if demand doubles? If a supplier misses a date? If connectivity is lost? If the system boundary includes emergency response? A good answer names the assumptions, traces the impact, and identifies the next information needed—not an improvised certainty.

### The continuing knowledge map
Later MBSE courses formalize trace relationships; conceptual design deepens architectures and trade studies; integration and test courses quantify evidence; project management controls commitments; simulation and decision science deal with uncertainty. This volume supplies the common conceptual spine, not a substitute for those deeper competencies.

## Worked example: reason through the case

**Example defense brief:** Claim: 'The proposed two-vehicle pilot can provide useful low-speed accessible transport.' Evidence: ConOps, pilot-route assumptions, requirements, scenario tests, access design, trade scoring and integration plan. Open conditions: safe pickup geometry unconfirmed, wet-weather sensor evidence incomplete. Recommendation: authorize detailed design and controlled test-site development, **not** public deployment. This is a defensible qualified decision because the authorization scope matches the evidence.

## Retrieval and transfer practice

1. Trace one stakeholder need through an architecture element to proposed acceptance evidence.
2. What should happen when a reviewer finds that an assumption makes the preferred trade option infeasible?
3. Design a 90-second defense of a concept with one important unresolved risk.

## A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: presenting unverified operational readiness when only concept feasibility has been demonstrated.
