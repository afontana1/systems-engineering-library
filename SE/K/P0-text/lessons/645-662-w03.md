## Conceptual explanation

### Describe use before specifying equipment
A mission statement says what worthwhile outcome a system enables and for whom. A Concept of Operations (ConOps) narrates how actors interact with the system across situations: normal operation, peak demand, equipment degradation, emergency response, maintenance and retirement. It is a bridge between a vague aspiration and the operational evidence by which success will be judged. Avoid writing a ConOps that presupposes a specific technology before alternatives are examined.

A stakeholder need expresses desired value from a particular viewpoint. A shuttle rider may want predictable wait time; an accessibility representative may need equitable boarding; security personnel require safe emergency intervention. Needs often conflict. Capture both the need and why it matters. Resolve contradictions through explicit decisions, not by merging incompatible text.

### Three different kinds of measure
A **measure of effectiveness (MOE)** evaluates mission outcomes in the intended context: e.g., percentage of rider trips completed within a service-time target. A **measure of performance (MOP)** describes system performance characteristics: e.g., fleet boarding throughput in passengers/hour. A **technical performance measure (TPM)** monitors a key design attribute while it matures: e.g., sensor detection latency or energy consumption per trip compared with the evolving target. The categories overlap in practice; label them by decision use rather than treating them as universal taxonomic boundaries.

Measures need a numerator, denominator, sampling period, population, conditions and data owner. 'Service reliable' is not an MOE. 'At least 95% of scheduled trips completed within five minutes of published arrival over a 30-day pilot, excluding only documented campus closures' is inspectable. Challenge the exclusions: a measure can look favorable by removing difficult conditions that real users face.

### Scenario to evidence chain
For each scenario, record trigger, preconditions, actor intentions, main steps, off-nominal branches and outcome. Link the outcome to a need, MOE, enabling functions and candidate requirements. This is a causal narrative, not merely a requirements list. If a need has no realistic scenario, challenge whether it is necessary. If a safety-critical scenario has no measure, ask how anyone will know whether the system is good enough.

## Worked example: reason through the case

**Example:** 'Student returns to residence after a late lab.' Assumptions: rain, limited lighting, one vehicle in maintenance. Need: safe and predictable service. MOE: fraction of requested late trips with safe completed drop-off inside 20 minutes; MOP: usable late-night fleet capacity; TPM: pedestrian-classification latency at night. The service may meet the latency TPM yet fail the MOE because dispatch cannot recover from maintenance. This shows why evidence must span levels.

## Retrieval and transfer practice

1. Write one normal and one degraded scenario for a campus shuttle route.
2. Classify waiting time, lidar processing latency and fraction of riders arriving by curfew as MOE, MOP or TPM.
3. Design an auditable success measure for accessibility.

<details>
<summary><strong>Reveal explained answers after attempting the problems</strong></summary>

1. Normal: user requests ride, receives pickup estimate, boards, reaches stop and exits. Degraded: communications fail; operator acknowledges, safe state is entered, alternate accessible transport is arranged, and closure is logged.

2. Fraction arriving by curfew is an MOE; waiting time can be a service MOE or a service MOP depending on decision framing; lidar latency is a design TPM. Explain intended decision for ambiguous cases.

3. For example, percent of mobility-device journeys completed without unplanned assistance under defined boarding scenarios over 100 audited trials; specify observer, denominator and failure categorization.

## A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: using easily measured component speed as a proxy for user success without evidence of the connection.
