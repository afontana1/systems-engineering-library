Risk, issue, opportunity and baseline change as one control system

Conceptual explanation

Keep uncertainty distinct from realized problems

A risk is an uncertain future event with potential consequence; an issue is a condition already occurring that needs resolution; an opportunity is potential favorable uncertainty with a plausible action. A risk register should include cause, event, consequence, likelihood/impact logic, owner, response, trigger and residual exposure. When a risk materializes, transfer it to issue control while preserving the original record for learning.

Responses differ: avoid a risk by changing strategy; reduce likelihood or consequence through mitigation; transfer certain contractual/financial consequences where meaningful; accept with accountable authorization and monitored contingency. Transferring a financial liability does not transfer moral or technical responsibility for a public safety outcome. Opportunities should be managed with the same skepticism about probability and evidence as threats.

The integrated change loop

A change request should connect the technical baseline (requirements/interfaces/architecture), management baseline (WBS/schedule/budget) and assurance plan (verification/quality). Impact analysis precedes approval; after approval, update all relevant controlled artifacts and communicate the effective version. Informal workarounds are especially risky when a safety-critical interface changes. Make the change board responsible for coherent dispositions rather than simply recording 'approved'.

Corrective versus preventive action

An issue can require immediate containment, root-cause analysis, permanent correction and verification of effectiveness. Quality assurance checks the process and controls; quality control inspects or tests outputs. Passing an end-item test may not justify a process that hides systematic defects, while perfect paperwork does not guarantee system performance.

Register hygiene

Review stale entries, triggered conditions, duplicate risks and disconnected owners. A risk score alone is not a decision: explicit thresholds and exposure to objectives matter. A high-probability minor inconvenience may rank differently from a low-probability fatal hazard; critical risks require consequence-based escalation independent of arithmetic scoring.

Worked example: reason through the case

Example: Risk: supplier firmware may not support the agreed versioned API by integration date, delaying full-system test (probability uncertain; high schedule impact). Trigger: versioned interface test fails at supplier demonstration. Mitigation: contract tests and emulator early; contingency: retain prior supported firmware and restrict features. When the demonstration fails, log an issue and initiate change analysis for revised integration plan, cost, test scope and acceptance evidence.

Retrieval and transfer practice

  1. Classify: "supplier might deliver late" versus "supplier missed the contracted date yesterday."
  2. A change reduces purchase cost but removes an emergency-access feature. What must the board assess?
  3. Name difference between mitigation, contingency and issue correction.

A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: treating a risk register as a monthly color chart unconnected to decisions, money, schedule or engineering changes.
