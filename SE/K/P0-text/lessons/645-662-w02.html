Conceptual explanation

A lifecycle is a risk-reduction logic

A lifecycle model organizes decisions across concept, definition, design, implementation, integration, acceptance, operation, sustainment and retirement. It is not a command to finish all thinking about requirements before discussing tests. Technical processes recur at increasing detail as understanding matures. In a V-shaped representation, decomposition and definition on the left prepare the evidence and integration paths on the right; it is a traceability metaphor, not a rule that no iteration is allowed.

The key deliverable at each transition is not a ceremonial slide deck. It is an explicit answer to: what uncertainties have been reduced enough to authorize the next investment? Before implementation, interfaces may need stability. Before operations, operational hazards, people, procedures, maintenance and acceptance must be credible. A review should judge the actual evidence for its purpose rather than demand an identical template from every project.

What tailoring actually means

Tailoring changes process depth, timing, format, staffing and review cadence according to mission consequences and project uncertainty. It does not mean skipping failure-prone activities because they are inconvenient. For a low-risk internal information display, a lightweight requirements table and test checklist may be enough. For an autonomous vehicle operating near pedestrians, traceability, safety analysis, independent review and off-nominal operational testing cannot be justified away by a short calendar.

Use a two-axis classification: consequence of failure and uncertainty of technology/integration. High-consequence, high-uncertainty work deserves early prototypes, formal checkpoints, independent challenge and operational constraints. Low-uncertainty, low-consequence work may permit direct implementation and rapid feedback. Tailoring rationale should state what control remains when a traditional document or review is reduced.

Review dispositions

Three useful dispositions are proceed, proceed with tracked conditions, and do not proceed. A conditional approval needs action owner, due date, closure evidence and authority to confirm closure. Important: a risk may be accepted by an authorized decision maker; an unsatisfied mandatory requirement cannot simply be labeled 'accepted risk' to manufacture compliance.

Worked example: reason through the case

Example: The pilot must operate near a newly redesigned crossing. A concept review finds that the crossing geometry is unverified. The team may be allowed to proceed with virtual interface modeling and depot testing, while road trials remain gated by verified geometry, pedestrian hazard analysis and a signed permit. This is targeted conditional approval, not a false claim that the design is ready for public operation.

A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: equating a review date with readiness. Evidence-based criteria must control whether approval is given.
