Conceptual explanation

Every estimate is a conditional model

An estimate is an inference from defined scope, quantities, productivity, rates and risk conditions. It is not a promise that numbers are exact. A basis of estimate records each major cost element, source of data, calculation method, exclusions, confidence, escalation assumptions and date. Common approaches are analogous (compare similar work), parametric (use calibrated unit relationships), bottom-up (aggregate detailed work), and expert judgment (preferably structured and challenged). Cross-check one method against another when practical.

Direct labor cost is commonly hours × loaded labor rate. Total planning cost also includes materials, subcontracts, tooling, travel, facilities, assurance and other applicable cost categories. Price to a customer is not identical to underlying cost; contract terms, profit or fee, indirect cost rules, and risk allocation matter. Never silently mix labor hours, calendar duration, dollars in different price years or fully burdened and unburdened rates.

Three-point reasoning

For an uncertain activity, elicit optimistic, most likely and pessimistic duration or effort estimates with coherent scenarios. A familiar illustrative PERT mean is (O + 4M + P)/6; it is a heuristic approximation, not a substitute for a probability model or data. A larger estimate range signals exposure but does not by itself specify the distribution's tail. Correlated uncertainties—one supplier delay affecting many packages—are especially dangerous if simply summed as independent buffers.

Reserves and budget logic

A cost baseline represents authorized planned work under stated conventions. Contingency for identified uncertainty is ordinarily integrated into the approved project plan according to the organization's rules; management reserve is held outside the performance measurement baseline for controlled use against unforeseen in-scope work, depending on the governing system. Neither reserve is an excuse to hide known mandatory scope. Explicitly distinguish baseline, funds authority, current forecast and total commitment.

Estimate updates

As prototype results, bids or performance data arrive, update the forecast and retain a record of why. Do not rewrite the original baseline simply to eliminate an unfavorable variance. A trustworthy estimate preserves versioned assumptions so an independent reviewer can reproduce it.

Worked example: reason through the case

Worked example (fictional): 160 engineering hours at $130/hr = $20,800. Equipment = $18,000, software license = $2,500, external test = $6,000. Direct illustrative subtotal = $47,300. Add a separately documented 10% contingency ($4,730) for a specific in-scope test uncertainty → planned amount $52,030. Do not call a blanket 10% an objectively calculated risk exposure: justify it, and follow the project's accounting conventions for where reserve sits.

A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: quoting an exact dollar figure while its scope, rates and risk assumptions remain undocumented.
