Conceptual explanation

A credible management baseline is an integrated explanation

The final management case connects charter and governance → customer requirements and bid compliance → product-oriented WBS → authorized work packages → resource-loaded schedule → estimate and budget → measured progress and forecast → risk/change/quality control → customer acceptance. A missing arrow implies a hidden assumption, an unowned decision or an untraceable cost.

Distinguish evidence of planned executability from evidence of completed delivery. At proposal stage, a sound risk-adjusted plan supports a commitment but does not guarantee it. During execution, EV, technical measures, test evidence and supplier status support the current forecast. In operations, acceptance and service outcomes establish whether the project actually created value.

Defend the numbers as conditional

Explain why estimates are calibrated and where they are weak. State the model assumptions behind the critical path, reserves, EAC and acceptance dates. Demonstrate how a plausible disruption changes the decision. A thoughtful defense uses ranges and named triggers where uncertainty matters, yet still makes a recommendation backed by authority and evidence.

Ethical project control

Managers should not disguise unfavorable status by moving baseline dates or assigning unearned progress. The legitimate response to bad news is early, decision-ready escalation with choices and consequences. Preserve original records. A forecast that is unpleasant but reproducible protects the sponsor more than a green chart that collapses at acceptance.

Learn across technical and management disciplines

Systems engineering asks what coherent capability and evidence are needed? Project management asks how will we deliver that capability with controlled commitments? These are intertwined: a requirements change affects scope, cost, schedule and verification; a resource constraint affects what can actually be integrated and proven. This dependency is the intellectual link between EN.645.662 and EN.645.667.

Worked example: reason through the case

Example final defense: The pilot offers limited two-vehicle operation, with public deployment conditional on accessible boarding and emergency response tests. Cost baseline is $500k and current EAC is $560k based on supplier quotes and remaining integration work. The critical path depends on campus charging availability; a delay contingency has a named decision date and sponsor. The plan asks for approval of a controlled pilot stage, not an unsupported guarantee of all-weather autonomous service.

Retrieval and transfer practice

  1. State the minimum four management artifacts needed to defend a project date and budget.
  2. What is the ethical response if current evidence implies EAC exceeds BAC by 15%?
  3. Connect one systems-engineering change to management consequences.

A failure mode to recognize

Mistake: defending baseline dates and dollars as certainty rather than a coherent, monitored and revisable commitment.
