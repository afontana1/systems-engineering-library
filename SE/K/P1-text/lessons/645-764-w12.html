The question to answer

How do we make an evidence-based software release decision?

Conceptual tutorial

Integrate claims across the lifecycle

A release package should show revisioned requirements and allocations, architecture decisions, interface specifications, risk controls, verified test results, residual defects, deployment/rollback plans and owner-approved operating constraints. Each claim must be bounded by environment and configuration. A readiness review is a decision under uncertainty, not a ceremonial celebration of completion.

Assess new technologies by operational fit, maturity, support horizon, security, data interoperability, cost and reversibility, not novelty. A generative-AI assistant, edge platform or novel broker may solve a genuine problem, but introduce failure and assurance assumptions. Demand comparative experiments and a fallback option. Distinguish a technology prototype's promise from a release candidate's evidence.

Make the recommendation conditional when necessary

Use a structured final disposition: release, release with specifically enforced conditions, or do not release. Name who can accept each residual risk, which configuration was reviewed, and what new evidence would change the recommendation. Preserve a handoff package implementers and operators can actually reproduce.

Worked application: follow the reasoning

Decision scenario. Dispatch simulation passed 180 automated checks, but field connectivity for two stops remains untested and a critical fallback mode has no operator rehearsal. The defensible recommendation is no public operation yet; a controlled lab demonstration may proceed under different constraints. Readiness should be scoped to the evidence, not conflated with project optimism.

Practice before viewing solutions

  1. What belongs in a controlled release package?
  2. Why does promising technology still require alternatives analysis?
  3. Can passing many tests compensate for one critical untested failure mode?

<details> <summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

  1. What belongs in a controlled release package? Trace/baseline, architecture decisions, tests and environment, risk dispositions, operational plan, defects, approvals and rollback evidence.
  2. Why does promising technology still require alternatives analysis? Benefits, integration risks, support, security, interoperability, cost and reversibility require comparison.
  3. Can passing many tests compensate for one critical untested failure mode? No; consequence and required assurance determine whether the gap is a release blocker.

</details>

The misconception to avoid

Watch for: Volume of evidence is not the same as sufficiency of evidence for the operational decision.

Applied exercise and portfolio handoff
