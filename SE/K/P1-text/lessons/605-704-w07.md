## The question to answer
> How do domain concepts become maintainable software design elements?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Design is a controlled change of abstraction
A domain `Trip` does not automatically become one class with every process attached. Design assigns responsibilities among entities, domain services, application coordinators, adapters and infrastructure. High cohesion means a component has a focused reason to change; low coupling means dependents know only the interfaces they need. Neither slogan should replace examination of concrete change scenarios.

Apply information-expert reasoning: place a decision near the information required to make it, unless a transaction boundary or authorization rule demands central orchestration. Distinguish *domain logic* from *integration plumbing*. The latter handles network retries, persistence and external services without redefining the domain language.

### Test change pressure
Consider introducing a second notification provider. If business policy classes must be edited, provider concerns probably leaked inward. Consider adding accessibility rules. If database migration code owns those rules, the persistence boundary is wrong. Use dependency direction intentionally and record tradeoffs rather than using layers by habit.

## Worked application: follow the reasoning

**Responsibility split.** `TripEligibilityPolicy` evaluates rider and stop constraints; `AssignmentService` coordinates a transaction; `AssignmentRepository` stores state through an interface; `MessageAdapter` translates outbound events. Tests can replace the repository and adapter with fakes while exercising policy independently.

## Practice before viewing solutions

1. What signals low cohesion?
2. When does a domain service make sense?
3. Why should business policy not know a cloud vendor SDK?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

1. **What signals low cohesion?** Unrelated change reasons, methods operating on unrelated data, and heavy cross-feature edits.
2. **When does a domain service make sense?** When domain behavior legitimately spans multiple entities without a natural single owner.
3. **Why should business policy not know a cloud vendor SDK?** It creates unnecessary coupling, weak testability and costly provider changes.

</details>

## The misconception to avoid

**Watch for:** Creating a layer for every noun can be just as harmful as putting everything in one controller.
