Model quality, queries, reports, change impact, governance, and SysML transition

Companion chapter: EN.645.631, Week 11 · Phase 1 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original course: Introduction to Model Based Systems Engineering — find Week 11 for assignment, assessment criteria and complete reading plan.
Concepts: model quality, query, change impact, v1/v2 transition
Use of case: Synthetic Autonomous Campus Shuttle case; all values and designs are educational, not field safety guidance.

The question to answer

How can we know whether a repository is trustworthy enough to reuse?

Conceptual tutorial

Model quality has multiple dimensions

A model can be syntactically valid and still be false, incomplete or internally inconsistent. Evaluate (1) semantic accuracy; (2) completeness for stated decisions; (3) consistency among views; (4) trace and evidence integrity; (5) configuration and provenance; and (6) maintainability. A model-quality query can find duplicate IDs, disconnected requirements, untyped interfaces, stale attributes and orphan tests. It cannot decide whether a safety assumption is reasonable without expertise.

Automated rules should return review queues, not unquestionable verdicts. A requirement may intentionally lack a test while awaiting approved disposition; the exception still needs ownership and a due date. Use issue severity rather than hiding defects in an overall percentage.

Governance enables change

When a speed limit changes, a change-impact report should identify affected functions, parametrics, allocations, interface data, tests and approved baselines. Log the rationale and reviewer. Ensure derived tables and exports carry revision metadata so a stale document cannot impersonate a current source of truth.

Moving from SysML v1 to v2 requires semantic assessment of what was actually represented, not merely diagram export. A pilot migration can expose missing mapping for profiles, custom tools and linked external evidence.

Worked application: follow the reasoning

Quality dashboard. 60 requirements: 3 missing owners, 2 without source, 4 tests tied to retired versions, and 1 duplicate ID. Simply saying '90% complete' conceals different remediations. The critical finding is the retired evidence on emergency-stop requirements; prioritize by consequence, not count.

Practice before viewing solutions

  1. What quality checks cannot a parser perform?
  2. Why should model checks produce review queues?
  3. What is risky about assuming automatic v1-to-v2 equivalence?

<details> <summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

  1. What quality checks cannot a parser perform? It cannot validate stakeholder meaning, physical feasibility or adequacy of safety assumptions on its own.
  2. Why should model checks produce review queues? Because legitimate exceptions exist and risk-based human judgment is needed to close or accept them.
  3. What is risky about assuming automatic v1-to-v2 equivalence? Language semantics, profiles, views and tool mappings can differ; conversion may lose constraints and metadata.

</details>

The misconception to avoid

Watch for: Optimizing an aggregate model health score can hide severe local assurance gaps.

Applied exercise and portfolio handoff
