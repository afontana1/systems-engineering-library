Manage delivery, operations, maintenance, evolution, and incidents

Companion chapter: EN.645.764, Week 11 · Phase 1 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)

Concepts: observability, SLO, incident response, evolution
Use of case: Synthetic Autonomous Campus Shuttle case; all values and designs are educational, not field safety guidance.

The question to answer

What changes when someone must operate the software every day?

Conceptual tutorial

Production is a different test environment

Monitoring must connect service outcomes to measurable signals. Distinguish metrics (rates/latencies), logs (events) and traces (cross-service paths). An SLO states a service-level objective over a defined population/window; an error budget translates agreed service reliability into a decision mechanism. Alerts should reflect user consequence rather than every noisy internal event.

Operational readiness covers deployment/rollback, configuration, secrets, support on-call, data restoration, incident roles, privacy retention and security response. A runbook gives actionable diagnosis and mitigation with escalation authority. Preserve distributed correlation IDs and operating assumptions. If a failure requires manual intervention, test whether the human workflow works in a rehearsal.

Learn from incidents without rewriting history

An incident review separates timeline, observed impacts, contributing conditions and corrective actions. Do not treat individual blame as root-cause analysis. Maintenance and upgrade plans should identify interface compatibility, database migration strategy and retirement of obsolete evidence. A model baseline must reflect the operating release.

Worked application: follow the reasoning

Incident exercise. Rider ETA updates become stale after a deployment. Metrics detect a queue lag, traces show notification consumers failing on a newly added field, logs identify API v2/v3 mismatch. The runbook rolls back the consumer, replays buffered events and opens a contract-test corrective action. Recovery metrics and status communication become part of the evidence package.

Practice before viewing solutions

  1. What is an actionable SLO?
  2. Why do you need correlation IDs?
  3. What makes a good post-incident corrective action?

<details> <summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

  1. What is an actionable SLO? A clear service measure, population, threshold and time window tied to user impact.
  2. Why do you need correlation IDs? They associate related events across asynchronous or distributed boundaries.
  3. What makes a good post-incident corrective action? Named owner, due date, change to system/process, and observable evidence that recurrence is less likely.

</details>

The misconception to avoid

Watch for: Equating monitoring dashboards with operational readiness ignores people, rollback and recovery.
