Baseline and defend the integrated OOAD design

Companion chapter: EN.605.704, Week 12 · Phase 1 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original course: Object-Oriented Analysis and Design — find Week 12 for assignment, assessment criteria and complete reading plan.
Concepts: design baseline, change trace, design defense, implementability
Use of case: Synthetic Autonomous Campus Shuttle case; all values and designs are educational, not field safety guidance.

The question to answer

What makes a design package credible to future implementers?

Conceptual tutorial

A useful design is traceable and buildable

The completed package must let a reviewer follow a rider goal into requirements, use-case steps, domain concepts, static responsibilities, interaction sequences, state constraints, database structures and verification ideas. It must not require the original author's memory to explain terminology or failure behavior. Controlled IDs and a glossary are more valuable than diagram count alone.

Defend why a given object owns a rule, why an interface exists, what happens when two operations race and how a future change would propagate. Mark what is domain truth, design choice or unresolved risk. A design is review-ready if multiple engineers could implement similar externally observable behavior without inventing contradictory semantics.

Demonstrate, do not merely describe

Walk a nominal trip, a canceled/reassigned trip and a capacity conflict. Compare the proposed design to core requirements. Apply a surprise change request and show edits to models and tests. Preserve revisioned source diagrams (not only PNGs), modeling assumptions, constraints, review issues, and rationale in the portfolio.

Worked application: follow the reasoning

Defense prompt. A campus now permits advance group reservations. The design review explores whether TripRequest cardinality, rider identity, selection strategy, persistence schema and notification events must change. The defender identifies affected artifacts before writing code and proposes transition tests. That is a design capability, not a notation exam.

Practice before viewing solutions

  1. What information should be revisioned besides diagrams?
  2. What should a concurrency scenario demonstrate?
  3. How do you know the domain vocabulary is coherent?

<details> <summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

  1. What information should be revisioned besides diagrams? Assumptions, source requirements, interface contracts, design decisions, model source, tests, known defects and change log.
  2. What should a concurrency scenario demonstrate? That critical invariants survive interleavings, retries or stale commands.
  3. How do you know the domain vocabulary is coherent? Terms have consistent definitions across requirements, use cases, classes, events and tests.

</details>

The misconception to avoid

Watch for: A final class diagram without behavioral or persistence evidence is not an integrated software design.

Applied exercise and portfolio handoff

Do: Give a 15-minute design defense with three scenarios and a change-impact table. Treat this as preparation for the fuller deliverables and rubric in the original Week 12 guide; the companion is not a substitute for those graded artifacts.
