# Discover candidate classes and build the static analysis model

**Companion chapter:** EN.605.704, Week 04 · Phase 1 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original course:** [Object-Oriented Analysis and Design](../../../C/P1/EN605704.md) — find **Week 4** for assignment, assessment criteria and complete reading plan.  
**Concepts:** entity, value object, association, multiplicity  
**Use of case:** Synthetic Autonomous Campus Shuttle case; all values and designs are educational, not field safety guidance.

## The question to answer
> How do we discover the stable concepts beneath software behavior?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Classes are not just nouns
Candidate discovery uses scenarios, events, rules, lifecycle history and roles. `Trip` may have durable identity and change states; `GeoCoordinate` is usually a value defined by latitude/longitude rather than identity; `VehicleAvailabilityPolicy` encapsulates a rule; `AssignmentChanged` represents an event worth recording. The word 'manager' rarely identifies a domain concept unless stakeholders actually mean something specific by it.

Use associations to express meaning, then check multiplicity and direction. A trip may have zero or one current assignment but many assignment-history records. Writing `Trip 1 → 1 Vehicle` incorrectly asserts assignment exists before scheduling and cannot change. Derived attributes should be marked derived rather than duplicated as independent truth.

### Distinguish analysis and design
Do not insert repositories, JSON schemas and ORM annotations into the conceptual diagram. These belong to design and persistence models. Before finalizing the model, choose a real scenario and walk each association: can a canceled trip have an assignment? Can one vehicle serve overlapping assignments? Are descriptions and role names unambiguous?

## Worked application: follow the reasoning

**Model evolution.** Initially `TripRequest` linked directly to `Vehicle`. A reassignment scenario shows why the link lacks history. Introduce `Assignment` with identity, start/end time, status and policy decision; link one trip to zero or more historical assignments, with at most one current assignment. This is domain discovery driven by behavior, not a class-count target.

## Practice before viewing solutions

1. Why is `Money` often modeled as a value object?
2. What mistake follows from assuming every trip always has a vehicle?
3. What can an assignment history entity represent?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why is `Money` often modeled as a value object?** Its meaning comes from amount/currency and invariants; equal values are interchangeable irrespective of identity.
2. **What mistake follows from assuming every trip always has a vehicle?** It blocks pending, failed or canceled requests and hides lifecycle transitions.
3. **What can an assignment history entity represent?** Reassignment events, accountability, temporal constraints and audit provenance.

</details>

## The misconception to avoid

**Watch for:** Blind noun extraction leads to anemic models and UI/database leakage.
