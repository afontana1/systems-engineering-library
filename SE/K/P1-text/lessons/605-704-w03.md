# Capture functional requirements with use cases and scenarios

## The question to answer
> How do use cases describe value rather than UI click sequences?

## Conceptual tutorial

### A use case is a goal with a contract
A use case describes a primary actor's goal, the system's responsibility and observable outcome. It is not a screen script or a list of HTTP endpoints. Begin with preconditions and trigger, then write numbered interactions in ordinary domain language: the rider requests pickup, the platform checks eligibility, the platform proposes an assignment, the rider receives status. State the success guarantee and minimal guarantee under failure.

Extensions matter more than decorative arrows. At any step a reservation may expire, a vehicle may become unavailable, a rider may cancel or a request may violate policy. An extension references the exact main step and states the outcome: retry, reject, escalate, roll back or compensate. Avoid forcing rare exceptions into the main flow unless they are central to the actor goal.

### Use cases guide, not replace, design
From each step, extract candidate domain concepts, system operations and persistent events. Later sequence diagrams realize the contract and may expose missing responsibilities. A good use-case review challenges missing stakeholders, inconsistent guarantees and non-observable postconditions.

## Worked application: follow the reasoning

**Use case: Request Accessible Trip.** Precondition: rider permitted to book. Main flow: request origin/destination and accessibility need → validate stop accessibility → find eligible vehicle → reserve assignment → show confirmation. Extension at reserve: vehicle unavailable → retry selection or communicate inability to fulfill. This reveals `AccessibilityRequirement`, `VehicleCapability` and `AssignmentReservation` as concepts.

## Practice before viewing solutions

1. Why is “click Submit” not a domain-level use-case step?
2. What is a minimal guarantee?
3. How do exception branches support class discovery?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why is “click Submit” not a domain-level use-case step?** It describes UI mechanics rather than actor-system goal and business meaning.
2. **What is a minimal guarantee?** An obligation that must hold even if the use case fails, such as no double booking and clear failure notice.
3. **How do exception branches support class discovery?** They expose states, events, rules and responsibilities absent from nominal stories.

## The misconception to avoid

**Watch for:** Confusing CRUD endpoints with user goals creates fragmented behavior and weak outcome testing.
