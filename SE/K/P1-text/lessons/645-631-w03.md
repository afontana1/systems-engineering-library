# Stakeholders, concerns, needs, context, ConOps, and operational scenarios

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.631, Week 03 · Phase 1 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)

**Concepts:** stakeholder concern, operational scenario, ConOps, context model  
**Use of case:** Synthetic Autonomous Campus Shuttle case; all values and designs are educational, not field safety guidance.

## The question to answer
> How do operational stories constrain what belongs in a technical model?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Translate voices into scenarios
Stakeholders express concerns, not always test-ready requirements. Riders may want predictable arrivals, safety staff need emergency access, and campus IT wants manageable identities. A concern becomes analyzable through an operational scenario describing actors, trigger, normal flow, abnormal variations, environment, and observable outcome. A **Concept of Operations** describes how the service is expected to be used, governed and sustained; it is broader than a list of features.

Construct one scene at a time. Define the system boundary and the environment. Then enumerate information or material exchanges with the dispatcher, vehicle, passenger, operator and external services. An external system is neither 'inside the model' nor 'irrelevant'—it can be outside the system-of-interest boundary while still represented as a dependency.

### Scenarios reveal hidden requirements
A request to 'show ETA' seems simple until the network drops, two riders share a stop, a shuttle is taken out of service or an ETA becomes stale. Such variations reveal freshness requirements, confidence intervals, fallback messages, responsibility for status updates and operator interventions. Model each variation as a traceable branch, not as prose buried in a meeting note.

Validate the operational story with someone who performs or owns the real workflow. A technically consistent model can still represent the wrong service.

## Worked application: follow the reasoning

**Scenario.** Trigger: passenger requests accessible pickup. Normal sequence: request → confirm boarding location → dispatch assignment → vehicle acknowledgement → passenger update. Exception: assigned vehicle becomes unavailable. The model identifies a reassignment function, a timeout, an accessible fallback and a notification responsibility. The exception exposes cross-organizational behavior absent from the happy path.

## Practice before viewing solutions

1. Why are needs and technical requirements not interchangeable?
2. What minimum fields make a scenario reviewable?
3. What does a network outage teach about scope?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show worked answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why are needs and technical requirements not interchangeable?** Needs express outcomes and concerns; technical requirements allocate measurable obligations to a specific system or element.
2. **What minimum fields make a scenario reviewable?** Actors, preconditions, trigger, steps, alternative flows, environment, measurable result and linked concerns.
3. **What does a network outage teach about scope?** Even if the network is external, its failure behavior changes system obligations and needs an interface and fallback scenario.

</details>

## The misconception to avoid

**Watch for:** Writing only a nominal workflow hides the conditions where interfaces become risky.
