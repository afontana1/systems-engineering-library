# Conduct the Final Design & Integration Review and hand off to T&E

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.768 · Week 12 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original study guide:** [System Design & Integration](../../../C/P2/EN645768.md) — consult **Week 12** for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.  
**Concepts:** final design baseline, design rationale, integration handoff, test readiness  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> What does the Test & Evaluation team need beyond a set of drawings and tests?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Hand off a controlled design and its open questions
The package should contain approved requirements, as-designed architecture, ICDs, component configuration, technical budgets, hazard/risk analyses, supplier constraints, prototype evidence, integration status, support provisions, and review decisions. Each evidence claim must identify which build and environment it describes. Open actions need owners and time-bound closure conditions. A good handoff makes unresolved tradeoffs visible instead of translating them into vague acceptance promises.

Agree on test articles and their representativeness, environments, instrumentation, fault injection boundaries, calibration, data retention, and expected acceptance authority. The T&E team should challenge untestable design decisions, missing operational modes and optimistic claims about demonstrated performance. They are independent evidence producers, not a ceremonial final gate.

### Use an explicit design-to-evidence argument
Trace each high-impact requirement through design implementation and planned verification, and each mission outcome through operational validation. State what could invalidate the argument. For a conditional design continuation, specify the corrective design work and exact evidence expected; do not advance an unsafe or unreviewed claim solely to maintain schedule.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Final package.** A design baseline `DES-1.3` includes `IF-03` revision B, test article `LAB-03`, security hazard `HZ-08`, and provisional accessible-boarding assumption `ASM-22`. The receiving T&E lead flags that `LAB-03` cannot exercise the interface's network-partition failure behavior. Design must provide representative fault injection or explicitly limit the scope of the claim; otherwise the planned test cannot close the associated requirement.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. Why must a test article have a pedigree?
2. What is an honest way to hand off an unresolved assumption?
3. When should the T&E team challenge the design?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why must a test article have a pedigree?** To know which configuration, environment and behaviors its results can legitimately represent.
2. **What is an honest way to hand off an unresolved assumption?** Label status, basis, affected claims, owner, test/closure condition and decision authority.
3. **When should the T&E team challenge the design?** As early as testability or representativeness can change architecture, interfaces or acceptance evidence.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Submitting an unresolved design action as a "test issue" without a resolution owner merely relocates risk.
