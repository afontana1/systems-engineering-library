## The engineering question

> How do you close a system specification without burying ambiguity in a document?

## Conceptual tutorial

### A specification is a set of contractual engineering claims
Requirements need clearly declared subjects, conditions, observable behavior, thresholds and verification methods. Where a requirement includes multiple obligations, split it or supply a decomposition that independently traces each testable clause. Define terms, units and operational modes. A statement such as "rapidly updates passengers" fails because "rapidly" has no numerical test and the update trigger is unclear.

Derived requirements may come from physics, interface constraints, safety analysis or design allocation. State their rationale and parent source, distinguishing them from discretionary preferences. Document assumptions about environment and inputs. Check for conflicts among performance, accessibility, cybersecurity and affordability. A requirements review is not satisfied by grammar: feasibility and completeness need architecture and operational scrutiny.

### Manage consistency and changes
A baseline is an approved, versioned reference against which change is assessed. Every change needs consequence analysis: impacted functions, components, interfaces, tests, plans and risks. Avoid retroactively overwriting old requirements and making earlier evidence appear to apply to the new target. Record proposed, approved and implemented status separately.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Ambiguity repair.** "The service shall notify riders quickly" becomes "For an accepted request during nominal connectivity, the dispatch service shall issue a status event within 900 ms of receipt, measured at interface IF-03, for at least 99% of requests under workload WL-01." The 900 ms and percentile are illustrative and require stakeholder negotiation; changing them requires updated processing, communications and test budgets.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. What makes a requirement objectively verifiable?
2. Why must a derived requirement identify its rationale?
3. Can evidence from a previous specification version prove a changed threshold?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **What makes a requirement objectively verifiable?** An observable obligation, well-defined conditions, reference point, method and measurable acceptance criterion.
2. **Why must a derived requirement identify its rationale?** So reviewers can challenge its necessity and trace consequences if parent assumptions change.
3. **Can evidence from a previous specification version prove a changed threshold?** Not automatically; applicability needs explicit assessment and possibly retesting.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Treating a proposed threshold as stakeholder-approved without evidence of agreement creates false compliance authority.
