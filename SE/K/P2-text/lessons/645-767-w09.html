Objectives, value functions, and multiattribute evaluation

Concepts: objectives hierarchy, value function, trade weights, Pareto dominance
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do you compare concepts without disguising preferences as mathematics?

Conceptual tutorial

Values are judgments, not measurements alone

An objectives hierarchy connects top-level mission outcomes to measurable attributes, with nonredundant branches and clear direction of improvement. Transform raw quantities to a common 0–1 value scale only when the anchors represent meaningful preference differences. For cost, a decreasing linear scale may suffice over a narrow justified range; for safety or access, a nonlinear threshold may be more appropriate.

A weighted additive score V = Σ wᵢ vᵢ(xᵢ) is meaningful under assumptions including appropriate scaling and additive preference independence. Weights are not percentages of attention or raw importance ratings. They represent tradeoff ratios between defined swings from worst to best attribute levels. Use stakeholder-informed swing weighting and publish sensitivity to changing weights.

Screen dominance first

A concept is Pareto dominated if another is no worse on every considered attribute and strictly better on at least one. Dominated options usually need no preference weights to exclude them, assuming no hidden constraints. For remaining alternatives, compare uncertainty bands, different stakeholder perspectives and possible regret. Keep mandatory requirements outside the compensating value model.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Transparent value calculation. Two feasible concepts have normalized values for availability and affordability: A (0.9, 0.4), B (0.7, 0.8). With weights (0.6, 0.4), A scores 0.70 and B scores 0.74. B wins narrowly. If availability weight changes to 0.7 (affordability 0.3), A scores 0.75 and B 0.73, reversing the choice. The small difference exposes preference sensitivity; it is not a measurement of engineering certainty.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What does a swing weight compare?
  2. When is an alternative Pareto dominated?
  3. Why can raw milliseconds not be added directly to dollars?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

  1. What does a swing weight compare? Preference for a defined change from one anchored attribute level to another.
  2. When is an alternative Pareto dominated? When another option is at least as good on every considered attribute and better on one.
  3. Why can raw milliseconds not be added directly to dollars? Different units and non-equivalent value scales make the sum meaningless without a preference model.

</details>

Critical misconception

Watch for: Declaring a close weighted score a "scientific winner" omits weighting uncertainty and stakeholder disagreement.
