# Generate physical concept families with morphological and set-based methods

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.767 · Week 07 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original study guide:** [System Conceptual Design](../../../C/P2/EN645767.md) — consult **Week 7** for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.  
**Concepts:** morphological matrix, set-based design, feasibility screening, concept diversity  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> How can you explore distinct physical concepts without converging on the first feasible idea?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Separate solution dimensions before combining them
Morphological analysis lists independent or weakly dependent design dimensions, such as transit mode (fixed-loop shuttle, demand-responsive shuttle, escorted walking service), dispatch method (scheduled, reservation, on-demand), and accessibility provision (vehicle adaptation, specialized service, hybrid). Combine options to produce candidate families, then prune only with documented hard constraints. A matrix of 3 × 3 × 2 options yields 18 raw combinations, not 18 credible system concepts.

Set-based design preserves a feasible region while evidence is collected. Instead of choosing one vehicle immediately, keep alternative families with ranges of acceptable speed, capacity, energy, labor and capital cost. Eliminate options only when requirements, physics, policy or data justify it. Diversity matters: three nearly identical vans are not equivalent to three distinct service architectures.

### Avoid fake precision in early screens
Use coarse bounds and pass/fail gates for safety and mission feasibility before fine weighted scoring. Mark uncertainty ranges and dependencies. For example, a high-capacity concept with no accessible boarding may fail a mandatory constraint regardless of its aggregate score. Record why each discarded concept was removed, what evidence supports the reason, and what could cause reconsideration.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Concept families.** Dimensions: vehicle `small / medium / mixed`, service `fixed / on-demand`, staffing `attendant / remote support`. The naïve set has 12 combinations. A policy requiring an onboard attendant rules out six remote-support combinations. A second restriction that small vehicles cannot meet a target boarding capacity may rule out more, but only after the required capacity and service context are established.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. What is the purpose of a morphological matrix?
2. Why must mandatory constraints precede weighted scores?
3. Why can an 18-cell matrix mislead?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is the purpose of a morphological matrix?** It systematically explores combinations of solution principles before premature commitment.
2. **Why must mandatory constraints precede weighted scores?** A high score cannot compensate for violating a genuine nonnegotiable safety or operational condition.
3. **Why can an 18-cell matrix mislead?** Some combinations are incompatible, duplicates, or unsupported by feasibility evidence.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Scoring every imagined combination to three decimal places creates certainty the evidence does not support.
