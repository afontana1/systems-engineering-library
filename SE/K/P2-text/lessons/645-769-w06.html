Write formal qualification procedures and conduct the Test Readiness Review

Companion chapter: EN.645.769 · Week 06 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)

Concepts: formal test procedure, readiness criteria, TRR, safety stop
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

What makes a formal qualification test repeatable, auditable and safe?

Conceptual tutorial

A formal procedure encodes a controlled claim

A formal test procedure needs objective, applicable requirement revision, article and test setup, environmental bounds, calibrated instrumentation, preconditions, detailed sequence, expected observations, data artifacts, pass/fail criteria, stop conditions, anomaly handling and signoff roles. Avoid instructions such as "verify good performance" without a precise measurement or threshold. Design procedural independence: someone other than its author should be able to execute and interpret it.

The Test Readiness Review (TRR) verifies the test is ready to produce trustworthy evidence under controlled risk. It examines completeness of requirements mapping, hardware/software configuration, environment, personnel training, calibration, test data, safety authorizations, dry runs and discrepancy procedures. It can approve, approve conditionally with controlled exclusions, or defer execution.

Do not turn readiness into a ritual

A signature on an incomplete checklist cannot fix an uncalibrated instrument or an unapproved hazardous action. Build stop-work authority into procedures and keep the record of deviations. A revised procedure needs revision control and an explicit decision about whether earlier evidence remains usable.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Procedure fragment. For a synthetic messaging-load test: precondition build D-21, workload WL-01, synchronized logs; stimulate 1,000 request events in the defined profile; archive raw ingress/egress records; compute eligible-response distribution; compare to declared acceptance rule. Include abort if timestamp drift exceeds the instrument budget or test-harness overload invalidates the nominal condition.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What makes a test pass/fail criterion defensible?
  2. Can a TRR signoff excuse absent safety authorization?
  3. Why is an automated script not the whole test procedure?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

  1. What makes a test pass/fail criterion defensible? Controlled baseline, observable quantity, units, threshold, defined conditions and approved decision rule.
  2. Can a TRR signoff excuse absent safety authorization? No. Missing authorization is an execution blocker in the relevant domain.
  3. Why is an automated script not the whole test procedure? It does not alone specify article pedigree, calibration, risk controls, roles, data governance or anomaly handling.

</details>

Critical misconception

Watch for: Defining acceptance limits after inspecting results invites post hoc confirmation bias.
