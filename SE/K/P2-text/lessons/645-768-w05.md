# Use prototypes and experiments to retire technology and design risk

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.768 · Week 05 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)

**Concepts:** technology readiness, prototype hypothesis, risk retirement, experiment control  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> How can a prototype genuinely retire a technical risk instead of merely impressing reviewers?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Prototypes should target decisions
A demonstrator may establish a behavior at small scale but fail to represent the operating environment. Start with a risk statement, its causal mechanism, the unknown parameter, and an experimental question. Choose prototype fidelity to represent the feature that matters: a software timing mock may probe the algorithm but not real network jitter; a bench mechanical fixture may measure boarding ergonomics but not crowd behavior.

Plan control variables, response measurement, acceptance threshold, adverse conditions and repeatability. Identify which design choice the result can validate, reject or narrow. Treat untested modes and extrapolation separately. A prototype can *reduce* uncertainty without eliminating it, especially where environmental, human or safety-critical interactions remain outside the experimental envelope.

### Record evidence pedigree
Document prototype configuration, calibration or instrument validity, software revision, test script, anomalies and raw data location. Peer review must be able to distinguish observation from interpretation. A slide labeled "successful prototype" without setup or failure boundaries is weak evidence.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Boarding risk experiment.** The concept assumes an average accessible boarding time below 90 seconds. A supervised, non-operational mock boarding station yields durations `85, 92, 100, 87, 96` seconds; average 92 seconds. The mean is over the assumed value, but five trials cannot establish operational distributions, human variability or safety. Update the assumption, improve the mock, and plan a larger study with relevant users and conditions.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. Why may a high-fidelity prototype still fail to retire a risk?
2. What should an experiment state before any data collection?
3. What inference can five mock-up trials support?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why may a high-fidelity prototype still fail to retire a risk?** It may omit the failure mechanism, environment, configuration or user population that causes the risk.
2. **What should an experiment state before any data collection?** Hypothesis, controlled factors, metrics, acceptance/decision rule, configuration and limitations.
3. **What inference can five mock-up trials support?** A preliminary measured sample and evidence to update assumptions, not a population guarantee.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Equating repeated success in a narrow test setup with safe operating authorization is unjustified.
