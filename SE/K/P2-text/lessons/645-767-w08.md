# Resource, affordability, schedule, feasibility, and optimization models

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.767 · Week 08 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original study guide:** [System Conceptual Design](../../../C/P2/EN645767.md) — consult **Week 8** for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.  
**Concepts:** resource budget, lifecycle cost, critical path, feasibility model, optimization constraints  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> How do affordability, schedule and feasibility constrain an attractive design?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Model the whole commitment
Concept affordability includes capital, staffing, energy, maintenance, training, spares, software support, infrastructure and retirement. A cheap acquisition can carry expensive recurring operations. Convert all alternatives to a common service horizon, price basis and usage assumption. A simple undiscounted lifecycle cost is acquisition + sum of annual operating costs + disposal; present-value analysis applies discount factors when appropriate and requires an explicit rate.

Schedule feasibility is not a list of optimistic dates. Represent predecessor relationships, resource availability and integration dependencies; the longest-duration path through the dependency network determines the earliest completion under the assumed deterministic durations. Resource constraints can lengthen this path. Avoid equating percent complete with remaining engineering difficulty.

### Optimization is conditional on model validity
A solver can maximize weighted value or minimize cost subject to hard constraints (capacity, staffing, range, safety, budget). It cannot decide whether the chosen objective function captures mission value. Check parameter units, bounds and sensitivity before trusting the numerical optimum. Keep scenario uncertainty separate from deterministic solver output.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Cost comparison.** Candidate A costs $600k to acquire and $180k/year to operate; candidate B costs $900k and $120k/year. Over five years, ignoring discount and residual value, A costs $1.50m and B costs $1.50m. Equal modeled cost does not imply equal affordability: early capital limits, risk, availability and service outputs still differ. A high-cost concept may also fail annual appropriations even if lifecycle totals match.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. What cost categories are omitted by purchase price alone?
2. What makes a schedule critical path contingent?
3. Does a solver optimum establish mission suitability?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **What cost categories are omitted by purchase price alone?** Operating labor, infrastructure, support, maintenance, training, disposal and financing/discount assumptions.
2. **What makes a schedule critical path contingent?** Precedence links, duration assumptions, resource constraints and the modelled scope affect it.
3. **Does a solver optimum establish mission suitability?** No. The solver optimizes the supplied model and objectives, which may omit mission outcomes and risk.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Comparing alternatives at different operating horizons or with inconsistent cost bases invalidates rankings.
