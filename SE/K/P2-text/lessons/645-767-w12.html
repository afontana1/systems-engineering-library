Final concept baseline, oral defense, and handoff

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 12 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 12 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: concept baseline, configuration control, oral defense, design handoff
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

What should the next design team inherit so it can challenge and mature the concept?

Conceptual tutorial

The deliverable is a controlled argument

The selected concept is a set of coherent decisions and evidence: operational need, scenarios, requirements, functional architecture, candidate comparisons, resource models, decision rationale, risk and evidence gaps. Version each artifact and identify an authoritative source. An attractive presentation is not a baseline if the receiving team cannot recreate its calculations or identify unsupported constraints.

Write the handoff contract as three classes: (1) fixed decisions approved for design maturation, (2) provisional assumptions requiring confirmation, and (3) deferred decisions with triggers. Include the review record, model/input versions, requirements and interfaces at their current maturity, unresolved review actions and change-control route. A receiving design engineer should be able to reject an infeasible assumption without implicitly violating program authority.

Defend with counterfactual reasoning

The strongest oral defense explains why the preferred concept remains sensible under plausible changes—and when it ceases to be preferred. Prepare for questions about omitted stakeholders, alternatives, cost basis, performance margins and unresolved failures. Identify what Phase 2 Design & Integration must not treat as a settled fact.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Handoff example. CON-04 selects demand-responsive service over fixed route for the laboratory design baseline. ASM-22 assumes average boarding time under 90 seconds; EXP-06 requires a measured accessibility evaluation. R-14 demands rider status feedback, while IF-09 remains a candidate interface requiring implementation selection. Packaging all four separately prevents assumptions and sketches from masquerading as requirements.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Which three classes should a concept handoff distinguish?
  2. What makes a cost spreadsheet reproducible?
  3. What is the purpose of a counterfactual defense?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

  1. Which three classes should a concept handoff distinguish? Approved decisions, provisional assumptions, and deferred decisions with closure triggers.
  2. What makes a cost spreadsheet reproducible? Versioned inputs, units, formulas, source basis and calculation environment.
  3. What is the purpose of a counterfactual defense? To show which evidence or changed constraints would reverse or qualify a recommendation.

</details>

Critical misconception

Watch for: Treating the chosen concept as fully validated confuses conceptual feasibility with verified design performance.
