# Reopen the problem and audit the incumbent baseline

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.767 · Week 01 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original study guide:** [System Conceptual Design](../../../C/P2/EN645767.md) — consult **Week 1** for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.  
**Concepts:** problem framing, incumbent baseline, assumption register, decision provenance  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> What evidence justifies reopening a design rather than polishing the incumbent solution?

## Conceptual tutorial

### The incumbent is a hypothesis, not the problem definition
A baseline is a controlled description of what exists or has been approved, including decisions and the reasons for them. It should not silently become the definition of stakeholder need. Suppose the campus has already purchased two shuttle vehicles. The problem is still to deliver accessible, safe, affordable movement; the purchased vehicles are constraints and sunk-history evidence, not proof that a two-vehicle shuttle service is optimal.

Begin with three separations: **observed facts** (measured rider arrivals), **assumptions** (future demand growth), and **decisions** (retain a fixed route). Put each in a register with source, date, confidence, owner, and consequence if wrong. Recover baselines of service intent, approved requirements, architecture, resources, schedule, and unresolved issues. Examine changes against the authority that approved them.

### Reframe the mission as a falsifiable question
An operational problem statement should specify who is affected, where, in what conditions, and how success will be recognized. Write a neutral formulation: "reduce the fraction of eligible riders unable to reach a campus destination during service hours." Avoid choosing dispatch algorithms or particular vehicles in the problem sentence. A good audit produces an evidence gap list rather than a preferred solution, and a decision log distinguishing "unknown" from "known but unfavorable."

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Incumbent audit.** An existing plan predicts 140 boardings/day. Logs show 90, 110 and 100 boardings on three measured days. Their average is 100, but the three-day sample cannot establish semester-wide demand. Record `OBS-01` (measured days and method), `ASM-07` (representative demand), and `DEC-03` (fleet size derived under the original 140/day assumption). Treat a 40-boarding difference as a reason to examine the model, not as proof of overcapacity: peak queues, reliability and accessible service may still dominate.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. Why can purchased equipment not define the stakeholder problem?
2. What information should an assumption register carry?
3. Does an average of 100 trips over three days justify halving the fleet?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why can purchased equipment not define the stakeholder problem?** Equipment is an incumbent constraint or candidate asset; outcomes and mission context define the problem.
2. **What information should an assumption register carry?** Assumption, basis, owner, confidence, validity interval, test plan, and consequence if false.
3. **Does an average of 100 trips over three days justify halving the fleet?** No. Sampling and peak demand are unresolved, and fleet sizing depends on cycle time and service constraints.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Treating an approved design choice as an unchangeable stakeholder requirement creates circular justification.

## Transfer exercise / portfolio artifact

**Make:** Audit five incumbent claims. Mark each fact, assumption, or decision; identify who could falsify it, then rewrite the mission need without solution language. Compare your work against the full original **Week 1** assessment, not only this short companion exercise.
