# Conduct the final evidence review and make the operational-readiness recommendation

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.769 · Week 12 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)  
**Original study guide:** [System Test & Evaluation](../../../C/P2/EN645769.md) — consult **Week 12** for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.  
**Concepts:** readiness review, evidence argument, decision authority, conditional acceptance  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> How do you produce a readiness recommendation that remains honest about gaps?

## Conceptual tutorial

### A readiness decision is a structured argument
Organize the final evidence around claims: which requirements were verified for which configuration and environment, which stakeholder outcomes were validated, which hazards and discrepancies remain, and what restrictions apply. Evidence needs provenance, reproducible interpretation and correct baseline. A single green dashboard conceals differences between strong proof, limited observation and absent evidence.

Possible dispositions include proceed within a defined operational envelope, conditional proceed after explicit controls, rework and retest, or do not proceed. State authority and consequence for each. Critical unresolved safety evidence must not be hidden under a weighted summary score. Residual risk is a management decision informed by technical evidence; analysts should identify its magnitude and uncertainty without self-authorizing acceptance.

### Package for the next lifecycle phase
Deliver requirement-status matrix, executed results, discrepancy/corrective-action log, operational validation findings, open risks, configuration manifest, monitoring and support plan, and the final review minutes. Explicitly list what the tests **did not** establish, so operators and later programs do not broaden the evidence beyond its domain.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Final judgement.** The synthetic dispatch platform meets its laboratory interface contract under `WL-01`, but the controlled test did not exercise campus-wide network partitions and the user-validation panel excluded some accessibility scenarios. A defensible disposition is *restricted lab demonstration only*, with two named evidence requirements before any broader field trial. It is not a green light for autonomous physical service.

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. What is the meaning of an operational-envelope limitation?
2. Who owns residual-risk acceptance?
3. Why must a final report include untested conditions?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **What is the meaning of an operational-envelope limitation?** Evidence applies only to the configurations and conditions actually represented or justified by analysis.
2. **Who owns residual-risk acceptance?** The authorized governance or operational authority, informed by independent technical recommendations.
3. **Why must a final report include untested conditions?** Future users must not mistake unexamined scenarios for proven safe or compliant behavior.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** A numerical readiness score that compensates away an open critical hazard is not a valid safety argument.
