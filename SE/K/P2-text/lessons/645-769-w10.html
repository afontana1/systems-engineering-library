Analyze results, determine evidence sufficiency, and recommend remedial action

Companion chapter: EN.645.769 · Week 10 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Test & Evaluation — consult Week 10 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: statistical conclusion, evidence sufficiency, residual risk, remedial action
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

When is enough evidence enough, and how should inconclusive evidence be reported?

Conceptual tutorial

Evidence sufficiency is relative to claims and consequences

A dataset can establish an average well but say little about tail performance or rare hazards. Evaluate sample selection, uncertainty, measurement validity, configuration representativeness and the severity of being wrong. Compare the analysis method to the claim: an empirical p99 from a small sample is unstable; an independence-based reliability bound is fragile when trials are correlated. Distinguish pass, fail, and indeterminate due to inadequate evidence.

For observed performance, provide point estimates with uncertainty ranges or justified qualitative limits. Test criteria defined in advance should control formal outcomes. Where the data disagree with model predictions, inspect assumptions, instrument bias and configuration before proposing either design or test changes.

Remedial action is not always more testing

If a subsystem cannot meet a physical budget, additional repetitions will not fix it. Possible recommendations include design change, revised operational envelope, risk mitigation, better instrumented experiment, or stop. State who accepts residual risk and what evidence would justify changing the recommendation.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Marginal result. A small trial reports 98 successful status updates out of 100 eligible requests. Observed success is 98%, below a hypothetical 99% requirement, while sampling uncertainty remains substantial. It is neither evidence of meeting the 99% requirement nor a reason to retroactively redefine eligibility. Investigate the two failures and the adequacy of the sample plan; report the formal disposition according to preapproved criteria.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why does a passing observed mean not establish a tail requirement?
  2. What is the difference between failure and inconclusiveness?
  3. When is a design change more appropriate than repeat testing?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

  1. Why does a passing observed mean not establish a tail requirement? Tail behavior and variability cannot be inferred from mean alone.
  2. What is the difference between failure and inconclusiveness? Failure is supported contradiction of a criterion; inconclusive means evidence is insufficient or invalid for the claim.
  3. When is a design change more appropriate than repeat testing? When credible evidence shows structural inability to meet a requirement under the declared envelope.

</details>

Critical misconception

Watch for: Calling an absence of detectable failure "proof of compliance" is an argument from ignorance.
