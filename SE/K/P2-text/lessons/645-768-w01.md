# Receive the concept baseline and plan design maturation

**Companion chapter:** EN.645.768 · Week 01 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)

**Concepts:** baseline acceptance, technical planning, design maturity, requirements volatility  
**Case:** Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

## The engineering question

> How do you receive an imperfect concept baseline without inheriting its assumptions as proven facts?

## Conceptual tutorial

### Acceptance means reviewing, not rubber-stamping
A concept package may contain stakeholder outcomes, functional architecture, selected service concept, cost/schedule envelope and unresolved assumptions. The design team should inspect completeness, source authority, maturity and contradictions before assigning physical components. Use an acceptance matrix showing each incoming claim, its status (approved, provisional, open), owner, change mechanism and evidence required to mature it. Preserve original IDs so the concept-to-design chain remains auditable.

Design maturation translates a concept into buildable product definitions. Plan the order of specifications, allocations, interface control, prototyping, discipline analyses, integration build-up and review. Establish gate criteria at the beginning: what evidence must exist for a Preliminary Design Review (PDR), an Integration Readiness Review (IRR), and a final detailed design review? Map long-lead materials and specialized equipment to the schedule before declaring milestones realistic.

### Treat uncertainty as work
An assumption is not merely a line in a risk log. It should generate a work package with a method, expected data, acceptance criterion, person responsible and time limit. Separate open design choices from defective source artifacts. An interface marked "TBD" is a threat to concurrent engineering unless owners and decision dates are controlled.

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

**Receiving `CON-04`.** The concept assumes 90-second average accessible boarding and a 24-minute route cycle. The design team accepts the mission objective but flags both parameters as unverified. It creates a human-systems boarding prototype and a cycle-time discrete-event study before freezing vehicle capacity or scheduling assumptions. Review question: which requirements and budgets would be revised if the measured boarding mean were 130 seconds?

## Retrieval questions — attempt these first

1. Why is a receiving-team audit essential?
2. What turns a provisional assumption into a design work package?
3. What must a technical gate define before the review date?

<details><summary><strong>Reveal explained answers</strong></summary>

1. **Why is a receiving-team audit essential?** It prevents errors, unsupported constraints and stale decisions from propagating into costly design commitments.
2. **What turns a provisional assumption into a design work package?** An owner, resolving method, measurable closure criterion and due milestone.
3. **What must a technical gate define before the review date?** Evidence required, decision authority, allowed dispositions and follow-up process.

</details>

## Critical misconception

**Watch for:** Declaring a package "complete" because every spreadsheet cell is filled misses semantic gaps and evidence quality.
