Phase 2 concept glossary

Acceptance: Authorized judgment that a delivered system meets agreed delivery criteria within a specified scope.

Allocation: Assignment of function, requirement or budget responsibility to an element or role.

Assumption register: Versioned inventory of unverified premises, rationale, owner and retirement evidence.

Baseline: Approved controlled configuration used as a reference for changes and claims.

Calibration: Establishing instrument measurement characteristics relative to a reference under stated conditions.

Common-cause failure: A shared mechanism capable of defeating nominally redundant elements.

ConOps: Concept of Operations: intended actors, modes, scenarios, interfaces and operational context.

Concept Validation Review: Decision on the credibility and maturity of a concept and its open commitments, not proof of field compliance.

Configuration pedigree: Traceable description of the actual product, software, environment and tools used for an observation.

Critical test parameter: A measured quantity whose value materially affects a requirement, risk or readiness decision.

DOE: Design of Experiments, planned combinations of factors and measurements for inference.

Decision authority: Person or body authorized to select, accept, restrict or reject a proposed action.

Deviation: Approved or unapproved departure from test setup or procedure; disposition needs control.

Discrepancy: Observed departure from expectation requiring classification and investigation.

Evidence sufficiency: Adequacy of observation, coverage, accuracy and inference relative to a specific claim and consequence.

FAR: Functional Architecture Review: checks the functions, flows, timing and responsibilities for concept maturity.

Fidelity: Degree to which a model or test environment reproduces mechanisms relevant to a specified use.

HIL: Hardware-in-the-loop: physical target components exercised within a controlled simulated loop.

Hard constraint: Non-compensating feasibility or compliance condition that a candidate must satisfy.

ICD: Interface Control Document specifying exchanged items, semantics, timing, failure handling and ownership.

IRR: Integration Readiness Review assessing whether components, interfaces and facilities are prepared for integration.

Inherent availability: Simplified availability based on mean time between failures and mean time to repair.

Integration build-up: Ordered assembly and interface-check sequence with defined entry/exit and rollback.

MOE: Measure of Effectiveness: operational mission outcome measure.

MOP: Measure of Performance: technical system behavior that contributes to a mission outcome.

Margin: Declared difference between target or allocation and predicted or measured performance under stated assumptions.

Measurement uncertainty: Characterized doubt associated with a measurement result under specified conditions.

Morphological analysis: Structured exploration of combinations of design principles and solution dimensions.

Operational design domain: Conditions within which a system or autonomy capability is intended to operate.

PDR: Preliminary Design Review examining architecture, allocation, feasibility, maturity and remaining technical work.

Pareto dominance: A choice is dominated when another is no worse on all attributes and better on at least one.

Percentile: A quantile of a defined population or empirical sample, not interchangeable with a mean.

Prototype: A representation designed to answer defined questions; credibility depends on fidelity to those questions.

Qualification: Demonstration by approved method that a product satisfies specified qualification requirements.

Reliability: Probability of successful function over a stated time/demand and operating envelope.

Representativeness: Degree to which sample, test article and environment match the intended inference domain.

Residual risk: Risk remaining after controls, to be acknowledged by authorized governance.

SIL: Software-in-the-loop, software tested in a simulated environment without relying on full physical surroundings.

Sample frame: Definition of which units/events can enter a sample; exclusions can bias conclusions.

Set-based design: Delaying premature convergence by preserving multiple viable solution ranges.

Swing weighting: Preference-weight elicitation using the value of changes between anchored attribute levels.

T&E: Test and Evaluation: planned evidence collection, analysis and assessment for decisions.

TPM: Technical Performance Measure tracked as a design develops against a technical target.

TRR: Test Readiness Review: evidence gate for safe, reproducible and authorized execution.

Uncertainty propagation: How variation or unknowns in model inputs affect output predictions and decisions.

Validation: Evidence of fitness for stakeholder intended use within specified operational context.

Value function: Mapping an attribute to preference over anchored outcomes, not just numerical normalization.

Verification: Evidence that a realized system or element satisfies its specified requirements.

Worst-case versus nominal: Distinction between typical planning values and bounding stated operating scenarios.
