SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 01

Reopen the problem and audit the incumbent baseline

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 01 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 1 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: problem framing, incumbent baseline, assumption register, decision provenance
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

What evidence justifies reopening a design rather than polishing the incumbent solution?

Conceptual tutorial

The incumbent is a hypothesis, not the problem definition

A baseline is a controlled description of what exists or has been approved, including decisions and the reasons for them. It should not silently become the definition of stakeholder need. Suppose the campus has already purchased two shuttle vehicles. The problem is still to deliver accessible, safe, affordable movement; the purchased vehicles are constraints and sunk-history evidence, not proof that a two-vehicle shuttle service is optimal.

Begin with three separations: observed facts (measured rider arrivals), assumptions (future demand growth), and decisions (retain a fixed route). Put each in a register with source, date, confidence, owner, and consequence if wrong. Recover baselines of service intent, approved requirements, architecture, resources, schedule, and unresolved issues. Examine changes against the authority that approved them.

Reframe the mission as a falsifiable question

An operational problem statement should specify who is affected, where, in what conditions, and how success will be recognized. Write a neutral formulation: "reduce the fraction of eligible riders unable to reach a campus destination during service hours." Avoid choosing dispatch algorithms or particular vehicles in the problem sentence. A good audit produces an evidence gap list rather than a preferred solution, and a decision log distinguishing "unknown" from "known but unfavorable."

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Incumbent audit. An existing plan predicts 140 boardings/day. Logs show 90, 110 and 100 boardings on three measured days. Their average is 100, but the three-day sample cannot establish semester-wide demand. Record OBS-01 (measured days and method), ASM-07 (representative demand), and DEC-03 (fleet size derived under the original 140/day assumption). Treat a 40-boarding difference as a reason to examine the model, not as proof of overcapacity: peak queues, reliability and accessible service may still dominate.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why can purchased equipment not define the stakeholder problem?
  2. What information should an assumption register carry?
  3. Does an average of 100 trips over three days justify halving the fleet?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why can purchased equipment not define the stakeholder problem? Equipment is an incumbent constraint or candidate asset; outcomes and mission context define the problem.
  2. What information should an assumption register carry? Assumption, basis, owner, confidence, validity interval, test plan, and consequence if false.
  3. Does an average of 100 trips over three days justify halving the fleet? No. Sampling and peak demand are unresolved, and fleet sizing depends on cycle time and service constraints.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Treating an approved design choice as an unchangeable stakeholder requirement creates circular justification.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 02

Stakeholder evidence and current-state analysis

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 02 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 2 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: stakeholder analysis, interview bias, current-state mapping, evidence triangulation
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do you translate competing stakeholder stories into defensible evidence?

Conceptual tutorial

Stakeholders hold different slices of the system

Riders observe waiting and comfort; drivers observe handoffs and route obstructions; facilities staff observe curb access; safety officers own acceptance boundaries; finance sees lifecycle affordability. None is the entire truth. Build a stakeholder map identifying influence, affected outcome, decision rights, and missing voices. Use observation, records, and structured interviews rather than letting one articulate sponsor define the whole problem.

Distinguish what people report, what the process actually does, and what available data can support. A process map with arrival, boarding, transit, missed pickup and exception paths often exposes hidden delays. Triangulate statements: compare waiting-time interview responses with timestamped dispatch data and on-site queue observation. Identify measurement bias: people who abandon a service may be absent from satisfaction surveys.

Convert findings into explicit uncertainties

For each concern write a claim, evidence source, alternative explanation, uncertainty and next investigation. Missing observations are not zero demand. Represent service variation by time, user group, weather and route rather than one average. Seek disconfirming evidence: interview riders who do not use the service and inspect days with abnormal demand. Ethical research requires privacy-conscious collection and appropriate consent for sensitive observations.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Three observations, one concern. A student says "I always wait 20 minutes." The app log reports 9 minutes median for completed pickups, and an observer records five riders who gave up before boarding. These measures have different denominators. Define waiting time starting at request submission, include abandoned requests as censored or failed-service records, and report the fraction of requests meeting a defined threshold alongside the completed-trip median.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why might completed-trip logs understate rider dissatisfaction?
  2. Name two independent ways to investigate a disputed waiting-time claim.
  3. What belongs in a current-state process map?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why might completed-trip logs understate rider dissatisfaction? People who cancel or abandon are missing from completed-trip statistics, creating selection bias.
  2. Name two independent ways to investigate a disputed waiting-time claim. Compare instrumented timestamps with direct observations or sampled rider reports; examine definitions and missing records.
  3. What belongs in a current-state process map? Normal stages, decision points, roles, handoffs, queues, exceptions, and observable measures.

Critical misconception

Watch for: An interview count is not a representative demand estimate without a sampling frame and definitions.

Transfer exercise / portfolio artifact

Make: Draft a 12-question interview guide for riders and operators, an observation protocol, and a claim/evidence table with two competing interpretations for each finding. Compare your work against the full original Week 2 assessment, not only this short companion exercise.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 03

Desired end state, ConOps, scenarios, and mission calculator v0.1

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 03 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 3 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: desired end state, ConOps, operational scenario, measure of effectiveness, mission calculator
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

What does mission success look like before the architecture exists?

Conceptual tutorial

Operational intent precedes components

A desired end state states the outcome in the environment, not the technology: accessible service during scheduled hours, predictable pickup, and clear exception handling. A Concept of Operations (ConOps) describes actors, operational phases, boundaries, external dependencies, information flows, and both nominal and adverse scenarios. Write a scenario as preconditions → triggering event → interaction → outcome → recovery; do not omit the operator during degraded operation.

Measures of effectiveness (MOEs) capture mission outcomes (for example, proportion of requested accessible trips fulfilled within a defined time). Measures of performance (MOPs) describe technical behavior (dispatch processing latency, vehicle turnaround time). Technical performance measures track a design's evolution against margins. Avoid using a low software latency as a substitute for proving that riders can actually board.

Build a transparent mission calculator

Start with a simple capacity relation: available trip opportunities ≈ vehicles × operating minutes × availability / average cycle minutes. State whether cycle time includes loading, layover and recovery. Represent peak variability and confidence separately; a single deterministic estimate is a planning scenario, not an operational guarantee. Expose inputs in a worksheet so reviewers can vary assumptions.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

First mission estimate. Two vehicles, a 480-minute service window, 0.85 operational availability and a 24-minute cycle imply 2 × 480 × 0.85 / 24 = 34 vehicle cycles/day. If each cycle carries three riders on average, the illustrative nominal capacity is 102 boardings/day. This ignores bunching, peak peaks, unusable trips and weather; thus it is a scenario calculation, not proof of satisfying a daily request target.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What distinguishes an MOE from an MOP?
  2. Why is 102 boardings/day not a reliability guarantee?
  3. What is the role of an adverse operational scenario?
Reveal explained answers
  1. What distinguishes an MOE from an MOP? MOE describes mission success; MOP describes an engineered attribute contributing to it.
  2. Why is 102 boardings/day not a reliability guarantee? Average capacity masks arrival variability, capacity imbalance, maintenance patterns and service failures.
  3. What is the role of an adverse operational scenario? It makes abnormal or degraded behavior, decision ownership, interfaces and recovery observable.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Calling estimated throughput "service effectiveness" skips the operational denominator and outcome definition.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 04

Conceptual requirements, use cases, gaps, and experiment planning

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 04 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 4 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: conceptual requirements, use-case boundaries, gap analysis, experiment hypothesis
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How does a narrative become a testable conceptual obligation without premature design?

Conceptual tutorial

Turn outcomes into obligations at the right abstraction

An operational need such as "predictable arrival" needs a declared condition, threshold, and unit of observation. A conceptual requirement might say: "For eligible trip requests during published service hours, the service shall communicate acceptance, rejection or a pending state within 15 seconds under the specified nominal network profile." This does not prescribe a cloud vendor or particular radio. Give each statement an ID, rationale, parent need, owner, verification idea and controlled version.

Use cases identify interactions between external roles and a system boundary. They are not merely screen mockups; include main success path and alternatives, particularly permission failures, sensor uncertainty and network outage. A gap is a measurable difference between present and desired outcomes. An experiment targets an uncertainty that matters to a design decision. State the hypothesis, measurement method, controllable factors and what result would trigger a different choice.

Avoid requirements inflation

Do not turn every design preference into a mandatory "shall". Distinguish stakeholder requirements from derived constraints and implementation decisions. Record quality attributes as measurable scenarios: stimulus, operating context, expected response and threshold. Revisit requirements after concept comparison when the evidence justifies precision.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Gap and experiment. Current service fulfills 76 of 100 recorded eligible requests during an observed window; target outcome is at least 90 of 100. The 14-percentage-point gap might be demand, routing or data loss. Before mandating a vehicle count, run a dispatch simulation under matched demand traces for two dispatch policies and compare fulfilled requests with paired random seeds.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why is a testable requirement different from a preference?
  2. Why must the eligibility denominator be defined?
  3. What makes an experiment decision-relevant?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why is a testable requirement different from a preference? It states an observable obligation under explicit conditions without embedding arbitrary implementation.
  2. Why must the eligibility denominator be defined? Changing eligibility criteria alters reported performance even if service behavior does not change.
  3. What makes an experiment decision-relevant? Its competing outcomes can alter a concept, assumption, threshold or decision with quantified confidence.

Critical misconception

Watch for: A requirements table full of solution names hides the alternatives conceptual design is meant to explore.

Transfer exercise / portfolio artifact

Make: Draft six conceptual requirements from your scenarios, one gap analysis and an experiment card that names the decision threshold before seeing results. Compare your work against the full original Week 4 assessment, not only this short companion exercise.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 05

Functional architecture and mission-to-function traceability

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 05 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 5 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: functional decomposition, functional flow, mission-to-function trace, allocation independence
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do you reason about what must happen before choosing what performs it?

Conceptual tutorial

Functional architecture is a responsibility model

Decompose mission outcomes into functions: receive request, establish eligibility, prioritize, allocate capacity, communicate status, transport safely, monitor service and recover from failure. Each function should have a purpose, input, output, precondition and successor relationship. Functions are verbs; components are nouns. "Provide rider status" is a function; "MobileApp" is one candidate performer.

Construct a mission-to-function trace: outcome → scenario step → function → data/control flow → conceptual requirement. A trace is not automatically valid because rows share labels. Ask whether losing the function would actually compromise the outcome and whether one function has multiple safety-critical beneficiaries. At concept stage, identify timing and information interfaces while avoiding fixed physical allocations.

Read flow models critically

Sequence matters. A dispatcher cannot truthfully announce a confirmed assignment before a vehicle or operator acknowledges it, unless the meaning of "confirmed" is explicitly different. Parallel tasks may complete in any order; timeouts and retry limits must be modeled. Include exceptional paths, as hidden exception work is a classic source of unexpected cost and integration defects.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

A trace that catches a gap. The scenario says a mobility-impaired rider must receive an accessible assignment. The proposed functions are F1 validate request, F2 choose nearest vehicle, and F3 send confirmation. None checks vehicle accessibility capability. Add F2a evaluate accommodation constraints and create corresponding data inputs, allocation criteria and verification intent. This is a functional omission discovered before selecting physical parts.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why is "wheelchair-capable vehicle" not a function?
  2. How can a trace matrix be 100% populated yet wrong?
  3. What is the missing function in a nearest-vehicle-only assignment scheme?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why is "wheelchair-capable vehicle" not a function? It is a property of a candidate physical resource; the function is to match a requirement to capability.
  2. How can a trace matrix be 100% populated yet wrong? Syntactic links can be semantically unjustified or point to obsolete versions.
  3. What is the missing function in a nearest-vehicle-only assignment scheme? Evaluate accommodation and operational eligibility before committing the assignment.

Critical misconception

Watch for: A functional architecture that follows vendor product menus is a disguised physical design.

Transfer exercise / portfolio artifact

Make: Draw a six-function flow with one timeout and one exception path; link each function to a mission outcome and flag any untraced outcome. Compare your work against the full original Week 5 assessment, not only this short companion exercise.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 06

Timing, interfaces, allocations, and Functional Architecture Review

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 06 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 6 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: end-to-end timing, interface ownership, functional allocation, Functional Architecture Review
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How can an architecture review expose missing contracts before hardware is chosen?

Conceptual tutorial

Follow budgets across organizational seams

An end-to-end response is a sum or composition of contributions: queueing, computation, communication, actuation, and operator delay. Under a serial simplification, total latency is the sum of stage latencies; under parallel activity it is governed by a critical-path maximum. Worst-case and percentile budgets cannot be added indiscriminately without assumptions about correlation and distribution.

An interface needs a sender, receiver, exchanged item, schema/units, update rate, accuracy, security boundary, state/timeout behavior, failure response and accountable owner. The Functional Architecture Review (FAR) should ask whether every mission-critical function and exchange is represented, whether performance budgets are plausible, and whether a proposed performer can be substituted without invalidating requirements.

Make review findings actionable

A review exit is not an attractive diagram. Use a discrepancy log: issue ID, evidence, consequence, accountable owner, resolution test and closure authority. Create distinct outcomes: approve for concept development, approve with tracked risks, or rework a missing functional argument. Explicitly retain unresolved assumptions instead of converting them to "TBD" in supposedly controlled interfaces.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Budget. An illustrative notification path allocates 80 ms ingress, 150 ms queue, 120 ms decision, 100 ms storage, and 200 ms network: serial sum 650 ms. A 900 ms target leaves 250 ms nominal margin. This does not prove the 99th percentile, and a loss of network connectivity is an entirely different mode requiring alternate behavior. Record allocation, condition, evidence method and owner for each stage.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Why is a 250 ms nominal timing margin not a tail-latency guarantee?
  2. Name four attributes an interface contract must define.
  3. What closes an architecture-review action?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why is a 250 ms nominal timing margin not a tail-latency guarantee? Variability, correlations, load and failure modes can move observed latency far beyond nominal values.
  2. Name four attributes an interface contract must define. At minimum: roles, exchanged content and units, timing, error policy; include ownership and version.
  3. What closes an architecture-review action? Evidence-backed resolution accepted by the authorized reviewer, not a promise or status label alone.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Adding stage p99 figures as if they describe a coherent system p99 can produce invalid conclusions.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 07

Generate physical concept families with morphological and set-based methods

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 07 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 7 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: morphological matrix, set-based design, feasibility screening, concept diversity
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How can you explore distinct physical concepts without converging on the first feasible idea?

Conceptual tutorial

Separate solution dimensions before combining them

Morphological analysis lists independent or weakly dependent design dimensions, such as transit mode (fixed-loop shuttle, demand-responsive shuttle, escorted walking service), dispatch method (scheduled, reservation, on-demand), and accessibility provision (vehicle adaptation, specialized service, hybrid). Combine options to produce candidate families, then prune only with documented hard constraints. A matrix of 3 × 3 × 2 options yields 18 raw combinations, not 18 credible system concepts.

Set-based design preserves a feasible region while evidence is collected. Instead of choosing one vehicle immediately, keep alternative families with ranges of acceptable speed, capacity, energy, labor and capital cost. Eliminate options only when requirements, physics, policy or data justify it. Diversity matters: three nearly identical vans are not equivalent to three distinct service architectures.

Avoid fake precision in early screens

Use coarse bounds and pass/fail gates for safety and mission feasibility before fine weighted scoring. Mark uncertainty ranges and dependencies. For example, a high-capacity concept with no accessible boarding may fail a mandatory constraint regardless of its aggregate score. Record why each discarded concept was removed, what evidence supports the reason, and what could cause reconsideration.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Concept families. Dimensions: vehicle small / medium / mixed, service fixed / on-demand, staffing attendant / remote support. The naïve set has 12 combinations. A policy requiring an onboard attendant rules out six remote-support combinations. A second restriction that small vehicles cannot meet a target boarding capacity may rule out more, but only after the required capacity and service context are established.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What is the purpose of a morphological matrix?
  2. Why must mandatory constraints precede weighted scores?
  3. Why can an 18-cell matrix mislead?
Reveal explained answers
  1. What is the purpose of a morphological matrix? It systematically explores combinations of solution principles before premature commitment.
  2. Why must mandatory constraints precede weighted scores? A high score cannot compensate for violating a genuine nonnegotiable safety or operational condition.
  3. Why can an 18-cell matrix mislead? Some combinations are incompatible, duplicates, or unsupported by feasibility evidence.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Scoring every imagined combination to three decimal places creates certainty the evidence does not support.

Transfer exercise / portfolio artifact

Make: Generate at least 12 raw combinations with three design dimensions, produce three genuinely distinct viable families, and record every elimination reason. Compare your work against the full original Week 7 assessment, not only this short companion exercise.

SYSTEM CONCEPTUAL DESIGN · WEEK 08

Resource, affordability, schedule, feasibility, and optimization models

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 08 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 8 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: resource budget, lifecycle cost, critical path, feasibility model, optimization constraints
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do affordability, schedule and feasibility constrain an attractive design?

Conceptual tutorial

Model the whole commitment

Concept affordability includes capital, staffing, energy, maintenance, training, spares, software support, infrastructure and retirement. A cheap acquisition can carry expensive recurring operations. Convert all alternatives to a common service horizon, price basis and usage assumption. A simple undiscounted lifecycle cost is acquisition + sum of annual operating costs + disposal; present-value analysis applies discount factors when appropriate and requires an explicit rate.

Schedule feasibility is not a list of optimistic dates. Represent predecessor relationships, resource availability and integration dependencies; the longest-duration path through the dependency network determines the earliest completion under the assumed deterministic durations. Resource constraints can lengthen this path. Avoid equating percent complete with remaining engineering difficulty.

Optimization is conditional on model validity

A solver can maximize weighted value or minimize cost subject to hard constraints (capacity, staffing, range, safety, budget). It cannot decide whether the chosen objective function captures mission value. Check parameter units, bounds and sensitivity before trusting the numerical optimum. Keep scenario uncertainty separate from deterministic solver output.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Cost comparison. Candidate A costs $600k to acquire and $180k/year to operate; candidate B costs $900k and $120k/year. Over five years, ignoring discount and residual value, A costs $1.50m and B costs $1.50m. Equal modeled cost does not imply equal affordability: early capital limits, risk, availability and service outputs still differ. A high-cost concept may also fail annual appropriations even if lifecycle totals match.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What cost categories are omitted by purchase price alone?
  2. What makes a schedule critical path contingent?
  3. Does a solver optimum establish mission suitability?
Reveal explained answers

Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do you compare concepts without disguising preferences as mathematics?

Conceptual tutorial

Values are judgments, not measurements alone

An objectives hierarchy connects top-level mission outcomes to measurable attributes, with nonredundant branches and clear direction of improvement. Transform raw quantities to a common 0–1 value scale only when the anchors represent meaningful preference differences. For cost, a decreasing linear scale may suffice over a narrow justified range; for safety or access, a nonlinear threshold may be more appropriate.

A weighted additive score V = Σ wᵢ vᵢ(xᵢ) is meaningful under assumptions including appropriate scaling and additive preference independence. Weights are not percentages of attention or raw importance ratings. They represent tradeoff ratios between defined swings from worst to best attribute levels. Use stakeholder-informed swing weighting and publish sensitivity to changing weights.

Screen dominance first

A concept is Pareto dominated if another is no worse on every considered attribute and strictly better on at least one. Dominated options usually need no preference weights to exclude them, assuming no hidden constraints. For remaining alternatives, compare uncertainty bands, different stakeholder perspectives and possible regret. Keep mandatory requirements outside the compensating value model.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Transparent value calculation. Two feasible concepts have normalized values for availability and affordability: A (0.9, 0.4), B (0.7, 0.8). With weights (0.6, 0.4), A scores 0.70 and B scores 0.74. B wins narrowly. If availability weight changes to 0.7 (affordability 0.3), A scores 0.75 and B 0.73, reversing the choice. The small difference exposes preference sensitivity; it is not a measurement of engineering certainty.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What does a swing weight compare?
  2. When is an alternative Pareto dominated?
  3. Why can raw milliseconds not be added directly to dollars?
Reveal explained answers
  1. What does a swing weight compare? Preference for a defined change from one anchored attribute level to another.
  2. When is an alternative Pareto dominated? When another option is at least as good on every considered attribute and better on one.
  3. Why can raw milliseconds not be added directly to dollars? Different units and non-equivalent value scales make the sum meaningless without a preference model.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Declaring a close weighted score a "scientific winner" omits weighting uncertainty and stakeholder disagreement.

DOE, parametric analysis, uncertainty, sensitivity, and Down-select Review

Companion chapter: EN.645.767 · Week 10 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Conceptual Design — consult Week 10 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: design of experiments, factor screening, uncertainty propagation, sensitivity, down-select
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How can a small experiment reveal which assumptions actually drive a concept decision?

Conceptual tutorial

Design comparisons, not anecdotes

A design of experiments (DOE) manipulates factors and observes responses according to a planned comparison. In a two-factor, two-level full factorial design, four treatment combinations estimate main effects and interaction under model assumptions. Randomize run order where practical, repeat runs when noise exists, and choose measurable response variables tied to decisions. In simulation, paired random seeds can reduce comparison noise between alternatives.

Sensitivity analysis varies model inputs to see how outputs or rankings change. Uncertainty analysis characterizes what is not known: distributions, intervals or scenarios. They are different tasks. A tornado chart showing output influence does not establish the probability of failure; a Monte Carlo run with unjustified probability distributions can be worse than transparent ranges.

Down-select with epistemic humility

For each choice present the current favored option, evidence, invalidating result, unresolved risks, and decision reversibility. If evidence supports only a restricted development continuation, say so. Keep test configuration, versions, seed, units and analysis script so another engineer can reproduce the conclusion.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Tiny factorial illustration. Examine average waiting time with fleet size 2/3 and dispatch fixed/dynamic. Synthetic outputs (min) are (2,fixed)=15, (2,dynamic)=12, (3,fixed)=10, (3,dynamic)=7. Increasing the fleet improves by 5 minutes at either dispatch setting; changing dispatch improves by 3 at either fleet size, with zero interaction in these noiseless numbers. Real results require replication, capacity analysis and uncertainty.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. Does a tornado chart give failure probability?
  2. What is the interaction in the synthetic two-factor example?
  3. Why might paired random seeds be useful in two simulation concepts?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Does a tornado chart give failure probability? No. It displays output sensitivity to chosen perturbations, not a calibrated probability measure.
  2. What is the interaction in the synthetic two-factor example? Zero minutes: the dispatch effect is the same at both fleet levels.
  3. Why might paired random seeds be useful in two simulation concepts? They help compare alternatives under similar sampled demand/noise, reducing variance in their difference.

Critical misconception

Watch for: A single simulation run is not enough to support statistical claims about variable demand.

The engineering question

What does a Concept Validation Review actually validate?

Conceptual tutorial

Evidence must match the decision maturity

A Concept Validation Review asks whether the selected concept could credibly satisfy the stakeholder mission within plausible technical, cost, schedule and organizational constraints. It does not prove the final engineered system works. Check ConOps completeness, system boundary, hard constraints, alternative comparisons, assumptions, evidence quality and how unresolved issues will be retired.

Distinguish risk (uncertain future event with consequence), issue (current known problem), and opportunity (uncertain beneficial outcome). A risk register should state cause → event → consequence, likelihood basis, owner, treatment, trigger and residual exposure. Numerical risk scores are aids, not precision predictions. Reserve management ties unknowns to budget and schedule capacity; do not spend all contingency in the baseline and simultaneously claim risk allowance.

Preserve a decision trail

A conditional recommendation names the exact preconditions and the evidence that will clear them. The review board needs to see how concept assumptions flow into design, integration and test plans. Make it possible for a later reviewer to answer what was known, what was assumed, and why the program advanced despite residual uncertainty.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Conditional concept recommendation. A preferred shuttle concept has credible capacity but unverified curb-access assumptions and uncertain annual staffing. A nominal capacity model alone is insufficient to clear the conditions.

  2. How does an issue differ from a risk? An issue already exists; a risk may occur in the future and carries uncertain likelihood.
  3. What must a conditional "proceed" include? Named conditions, owners, evidence requirements, review authority, and consequences of failure.

The engineering question

How do you close a system specification without burying ambiguity in a document?

Conceptual tutorial

A specification is a set of contractual engineering claims

Requirements need clearly declared subjects, conditions, observable behavior, thresholds and verification methods. Where a requirement includes multiple obligations, split it or supply a decomposition that independently traces each testable clause. Define terms, units and operational modes. Worked example: follow the reasoning

Ambiguity repair. "The service shall notify riders quickly" becomes "For an accepted request during nominal connectivity, the dispatch service shall issue a status event within 900 ms of receipt, measured at interface IF-03, for at least 99% of requests under workload WL-01." How does a requirement become one or more accountable component obligations?

The engineering question

What evidence makes a preliminary design review a decision rather than a status meeting?

Conceptual tutorial

The interfaces are the design

Electrical, mechanical, controls, software, human factors and operations groups can all optimize locally and still create an infeasible system. Integration of discipline models means checking interface consistency, shared assumptions, technical budgets, change status, and whether failure containment works. A PDR-like event evaluates the baseline's technical adequacy and remaining path to detailed design. Review maturity is defined by the program, not by copying a fixed checklist without tailoring.

Require credible evidence for configuration completeness, major requirement allocations, preliminary ICDs, safety/security assumptions, predicted performance with margins, technology risk retirement and an integration/test approach. Separate review findings (specific problems) from deferred work (accepted remaining maturity with controls). Use objective entry/exit criteria and a signed decision record.

Review for design coherence

One discipline's improvement may impair another's target: a larger battery raises mass; more telemetry affects power, network and data management; a new operator step changes staffing and response times. Test at least one end-to-end scenario by walking physical interactions, interfaces and evidence ownership across every participating discipline.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

PDR finding. A design promises nominal 650 ms rider notifications and separately introduces 400 ms worst-case security inspection without revising the path budget. In the serial worst-case scenario the stated total becomes at least 1,050 ms before stochastic overhead, exceeding the 900 ms target. This is not resolved by renaming the inspection "security"; the architecture or requirement assumptions must change.

Design for reliability, availability, and maintainability

Companion chapter: EN.645.768 · Week 07 · Phase 2 edition 1.0 (2026-10-30)
Original study guide: System Design & Integration — consult Week 7 for the complete assignment, original source readings and mastery rubric.
Concepts: reliability block diagram, availability, maintainability, common-cause failure
Case: Fictional Autonomous Campus Shuttle, dispatch and transport service; all figures are illustrative, not deployment guidance.

The engineering question

How do reliability and maintainability change the architecture rather than just the maintenance plan?

Conceptual tutorial

Distinguish three related properties

Reliability is the probability of performing a defined function without failure over a specified interval under stated conditions. Availability is the fraction or probability of being able to serve when required; it depends on failure and restoration. Maintainability concerns ability to perform restoration under stated resources and conditions. A common simplified steady-state inherent availability model is A = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR), requiring restrictive assumptions and consistent definitions; scheduled maintenance and logistics require more realistic operational-availability models.

Reliability block diagrams can approximate serial or redundant success paths. Under independent component reliabilities, serial success multiplies probabilities. For two independent parallel components with identical success probability p, combined success is 1 − (1 − p)^2. Shared power, software defects, environment and common-cause failures violate independence and can destroy the expected redundancy benefit.

Design for diagnosis and repair

Provide fault detection, isolation, spare strategy, accessible components, configuration recovery and operator procedures. A mean repair time that excludes parts delivery may hide real downtime. Choose architecture based on mission service, not a generic desire for redundancy.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Reliability example. Two independent serial elements each have reliability 0.98 over the mission interval; system reliability is 0.98² = 0.9604. Redundant independent elements each at 0.98 yield 1 − 0.02² = 0.9996 for that one function. If both share a single critical power feed, the redundancy model is invalid unless the shared feed is included and failure dependence addressed.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  1. What is the difference between reliability and availability?
  2. Compute serial reliability for two independent 0.98 elements.
  3. Why can redundancy have disappointing results?
Reveal explained answers
  1. What is the difference between reliability and availability? Reliability concerns no failure in an interval; availability concerns readiness to serve and restoration.
  2. Compute serial reliability for two independent 0.98 elements. 0.9604 or 96.04%.
  3. Why can redundancy have disappointing results? Shared causes, imperfect detection/switchover, common components or maintenance constraints may dominate.

Critical misconception

Watch for: Using MTBF as though it were a no-failure probability mixes a time statistic and a probability.

The engineering question

How do safety, usability and security obligations survive architectural tradeoffs?

Conceptual tutorial

Design for the real socio-technical system

Humans are system participants, not noise around the technology. Analyze who detects failures, interprets alerts, authorizes recovery and manages overload. A human-systems integration assessment asks whether interface cues, workload, accessibility, training and error tolerance support realistic operation. A nominal screen demonstration does not validate emergency behavior under pressure.

A hazard is a condition that can lead to harm; risk analysis examines causal scenarios, controls and residual exposure. Threat modeling considers adversarial objectives, trust boundaries and misuse paths. Although safety and security use different reasoning tools, they intersect: an unauthorized command may create a physical hazard, and a rushed operational fallback may undermine security. Define controls at prevention, detection, containment and recovery layers.

Resilience includes degraded modes

Specify what service remains when communication fails, sensors disagree, or operators are unavailable. Avoid promising "fail-safe" without defining the safe state, who has authority to initiate it, and evidence from the independent physical safety controller. The teaching shuttle dispatch platform must not be mistaken for a certified vehicle-control system.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Human-and-cyber hazard chain. An attacker replays an old "vehicle available" status. Dispatch incorrectly assigns a rider while the actual vehicle is unavailable. Controls include authenticated event provenance, sequence or revision checks, age limits, operator-visible conflict alert and a bounded degraded mode. The model still requires testing against reordering and connectivity faults; encryption alone does not prevent stale but authentic data.

Reveal explained answers
  1. Why is a hazard not the same thing as a failure mode? Hazard describes a potentially harmful condition; failures and misuse can cause or contribute to it.
  2. Can encryption prevent all stale-message assignments? No. Valid but stale messages require freshness, version and state validation.
  3. What belongs in a degraded-mode specification? Trigger conditions, remaining functions, authority, safe boundaries, notification, recovery and evidence.

The engineering question

Engineer the enabling system

The engineering question

How can integration proceed without discovering basic incompatibilities on the full system?

Conceptual tutorial

Integration is a staged argument

Plan the build-up from tested components to subsystems, then to integrated service under increasingly realistic environments. Each step must name input artifacts, versioned configurations, enabling equipment, interface checks, safety conditions, expected results, fault isolation and rollback procedure. Use integration dependencies to determine order; integrating everything simultaneously obscures which seam caused failure.

The Integration Readiness Review checks whether elements, facilities, personnel and procedures are actually ready. Entry evidence includes completed lower-level checks, correct hardware and software versions, accepted ICDs, calibrated instrumentation where required, known hazards controlled, anomaly triage, and access to logging. Rehearsal results from a simulator are not a substitute for interface evidence in a new environment.

Establish a failure-localization strategy

At each increment, record a baseline fingerprint and health checks. When an integration step fails, return to a known-good state, isolate the changed interface or component and preserve diagnostic data. A stub can mimic a missing component only if its behavioral and timing fidelity are stated.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Integration build-up. Step 1 confirms dispatch API schema with a stub. Step 2 integrates real persistence under duplicate-message tests. Step 3 connects a simulated vehicle event gateway. Step 4 integrates a supervised operator console in a closed lab. Every step has a signed configuration record and a rollback. Skipping directly to live vehicles would add uncontrolled safety and diagnostic risk.

Retrieval questions — attempt these first

  2. What does an integration configuration fingerprint contain?
  3. When is a stub insufficient?
Reveal explained answers
  1. Why is big-bang integration diagnostically weak? Many interfaces change together, so failures have ambiguous causes and risky rollback.
  2. What does an integration configuration fingerprint contain? Hardware identifiers, software builds, configuration, versions, test environment and dependent service revisions.
  3. When is a stub insufficient? When it does not reproduce timing, failure behavior, state or protocol constraints relevant to the test claim.

Critical misconception

Watch for: An integration plan that omits environment and rollback controls is not executable safely.

The engineering question

How do you know the design can eventually be verified and validated?

Conceptual tutorial

Verification and validation answer different questions

Verification asks whether a realized element satisfies a specified requirement; validation asks whether it is suitable for intended use in its intended environment. Both require planned conditions, authoritative baselines and interpretable evidence. A requirement can have a test case assigned but remain unverifiable if there is no accessible measurement point, required facility or feasible test method.

Build a verification matrix with requirement ID/revision, planned method (inspection, analysis, demonstration, test), item level, setup, procedure/evidence link, threshold and authority. Separately connect stakeholder expectations and ConOps scenarios to validation events with representative users and conditions. Readiness is an evidence capability claim

A design review should demonstrate a credible path to formal test and acceptance: access points, representative environments, calibrated sensors, testable fault injection, telemetry retention, configuration records and anomaly reporting. Do not claim future verification as completed compliance; use explicit statuses such as planned, ready, executed, passed, failed and conditional.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Evidence design. Requirement R-14 has a 900 ms 99th-percentile response threshold under workload WL-01. The verification plan requires synchronized ingress and egress timestamps, workload version, defined eligible requests and traceable test build. A nominal single-message timing test cannot verify a percentile threshold; the design must expose adequate instrumentation for the planned experiment.

Reveal explained answers
  1. What distinguishes planned verification from verified compliance? Planning describes intended evidence; compliance requires executed, configuration-matched, accepted results.
  2. Why may a test method be impossible despite a test-case ID? Lack of measurement access, proper environment, safe injection, instrument capability or clear thresholds.
  3. What connects validation to the customer? Stakeholder expectations, representative operational scenarios and actual intended use conditions.

What does the Test & Evaluation team need beyond a set of drawings and tests?

Hand off a controlled design and its open questions

Evidence ladder. How do requirements and stakeholder outcomes become defensible test objectives?

Every test starts with an assertion

The engineering question

How do you match a test environment to the claim being evaluated?

Conceptual tutorial

Choose the test environment by the mechanism

A component bench can isolate an algorithm; software-in-the-loop reproduces the software against simulated surroundings; hardware-in-the-loop connects physical controllers to simulated processes; a supervised field exercise introduces human and environmental variation. Higher realism can improve external validity but may reduce repeatability, introduce hazards or make diagnosis harder. No environment is universally superior.

Build a test architecture showing test articles, stimulation, monitors, network and power dependencies, safety barriers, automation, operator interfaces, data flow and configuration control. Describe which relevant failure modes can be provoked and which cannot. Virtualization does not reproduce timing or electrical faults unless explicitly modeled and verified.

Make model credibility explicit

For model-based evidence, track intended use, conceptual assumptions, code verification, parameter sources, validation against relevant observations and uncertainty. A simulator validated for average arrival time may not be credible for rare emergency stops. Match the strength of the claim to the validation domain and evidence limits.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Environment comparison. A simulated network injects 300 ms latency and 10% message loss to exercise dispatcher retry logic. This supports software fault-handling claims if the injection harness is correct; it does not reproduce all radio interference patterns or prove physical vehicle safety. Add instrumentation for timestamp coherence and event ordering, then state unrepresented mechanisms in the test report.

  3. Can a network simulator certify a vehicle stop?

The engineering question

How can numerical test results support a decision without overstating certainty?

Conceptual tutorial

Measurement is a chain

A numerical result depends on instrument resolution, bias, calibration status, repeatability, timestamps, operator procedure and the definition of the measured quantity. Separate measurement uncertainty from population variability and model uncertainty. A 900 ms threshold cannot be adjudicated from a clock system with unspecified synchronization and unknown latency overhead.

Sampling plans should be tied to desired inference. For a mean from approximately independent observations, an illustrative confidence interval uses x̄ ± t* s/√n, provided assumptions are suitable. Quantiles, reliability and rare events require different methods. Observing zero failures is not equivalent to proving perfect reliability. For zero failures in n independent Bernoulli trials, the approximate one-sided 95% upper failure-probability bound is 1 − 0.05^(1/n); model assumptions and confidence definition must be stated.

Design experiments to expose causes

Use designed factor combinations to estimate influences and interactions. Factorial designs and randomized run order can reveal interactions invisible to one-variable-at-a-time tests. Run sufficient replications for uncertainty, and avoid treating deterministic simulation responses as if they were independent physical trials.

Worked example: follow the reasoning

Zero-failure bound. In 100 independent representative trials, no failures are observed. Approximate 95% one-sided failure-probability bound is 1 − 0.05^(1/100) ≈ 2.95%. The engineering question

Execution produces records, not just verdicts

One mission, three baselines: the decision-and-evidence chain

Why the baseline has to evolve

Systems development has a continuity problem. Concept development speaks in outcomes and viable alternatives; design groups speak in allocations, interfaces and parts; test groups speak in articles, environments, procedures and observed results. Teams can each create excellent local documents yet lose the link between what was needed, what was built and what was actually established. The cure is not one enormous table. It is a small set of typed, revisioned relationships maintained across three changing baselines.

An operational need links to a scenario and an effectiveness measure. A conceptual requirement links to functions and selected concept rationale. The design baseline adds physical owners, budgets and ICDs. The evidence baseline links the requirements and use scenarios to test procedures, executed configurations and accepted results. Every relationship answers a question: why does this element exist? what must change if it changes? what evidence supports the claim? A status icon is not itself evidence.

A running example

For our fictional dispatch service, NEED-04 says riders should know whether transport is available. SC-05 describes a request during normal service; MOE-02 measures the fraction of eligible requests served. The system requirement REQ-14/C defines event delivery under workload WL-01, and IF-03/B specifies its message contract. The design record ADR-11 selects an asynchronous event service to meet resilience objectives. Formal test VT-14 links not merely to REQ-14 but to executed data RUN-38, article LAB-03, build D-21 and approved analysis ANA-14. A controlled trial with riders adds VAL-06, which speaks to usefulness—not only technical latency.

Now suppose the 900 ms requirement becomes 600 ms. A query should find all downstream budget allocations, simulation assumptions, interface constraints, test thresholds and customer communications that require re-review. An old successful 900 ms test does not demonstrate the new limit. A real trace system retains historical versions rather than overwriting old results.

Build a minimum useful schema

Use IDs and link types such as motivates, derivedFrom, allocatedTo, constrainedBy, testedBy, executedAs, verifiedBy, and validates. Store link rationale and owner where necessary. A reviewable query is: "show all approved requirements with no accepted test evidence against their current revision". Another is: "find assumptions cited by a concept choice that have no planned experiment". Be prepared to explain why each link is semantically valid.

Exercise and model answer

Exercise: An integration change moves an operator acknowledgement from optional to mandatory. What is affected? Reasoning: amend the operational scenario and state machine, specify who provides and times out the acknowledgement, revise interface messages, update timing and staffing budgets, add tests for acknowledgement failure, and update the operational validation plan. The point is not to add links indiscriminately but to show the actual causal consequences.

Trade studies: feasibility, preference and uncertainty are different axes

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

The four gates in a defensible decision

A decision analysis starts with a reason to decide, not a weighting spreadsheet. First identify the objective and authority: what will the choice enable, who owns it, and what downstream commitments does it entail? Second screen mandatory feasibility constraints. A concept lacking an approved safe operating mode cannot be rescued by cheap acquisition cost. Third compare value among the feasible choices through well-defined attributes and preference functions. Finally, examine uncertainty, model defects and decision reversibility.

In additive multiattribute evaluation, a score V(x) = Σ w_i v_i(x_i) can be practical, but only if weights and value scales have coherent interpretation. Values v_i must map raw attribute levels to stakeholder preference over clearly defined ranges; weights compare the value of moving from the low to high anchor of each attribute. A 0.9 "availability value" does not directly mean 90% probability of availability. A weight of 0.6 is not necessarily a statement that one criterion is morally 60% important.

A transparent ranking reversal

Consider two feasible dispatch service concepts. A has normalized availability 0.9 and affordability 0.4; B has values 0.7 and 0.8. With availability weight w, A = 0.9w + 0.4(1-w) = 0.4 + 0.5w. B = 0.7w + 0.8(1-w) = 0.8 - 0.1w. They tie when 0.4 + 0.5w = 0.8 - 0.1w, hence w = 2/3. Below two-thirds, B wins; above two-thirds, A wins. A scoring tool that only outputs "B wins" at w = 0.6 hides the central result: this decision is preference-sensitive.

If performance estimates are uncertain, sensitivity is wider still. Analyze optimistic/pessimistic data and seek a decision-relevant experiment where new observations could change the choice. The relevant output may be a decision to keep both options alive, not a false winner.

Common mistakes

Raw weights applied directly to unlike units; combining safety gate violations with compensating scores; ignoring correlated attributes; turning one stakeholder's preferences into organizational consensus; and reporting three significant digits from a few interviews. A Pareto screen can remove dominated alternatives before preference modeling, but only after all relevant costs/benefits and constraints are represented.

Exercise

Recalculate the A/B ranking at w = 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8. Then shift B's availability value from 0.7 to 0.65. Determine the new tie weight. Answer: 0.4 + 0.5w = 0.8 - 0.15w, so w = 0.4/0.65 ≈ 0.6154. The choice becomes more favorable to A as B's estimated availability declines.

A small factorial experiment, explained from first principles

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

Contrast rather than guess

In a two-factor design, factors A and B each have low (−1) and high (+1) settings. Running all four combinations gives average responses y--, y+-, y-+, y++ using the first sign for A. A main effect is the average outcome at the high level minus that at the low level, averaging over the other factor. The interaction describes how one factor's effect changes depending on the other.

Define A = fleet size (2 versus 3), B = dispatch (fixed versus dynamic), with waiting time in minutes as the lower-is-better response. Our illustrative deterministic outputs are y-- = 15, y+- = 10, y-+ = 12, y++ = 7. Effect A = (10 + 7)/2 - (15 + 12)/2 = −5 minutes. Effect B = (12 + 7)/2 - (15 + 10)/2 = −3 minutes. The difference-of-differences, [(7 − 10) − (12 − 15)] = 0, shows no interaction under these four sample values. That does not imply no interaction in reality.

A different set, 15, 11, 12, 5, makes the dispatch improvement 3 minutes for a two-vehicle fleet and 6 minutes for a three-vehicle fleet. The difference-of-differences is −3 minutes. This might make a combined design attractive even if each factor alone looked modest.

What experiment design must control

Randomize run order when physical drift or operator learning might matter. If using simulation, employ repeated demand seeds and consider common random numbers for direct comparisons. Define response units, observation window and inclusion rules. Replicate each cell to estimate run-to-run noise; a four-number table without replication describes a deterministic scenario, not a hypothesis test with uncertainty. Report confounding, failure to randomize, model applicability and missing factors.

From experiments to decisions

Imagine a design review requires mean wait below 9 minutes without violating accessibility or cost limits. A four-cell table may suggest only the three-vehicle dynamic concept clears the wait target. But a robust decision also checks distribution tails, rider demand composition, budget and sensitivity to demand surges. Experiments inform the design argument; they do not decide the stakeholder objective for you.

Practice with a solution

Question: The four results are 14, 9, 11, 5 in (A−B−, A+B−, A−B+, A+B+) order. Compute A and B main effects and difference-of-differences. Answer: A = (9+5)/2 − (14+11)/2 = −5.5. B = (11+5)/2 − (14+9)/2 = −3.5. Interaction difference-of-differences = (5−9) − (11−14) = −1. Report results in minutes and note the need for replications.

Interfaces, end-to-end margins and hidden coupling

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

A connection is not a contract

A functional architecture shows necessary exchanges, but real integration demands much more: schema version, byte order or units, maximum and typical rates, sender/receiver authority, security identity, timeout, retry behavior, staleness, concurrency and shutdown state. An Interface Control Document should state who controls each field and what happens when assumptions break. Two components can both pass their unit tests while disagreeing about milliseconds versus seconds or "confirmed" versus "acknowledged".

A timing budget is useful because it forces ownership. Under strictly serial stages, total processing time is the sum. In an example with ingress 80 ms, queue 150 ms, selection 120 ms, storage 100 ms, network 200 ms, the nominal total is 650 ms and a 900 ms threshold provides nominal margin 250 ms. This is not enough to claim the 99th percentile meets 900 ms. Stage latency is variable; correlations and shared resources matter, and high-percentile stage statistics do not combine by simple arithmetic into a system percentile without distributional reasoning.

Coupling changes the shape of failures

A new encryption gateway adds workload that consumes CPU cycles shared with queue scheduling; a growing queue increases latency nonlinearly as utilization approaches capacity. A higher polling rate raises bandwidth demand and potentially causes the very message staleness it was meant to prevent. A larger battery can extend range while affecting mass and stopping assumptions. System-level margins must be tracked across physical disciplines and test conditions.

For a stable arrival rate λ and service rate μ in a very simplified M/M/1 queue, expected time in system is 1/(μ−λ) when λ < μ. At μ = 10 requests/s and λ = 8, mean time is 0.5 s; at λ = 9.5, it is 2 s. This demonstrates sensitivity near saturation, not a valid prediction for a real dispatch system without verifying queue assumptions.

A testability checklist

Every critical interface needs observable ingress/egress or equivalent instrumentation, a fault-injection mechanism and traceable revision. Review interface budget owners together, rather than checking each in isolation. Test degraded modes independently: a nominal latency budget says nothing about no connectivity. If the design chooses buffering, specify retention and stale-data handling.

Exercise

An added stage costs 180 ms nominal. With unchanged original stages, nominal total becomes 830 ms and margin falls to 70 ms. Question: Does the system meet the 900 ms 99th-percentile requirement? Answer: Not established. You need a defined workload, synchronized measurement, population rules and enough tail data; this arithmetic only exposes shrinking nominal headroom.

Reliability, redundancy and the cost of assumptions

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

What probability describes

Reliability is a probability of successful function across a specified time or demand under stated conditions. A component marked "99% reliable" is meaningless until the mission length, operating envelope and failure definition are known. In serial systems, independent component reliabilities multiply: for two required elements with R1 = R2 = 0.98, Rsys = 0.9604. In redundant parallel success, the independent failure probabilities multiply: Rsys = 1 − (1−R1)(1−R2) = 0.9996 for the same two components.

The arithmetic is not the main difficulty. Independence is. If both branches share a power bus, network, software library or maintenance procedure, a common cause can defeat both. Introduce a simple shared common-cause success probability q that must hold before independent branches can operate: Rsys ≈ q × [1 − (1−p)^2]. If q = 0.99 and each branch p = 0.98, then Rsys ≈ 0.989604, much less than the idealized 0.9996. This is still only a schematic model; common-cause analyses can require more detailed mechanisms.

Repair changes the objective

Steady-state inherent availability under a particular repairable-system model is A = MTBF/(MTBF+MTTR). At MTBF 500 h and MTTR 5 h, A is approximately 0.9901. It excludes waiting for spares, operator response, transport, scheduled outages and logistical downtime. A repairable component can be reliable over a short mission but operationally unavailable because repair support is slow. Identify what "time down" includes.

Test planning matters

A high numerical reliability claim demands evidence across relevant demands, environments and time. A few no-failure demonstrations do not prove extremely low rare-failure rates. Reliability analysis should inform architecture, fault detection, maintainability provision and test design together. Different failure modes may warrant different measures: rate of hazardous failures, mean downtime, probability of service loss or safe degradation.

Exercise with answer

Two independent branches each have 0.95 reliability, but a shared common-cause element has 0.98 success. The illustrative system probability is 0.98 × [1 − (0.05)^2] = 0.97755. Lesson: redundancy's benefit can be dominated by a 2% shared failure chance. Improve architecture by separating the common cause or explicitly designing a safe degraded state; merely adding a third dependent branch may do little.

How many tests, and what a pass really means

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

Separate statistics from acceptance authority

A test requirement needs a measurement quantity, population, environment and threshold. An empirical pass proportion measures observed outcomes for the tested sample, not automatically long-run reliability. If trials are independent, identical Bernoulli demands with unknown failure probability p, observing zero failures among n trials gives a one-sided upper 95% bound p_upper = 1 − 0.05^(1/n) by solving (1 − p)^n = 0.05. This approximation is exact under that simple Bernoulli model for zero failures.

For 100 trials it is about 2.95%; for 3,000 trials it is about 0.0998%. This does not mean 3,000 similar button clicks can validate a complex autonomous system: independence, representativeness and comprehensive failure definition may be much harder than increasing the count. If test cases share a bug trigger or environment, effective independent information can be far lower.

Means and percentiles ask different questions

For latency measurements x1,…,xn, the sample mean estimates central tendency. The 99th percentile concerns the upper tail and should not be estimated using the average or merely mean + 2 × standard deviation without a justified distribution. One simple empirical nearest-rank percentile is the sorted value at index ceil(0.99n). This gives a descriptive result, not a confidence bound on population p99. For 100 observations, nearest rank p99 is the 99th sorted observation, extremely sensitive to tail sampling and workload selection.

An acceptance plan for p99 under a threshold needs predetermined sample scheme, treatment of timeouts, instrumentation uncertainty and a statistical decision rule agreed with the authority. If the threshold is near the instrument's uncertainty range, classify the outcome as inconclusive or use an explicit guard band as policy dictates.

Measurement uncertainty cannot be ignored

A clock offset of 200 ms may dominate a 900 ms response limit. Calibrate or verify synchronization at relevant start and end points. Capture analysis code and raw logs so stakeholders can audit exclusions and transformations. For censored, dropped and timed-out observations, the data collection design must preserve the existence of failed requests; otherwise the reported tail is selected for success.

Exercises and answers

  1. Zero failures in 200 trials: 1−0.05^(1/200) ≈ 1.487% one-sided upper bound under the Bernoulli assumptions.
  2. Nearest-rank p99 of sorted values with n=50: ceil(49.5)=50; the maximum observed value, not a population confidence statement.
  3. Two failures in 100 trials: empirical success is 98%. This alone cannot establish a 99% underlying success requirement, and the formal disposition depends on the preapproved acceptance rule.

From an integration build-up to a formal evidence package

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

The same experiment changes status through the lifecycle

During design, an informal bench script may reveal a faulty field mapping. During integration, the script becomes a repeated regression check across controlled interfaces. For formal qualification, it must be transformed into an approved procedure that is traceable to requirements, executable against a documented test article and supported by calibrated instrumentation, data control and authorized dispositions. Automation alone does not perform that transformation.

Consider a staged path: mock interface schema → real database and queue → simulated vehicle gateway → supervised operator console → system test bed → controlled validation scenario. Every stage has an identified purpose and a scope limitation. A failure in the second stage should not require rebuilding the entire architecture. Preserve known-good images and record which release, hardware and configuration produced each outcome.

Readiness reviews are evidence checks

The Integration Readiness Review asks whether interfaces, elements, personnel, enabling equipment and safe work procedures are ready for assembly and integrated testing. The Test Readiness Review asks whether a specific formal test can execute safely and produce valid evidence. The final evaluation review asks which claims the collected evidence actually supports. All three involve reviews, but they examine distinct obligations and cannot substitute for one another.

A strong test procedure names the controlled requirement, objective, article serial/build, environment, instrument pedigree, stimulus sequence, expected observation, stop criteria, data retention and results authority. It also defines how a deviation is recorded and what evidence supports retesting. If the harness silently discards missing responses, formal test results will be systematically biased regardless of how precisely the other timestamps are measured.

How to trace one discrepancy

A timed request fails. Record the original run and data without alteration. Investigate whether the device, interface or measurement chain caused the mismatch. If device behavior failed a valid criterion, open a product discrepancy and corrective action. If timestamp clocks were invalid, document an instrument/test-system discrepancy; after authorized disposition, repair the test setup and rerun. In either case, preserve the failed/invalidated run rather than silently deleting it.

Exercise

Create a promotion checklist that turns an exploratory network-partition test into a qualification procedure. Model answer elements: stable input stimulus and versioned harness; verified failure injection; controlled test article; preapproved thresholds; time synchronization; evidence trace to a requirement; operator safety stop; configuration manifest; deviation workflow; independent review and signoff.

Operational readiness as claims, arguments, evidence and limits

Phase 2 concept library deep dive · Independent instructional synthesis

A review is not a vote for optimism

An operational readiness recommendation states what is supportable now, within which configuration and operating context, under whose authority. Construct the case from explicit claims, evidence, assumptions, and defeaters—observations or unresolved risks that could invalidate the argument. Visual status dashboards can help navigation but cannot turn a critical untested hazard into a green overall verdict.

A well-structured claim set includes: requirements verified for design baseline DES-1.3, operational goals evaluated for a stated user group, safety controls independently reviewed within their authorized domain, supportability arrangements ready, and known anomalies dispositioned. For each claim name accepted results, test environment and validity limit. A simulation can support claims about its modeled behavior only to the extent its model credibility permits. A restricted lab demonstration does not establish street-operation readiness.

Example of a conditional argument

Claim: a dispatch software demonstrator can be used in a closed laboratory. Evidence: controlled builds passed specified functional, fault-injection and latency tests under WL-01. Counterpoint: no representative campus-wide network partition and no complete accessibility user study. Disposition: restricted lab continuation; field trial not approved. Condition to expand: an authorized network-resilience evaluation, stakeholder validation with appropriate access needs, and updated incident-response procedures. A governing body still has to decide whether and under what rules any broader trial is lawful or acceptable.

The final package should let a future reviewer reproduce the evidence story: baseline manifest, verified requirement statuses, validation findings, test procedures and runs, uncertainty calculations, issue/risk registers, signed actions and explicit unknowns. If a design revision follows the test, the old evidence must be reassessed for relevance.

A counterfactual drill

Suppose 99th-percentile response latency passes in the lab but the fleet telemetry system changes to a cellular provider whose coverage has not been measured on the campus route. Does the old performance test establish field readiness? No. The operating environment, failure modes and interface assumptions changed. The right response is to restrict the claim, plan representative evidence, update the architecture's degraded-mode handling and document authority for any field test.

Final self-check

Ask an independent reviewer to answer: What exactly passed? Against which requirement revision? On which article and environment? Who signed the evidence? What does it not prove? Which observation would force the organization to stop or rework? If any of these lacks an answer, readiness is an open engineering question.
