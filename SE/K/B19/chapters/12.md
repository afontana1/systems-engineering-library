## The problem you are learning to solve

Imagine the regional mobility council meets after an emergency drill. Agencies individually meet many of their own targets, yet some residents still cannot reach essential care. A sponsor wants a decisive engineering answer and a single performance number. You are responsible for making the inquiry sufficiently precise to support action without pretending away divided responsibility, incomplete evidence or affected people.

This week's question is **final enterprise transformation review and live enterprise change**. The key conceptual move is *the enterprise release must survive a change in politics*. The decision is not merely whether an artifact can be drawn: it is whether the claims made by that artifact survive a realistic change in authority, timing, evidence or public consequences. We therefore work from a real decision question toward an explicit representation, then test where the representation breaks.

## The central concept, built carefully

Defend a transformation with a clear outcome theory, credible baseline, funded transition, measured distribution of benefits, independent challenge and adaptable governance. No committee can guarantee participation forever.

In a live review a newly elected council removes a subsidy and a supplier withdraws. Trace effects on affordability, incentives, portfolio, workforce and service equity.

The engineering reasoning should move in a disciplined order. First name the observed or desired outcome, the boundary, the actors who have the right to act, and the time horizon. Next identify the mechanism hypothesized to link an intervention to that outcome. Then choose a representation—network, causal loop, interface contract, process model, probability table, capability map or deliberative inquiry—whose assumptions match the phenomenon. Finally specify evidence able to disconfirm the model. That last step matters because a persuasive representation can still be false, incomplete, or legitimate for only a narrow class of decisions.

### Foundational distinctions from the course guide

* A final release is a controlled evidence chain, not a folder of disconnected weekly submissions.
* Executive communication should state the decision, value, authority, cost, timing, risks, conflicts, and evidence without overstating certainty.
* Technical review should expose model scope, uncertainty, correspondence, reproduction, and unresolved findings.
* Live-change analysis tests whether architecture and evidence are connected enough to support real revision.
* The complexity handoff should identify adaptive and emergent mechanisms that enterprise planning could not resolve.

## Graduate synthesis: defending enterprise transformation under a change of authority

### Transformation is a sequence of partially irreversible commitments

Enterprise change is not a single “to-be” architecture approved once. Investments alter workforce skills, supplier dependence, data regimes, governance and the future set of feasible choices. Some decisions are reversible, such as a limited process pilot; others are expensive to undo, such as closing a depot or replacing a contractual platform. A transformation roadmap should therefore stage commitments according to uncertainty and reversibility. The concept of a real option is useful: defer an irreversible investment when information gained from a smaller step may materially change the choice, provided the cost of delay is acceptable.

For a simple project, net present value is `NPV = -C0 + Σ_t (Bt-Ct)/(1+r)^t`, but enterprise programs create interacting benefits and governance risks. A pilot with a positive financial estimate does not automatically justify national scale: workforce structure, procurement rights, local trust and data availability may differ. Record the assumptions under which effects are expected to transfer. A plan that merely multiplies pilot savings by the number of sites implicitly assumes constant treatment effect and negligible coordination cost—often implausible.

### Measure distribution, not only aggregate benefit

A transformation can improve total trips while worsening access for residents with complex mobility needs. This illustrates the difference between *efficiency*, *effectiveness* and *legitimacy*. Efficiency concerns resources per unit output; effectiveness concerns mission outcomes; legitimacy concerns whether those affected have acceptable voice, rights and governance. They are distinct dimensions. A single weighted index may be useful for comparing eligible scenarios, but it should not erase binding obligations or dissent. Present disaggregated performance, service floors and unresolved conflicts alongside summary indicators.

The causal case should distinguish observed results from hypothetical counterfactuals. If demand declined after a new transit policy, attribution requires examining other influences: weather, school schedules, competing services, reporting changes and economic conditions. A credible transformation defense specifies baseline period, comparator, potential confounders, outcome lag and residual uncertainty. Some benefits, particularly resilience and institutional learning, may be observable only under disruptions; a normal-week pilot cannot certify them.

### Live political change is a design test

Suppose a council removes the shared-service subsidy and a constituent supplier withdraws. The earlier financial plan becomes infeasible. The correct response is not to preserve the preferred target architecture by hiding the lost funding. Update the resource envelope, dependency graph, service obligations, decision rights, interim capability, and evidence plan. Identify which parts of the roadmap can continue without violating safety or public-service commitments.

Scenario planning can expose robust options. A portfolio that performs acceptably across subsidy-retained, subsidy-reduced and supplier-withdrawal scenarios may be preferable to a high-return plan that collapses in one plausible state. A minimax-regret calculation can summarize sensitivity when probability estimates are unreliable, but the regret metric itself still reflects stakeholder judgments. Provide the ranking under several plausible value perspectives instead of presenting a spurious universal optimum.

### Institutionalize governance capable of dissent

An enterprise review needs more than technical concurrence. Name the authority that can release funds, alter policy, accept operational risk, govern data, and represent affected populations. Preserve dissent and the evidence behind it. Commit to re-review triggers—loss of a constituent, cost growth, service-equity breaches, or changed legal conditions—rather than defining success as eventual adherence to the original roadmap.

The final architecture is therefore a *revisable public commitment*: what will be attempted, why it should work, how it will be paid for, who is affected, how evidence will be checked, and when the institution must change course. This is the bridge from enterprise engineering to the broader management of complex, contested systems.

### Where this method stops working

A transformation can be technically feasible yet publicly illegitimate, legally blocked or dependent on heroic individual action.

A responsible engineer should separate *model correctness* (the math or logic follows from its premises), *model credibility* (the premises are supported for this use), and *decision legitimacy* (the process respects authority, rights and affected parties). Passing the first test does not guarantee the other two. In particular, neither a hypothetical exercise nor an attractive decision matrix certifies an operational regional service.

## Reading the broader system

The three Phase 5 books offer complementary lenses. The systems-of-systems lens asks which independently managed constituents must cooperate and what technical evidence can travel between them. The enterprise lens asks how resources, incentives, governance and institutional history shape that cooperation. The critical-complexity lens asks whose interpretation dominates, which boundaries exclude harms and when no single technical objective deserves priority. Use all three when a proposed solution changes both technology and public obligations. Their explanations can disagree without one being automatically mistaken: they may be answering different questions.

## Step 03 — NS-ENT19.1 enterprise transformation laboratory
### Final review: claims, evidence, authority and live change

The final enterprise review should not reward an elegant but unactionable architecture. Present a controlled chain: decision purpose → material boundary → affected actors and independent rights → outcome and value measures → current-state process/dynamics → candidate capabilities/portfolios → scenarios and stress → transition and pilot rules → independent decision. The controlled case supplies [computed evidence](../case/computed-evidence.json), [editable sources](../case/controlled-inputs.json) and a [decision register](../case/decision-and-evidence.csv); it does **not** contain field approvals or true regional performance data.

A defensible joint decision makes three different findings: **numeric screening**, **institutional implementability**, and **assurance sufficiency**. In this release, the most numerically successful resource-feasible portfolio reaches 85% constructed scenario coverage against the 95% instructional target. The fully integrated portfolio passes all four scenarios but exceeds both illustrative funding and staffing caps ($4.90M/12 FTE versus $3.60M/8 FTE). All required rights/consent remain unapproved. Therefore **HOLD for enterprise deployment**, while allowing controlled model learning and negotiation of a bounded reversible pilot subject to separately obtained permissions.

### Live-change drill with a transparent re-evaluation

The steering group announces sustained review arrivals of **65 instead of 50 per week**, without new staffing. Do not adjust the model quietly: create a controlled input version, identify the changed assumption, rerun the queue and preserve the old outcome. For capacity-first in the nominal scenario the closure fraction becomes \(1438/1710\approx84.09\%\), with 272 requests open. The physical four-hour capacities did not change, so a model claiming overall mission readiness from physical access fractions alone would be wrong. A second drill suspends the D data-sharing permission: every dependent portfolio must be classified unapproved regardless of how attractive its queue result is. This is a *contractual* failure, not a probability to be traded away.

For independent review, require a claim-to-evidence matrix. Examples: “The finite search is exhaustive” is supported only for the six provided options and their combinations; “the public accepts the pilot” is **not supported** by a constructed authority registry; “a true flood probability is 15%” is **not supported** by assumed scenario weights; “the model conserves work” can be checked by replaying every week's arrivals, completions and backlog. A simulator is neither a formal social-choice mechanism nor a deployment authorization.

### Final exercise and reasoned disposition

**Exercise.** The executive committee offers an emergency exception to launch fully integrated immediately. State what the numerical model proves and which actions remain required. **Solution.** It proves only that under the stipulated conditions the computed four-scenario capacity and coordination thresholds pass. The $1.30M overrun and 4-FTE excess remain, as do contracts, approvals, community review, interoperability tests, evidence validation and emergency escalation duties. An accountable lawful body—not the analyst—must decide whether an exception exists, what it covers, and how risks and affected communities are protected. Until then record **HOLD**, create a change request and propose bounded, reversible evidence-gathering work.

**Handoff:** deliver unresolved behavioral, adaptation, trust, legitimacy and path-dependence hypotheses to the next complex-systems course. The correct professional output is a conditional argument under version control, not an unconditional transformation promise.
